# The enigma of consciousness

**Is lived experience something more than the structure and functioning of the brain?**

*The world · descriptive (theory) · categorical*

Pain, the red I see, the taste of coffee: cognitive science describes ever more finely the brain mechanisms that accompany them, but does lived experience reduce to them? To take a position is to say whether experience is nothing more than the brain's organization, whether it adds something irreducible to it, whether it rests on an inwardness already present in matter, or whether introspection deceives us about its very existence. The axis assumes a brain that is genuinely real: those for whom mind comes first and matter is an appearance take their position on [the nature of reality](../axes/reality-nature.md).

## Poles

### Physicalism

Consciousness is nothing but the brain's activity: experience is not a further fact added to the brain's workings, it is part of them, however hard it may be to explain. How remains to be understood, which most take to be a difficulty of research rather than a mystery of principle. The pole gathers several shades: identity theory, for which every mental state will one day be identified with a brain state, and biological naturalism, for which consciousness is a feature of the brain as ordinary as digestion, irreducible to a third-person description without being one more ingredient in the world. Whether that reduction succeeds is settled on the axis of wholes and parts; whether the substrate must be biological, on that of the thinking machine.

*[Smart](../philosophers/smart.md) · Place*

### Substance dualism

The brain will never fully explain experience, because consciousness is not its product: it is a reality of another nature, the soul, which could in principle exist apart. The spontaneous view of many and of many religions, it is also defended by philosophical arguments: if I can conceive my mind without my body, perhaps the two are not one single thing.

*[Descartes](../philosophers/descartes.md) · Swinburne*

### Property dualism

The brain is physical, but it has irreducible properties: what it is like to see red or to suffer. Here lived experience is a further fact about the world, tied to the brain by a law of nature rather than identical to it; that is what separates this position from physicalism, which admits nothing beyond the brain's workings. And this is the "hard problem" of consciousness.

- David Chalmers calls his position a "naturalistic dualism", and openly declares himself drawn to panpsychism, which he takes to be a serious option.
- Thomas Nagel refuses reductionism without concluding to dualism, rejects labels, and leans toward a neutral monism.

*[Chalmers](../philosophers/chalmers.md) · [Nagel](../philosophers/nagel.md)*

### Panpsychism

Experience did not arise by miracle in a dead universe: a form of inwardness is a fundamental property of matter, present everywhere in varying degrees, of which our consciousness would be the most elaborate form.

- Galen Strawson frames his view as a "real physicalism", not as adding mind to otherwise inert matter.

*[Strawson](../philosophers/strawson.md) · Goff*

### Illusionism

The qualitative feel we think we grasp by introspection is an illusion the brain makes about itself. What is denied is not our experiences, but qualia as ordinarily conceived, private and ineffable properties: what remains to be explained is not those properties, but why they seem so obvious to us.

- Dennett rejects qualia as ordinarily conceived, not the existence of conscious life.

*[Dennett](../philosophers/dennett.md) · Frankish*

## Live problems

- **What it is like** : There is something it is like to be me, and nothing of the sort, presumably, for a stone. But is that "something" one more reality in the world, which physical description lets slip, or only a limit of ours, unable as we are to occupy another's point of view? → [What is it like to be a bat?](../thought-experiments/what-is-it-like-to-be-a-bat.md)
- **Mary's room** : A scientist knows everything physics can say about colour, without ever having seen any: the day she sees red, does she learn something? If she does, lived experience would be one more fact, which objective knowledge did not contain; if not, one must say what she gains while learning nothing. → [Mary's room](../thought-experiments/marys-room.md)
- **The philosophical zombie** : Imagine a double physically identical to you, acting and speaking as you do, but feeling nothing: no one home inside. If such a double is even conceivable, matter does not suffice to fix experience; but does what we can imagine prove anything about what is possible?
- **The knot of mind and body** : My decision raises my arm, a glass of wine muddles my thinking. But if the mind is not material, how does it act on muscles; and if the physical order is self-sufficient, does experience change anything at all in what happens?
- **Is my mind transparent to me?** : I believe I know better than anyone what I think and feel. But the mind may exceed what it perceives of itself: what if introspection told, after the fact, a story about a mental life that largely escapes it?
- **Being wrong about what one feels** : I can be wrong about the world, and even about my own motives, but about my very pain? It seems the one point where seeming and being coincide. What if that very obviousness were the best-installed illusion of all, a brain representing to itself an inner life as it believes it to be?
- **Minds other than my own** : I never perceive another's experience, only bodies behaving: how do I know there is someone behind them? The only consciousness I have direct testimony of is my own.
- **Where does consciousness begin?** : Does a dog suffer as I do? And a fish, an oyster, an embryo, a plant, a thermostat? Between what undergoes something and what undergoes nothing, is there a sharp threshold, a continuous slope, or is the question ill-posed?
- **Consciousnesses that add up?** : If a spark of experience already inhabits matter, how do billions of micro-experiences make a single subject, my one consciousness, rather than a crowd?
- **What holds experience together** : At every moment I see, hear and think at once, yet all of it comes to me as a single experience. What holds it together? When the bridge between the two hemispheres is severed, one and the same brain seems to host two streams of experience that ignore each other.
- **The instrument or the source** : A glass of wine, a lesion, age: the body alters thinking down to character itself. Does this dependence prove that the mind is bodily through and through, or only that its instrument is damaged, like a musician with a broken violin?
- **Is emerging explaining?** : The most common answer today: consciousness "emerges" from the brain as life emerged from chemistry. But is naming an emergence explaining how experience can arise from what has none?
- **What if mind came first?** : The whole axis asks how matter produces mind. But the question can be reversed: what if mind came first, and "matter" were an appearance within it?

Metaphysical dualism directly grounds substance dualism in the philosophy of mind, of which [Descartes](../philosophers/descartes.md) gives the classic formulation.
- *[Nature of reality](../axes/reality-nature.md) : Materialism* grounds *[The enigma of consciousness](../axes/consciousness-nature.md) : Physicalism* : If everything that exists is material, consciousness can only be a property of the brain: mental states are physical states that science will eventually identify. Materialism thus leads to reductive physicalism, which denies mind any reality distinct from neural activity.
- *[The enigma of consciousness](../axes/consciousness-nature.md) : Substance dualism* grounds *[Moral circle](../axes/moral-circle.md) : Anthropocentrism* : If only the rational soul confers genuine inwardness, animals, lacking this thinking substance, are nothing but mechanisms without worth of their own. Substance dualism supports moral anthropocentrism: for [Descartes](../philosophers/descartes.md), the "animal-machine" does not enter the circle of our direct duties. The link is looser, however, among anthropocentrists who, like [Kant](../philosophers/kant.md), ground dignity in reason rather than in a soul-substance.
- *[The enigma of consciousness](../axes/consciousness-nature.md) : Substance dualism* grounds *[Being or having a body](../axes/embodiment.md) : The body as instrument* : If I am essentially a mind distinct from matter, my body becomes an instrument I use, even a prison I set myself apart from. Substance dualism supports the body-as-instrument conception, from [Plato](../philosophers/plato.md) to [Descartes](../philosophers/descartes.md), even though the latter eventually insisted on the union of soul and body.
- *[The enigma of consciousness](../axes/consciousness-nature.md) : Substance dualism* grounds *[Freedom of the will](../axes/freedom.md) : Free will* : If the mind does not belong to the mechanical order of matter, the will escapes the determinism of physical causes and can determine itself. Substance dualism thus supports [free will](../glossary/free-will.md), by removing decision from the chain of natural causes.
- *[The enigma of consciousness](../axes/consciousness-nature.md) : Substance dualism* grounds *[After death](../axes/afterlife.md) : The immortal soul* : If the mind is a substance distinct from the body, its survival becomes conceivable: substance dualism is the metaphysical ground of the immortal soul, from [Plato](../philosophers/plato.md) to [Descartes](../philosophers/descartes.md).

Substance dualism grounds substantialism: [personal identity](../glossary/personal-identity.md) rests on the permanence of the soul, not on memory or narrative. This is [Descartes](../philosophers/descartes.md)' position.
