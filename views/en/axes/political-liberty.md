# Political liberty

**What is it to be politically free?**

*Others · descriptive (theory) · categorical*

To be free, is it to be unhindered, to govern oneself, or to depend on no one's good will? Depending on the answer, what a society must guarantee, and what it must protect us from, changes entirely.

## Poles

### Negative liberty

To be free is the absence of hindrance: no one prevents me from acting as I see fit. Liberty is measured by the space left without interference, and the law itself should confine itself to protecting that space rather than narrowing it.

*[Constant](../philosophers/constant.md) · [Berlin](../philosophers/berlin.md) · [Hayek](../philosophers/hayek.md)*

### Positive liberty

To be free is to govern oneself: to be master of one's desires and, as a citizen, to [share in making the laws one obeys](../axes/who-decides.md). Liberty is not merely being left alone, but being the author of one's life and one's city.

- Rousseau is also claimed by the republican non-domination tradition.

*[Rousseau](../philosophers/rousseau.md) · [Hegel](../philosophers/hegel.md)*

### Republican non-domination

To be free is to depend on no one's arbitrary power. The slave of a kind master who never interferes is still a slave: he lives under an ever-possible domination. Liberty requires protections that make arbitrariness impossible, not merely its present absence.

*Pettit · [Skinner](../philosophers/skinner.md)*

### Capability freedom

To be free is not merely to be unhindered, it is to have the real means to act: [without health, education or resources](../axes/distributive-justice.md), a purely formal liberty stays a dead letter. Liberty is measured by the effective capabilities to be and to do.

*[Sen](../philosophers/sen.md) · [Nussbaum](../philosophers/nussbaum.md)*

## Live problems

- **The limits of speech** : Speech that demeans without striking a blow: must a free society protect it in the name of freedom itself, or restrain it in the name of the freedom of those it targets?
- **Freedom on paper** : The law forbids no one to travel, study, or start a business, yet without money, health or connections none of it is within reach. Is the penniless person unfree, or merely unable? And does calling poverty a lack of freedom sharpen the idea of liberty, or dissolve it?
- **Freed against one's will** : From seatbelt laws to compulsory schooling to the addict weaned off against their will: can coercion make a person free? And if my "true" will can be invoked against the will I actually express, who decides which one is me?
- **Free from the law, or free through the law?** : Every law takes something from what I may do; where laws fall silent, I do as I please. Yet without law the weak are at the mercy of the strong, and a people gives itself laws precisely to be free. Does law limit freedom, protect it, or constitute it?
- **Taking part, or being left in peace** : One age placed freedom in sharing public power, another in the quiet enjoyment of private life. If the moderns desert the assembly to cultivate their independence, do they hand over the very guarantee of that independence?
- **Free in chains?** : If freedom is won within, a clear-eyed person can be free even under a tyrant. Is that inner refuge the one thing no power can confiscate, or a consolation that teaches the oppressed to put up with their chains?

## Grounding relations

- *[Individual and society](../axes/individual-society.md) : Holism* grounds *[Political liberty](../axes/political-liberty.md) : Positive liberty* : If the individual is realized only within the social whole, to be free is not to be left in peace but to take part in the government of one's city. Holism supports positive liberty: in [Rousseau](../philosophers/rousseau.md), one is free only by obeying the law one has prescribed for oneself, and in [Hegel](../philosophers/hegel.md), freedom is fulfilled within institutions.
- *[Individual and society](../axes/individual-society.md) : Individualism* grounds *[Political liberty](../axes/political-liberty.md) : Negative liberty* : If only individuals and their choices are real, to be free is first of all to have a space where no one interferes. Individualism grounds negative liberty: for [Mill](../philosophers/mill.md), society may constrain the individual only to prevent harm to others.
