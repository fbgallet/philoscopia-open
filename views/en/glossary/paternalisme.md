# Paternalism

An attitude or policy that constrains or steers someone "for their own good", as a father would his children: mandatory seatbelts, banned substances, forced savings. Paternalism sets benevolence against autonomy: is protecting people from themselves serving them, or treating them as minors? Since [Mill](../philosophers/mill.md), liberalism has met it with the harm principle: coercion is legitimate only to prevent harm to others, never to impose on someone their own good. A final distinction sets hard paternalism, which overrides a free and informed choice, against soft paternalism, which steps in only when the choice is not fully voluntary or informed; as for the nudge, which steers without coercing, it claims the name of "libertarian paternalism". The question runs through the whole of [political liberty](../axes/political-liberty.md).
