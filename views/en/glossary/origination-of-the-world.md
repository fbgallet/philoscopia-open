# Origination of the world (ḥudūth al-ʿālam)

In [kalām](../movements/kalam.md), the origination of the world means its beginning in time, and serves as the starting point for arguments for a creator. One medieval demonstration holds that bodies are never without temporal accidents, such as motion and rest, and then rules out a beginningless succession of such accidents. It stands notably against the doctrines of an eternal world defended by [al-Fārābī](../philosophers/al-farabi.md), [Avicenna](../philosophers/avicenna.md) and [Averroes](../philosophers/averroes.md). In the twentieth century, the "kalām cosmological argument" took up its theme of a beginning in a renewed argumentative framework.
