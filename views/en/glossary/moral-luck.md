# Moral luck

A phrase from [Bernard Williams](../philosophers/williams.md) and [Thomas Nagel](../philosophers/nagel.md) (1976): we often judge people on what did not depend on them. Two equally careless drivers are not blamed alike if one of them hits a child who was crossing and the other does not. Luck enters into the consequences of our acts, into the circumstances that put us to the test, and even into the character we inherit. The problem: if we should only be judged on what we control, almost nothing is left to judge; if we accept luck's share, we must explain how a judgment can stay fair while depending on what no one controls.
