# Definite description

*Concept associated with [Russell](../philosophers/russell.md)*

An expression of the form 'the such-and-such' — the king of France, the author of Waverley, the smallest prime. [Russell](../philosophers/russell.md) holds that such an expression is not a name: it designates nothing by itself, and the sentence in which it occurs must be rewritten as a claim of existence and uniqueness. 'The king of France is bald' becomes: there is an x, exactly one, that reigns over France, and that x is bald. Three gains at once: statements about non-existent objects become false rather than nonsensical, negation stops being ambiguous (the denial may bear on the baldness or on the existence), and the ontology slims down. The general lesson is that the grammatical form of a sentence is not the logical form of the fact it states.
