# Knowledge by acquaintance

*Concept associated with [Russell](../philosophers/russell.md)*

A distinction [Russell](../philosophers/russell.md) draws between two ways of knowing. We are acquainted with whatever is directly present to the mind, without passing through any proposition: this red patch, this sound, this memory. Everything else — Caesar, the electron, the centre of the Earth — we know by [description](../glossary/definite-description.md), that is, through propositions that reach it as 'the such-and-such'. What is at stake is a principle about meaning: every proposition we understand must be composed wholly of terms with which we are acquainted, which forces us to rebuild by description everything we do not meet.
