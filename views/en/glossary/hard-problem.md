# The hard problem of consciousness

*Concept associated with [David Chalmers](../philosophers/chalmers.md)*

[David Chalmers](../philosophers/chalmers.md) calls "easy problems" the ones brain science knows how to tackle: how we discriminate colours, memorize, control our movements, report on our own states. "Easy" does not mean simple, but that we know what a solution would look like. The hard problem is the one left over when all of those are solved: why is all that work accompanied by experience, why is it like anything at all to be us? For some, this gap shows that experience is a further fact about the world; for others, it measures only our ignorance, and [illusionism](../axes/consciousness-nature.md) holds that there is no problem here, only an illusion to be explained.
