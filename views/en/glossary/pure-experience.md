# Pure experience

In his 1911 An Inquiry into the Good, [Nishida](../philosophers/nishida.md) calls "pure experience" (junsui keiken) immediate experience before its reflective division into a knowing subject and a known object. Hearing a sound before thinking "I hear a sound" illustrates it. He takes over, notably, an expression of [William James](../philosophers/william-james.md), but gives it a metaphysical scope of his own: subject and object become differentiated within this fundamental experience. The book is retrospectively regarded as a founding text of the [Kyoto School](../movements/kyoto-school.md), which took shape later.
