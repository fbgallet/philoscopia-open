# Sense-data

What is immediately given in perception, prior to any interpretation: this brown shape, this taste, this noise — not the table, but the table-appearance I have now, from this angle and in this light. The term was spread by [Moore](../philosophers/moore.md) and [Russell](../philosophers/russell.md) in the early twentieth century and became the centrepiece of an empiricist [foundationalism](../axes/epistemic-justification.md): the data are what resist the utmost effort of doubt, and all the rest — the object, other minds, the past — must be inferred from them or built out of them. The difficulty that eventually sank the notion is that these data are private and momentary: the public world becomes very costly to rebuild, and [Wittgenstein](../philosophers/wittgenstein.md) and his heirs disputed that anything given could be at once experienced and propositional.
