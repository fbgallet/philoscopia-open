# Epiphenomenalism

The doctrine that mental life is produced by the brain without producing anything in return: experience accompanies physical processes as smoke accompanies the locomotive, without driving it forward. Thomas Huxley fixed the image in the nineteenth century. It has the advantage of respecting [causal closure](../glossary/causal-closure.md) while granting that experience exists, but at a steep price: if experience does nothing, it is not what makes us speak of it, and our testimony about our own consciousness is cut off from what it testifies to.
