# Neutral monism

The position that neither matter nor mind is what lies at the bottom of things: there is only one kind of stuff, neither mental nor physical, of which both are groupings. The same events make up a brain when grouped by the laws of physics, and a thought when grouped by those of psychology. [Russell](../philosophers/russell.md) adopted it in 1921 after long opposing it, resting on the idea that physics gives us only the structure of things, never their intrinsic nature: a vacant place remains, where experience can be lodged. The family of contemporary positions that exploit this place has since been called 'Russellian monism', often by peopling it with proto-experiential properties — which brings them close to panpsychism, something Russell himself did not want.
