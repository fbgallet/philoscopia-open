# Dirty hands

The problem of dirty hands takes its name from [Sartre](../philosophers/sartre.md)'s play Dirty Hands (1948), and its most debated formulation from [Walzer](../philosophers/walzer.md) (1973), in a line that goes back to [Machiavelli](../philosophers/machiavelli.md) and [Weber](../philosophers/weber.md). A political leader may face a situation in which acting well requires an act they themselves judge wrong: lying, coercing, torturing to save lives. For a classical [consequentialist](../glossary/consequentialism.md), an act that was the best one cannot also be a wrong, though one may regret the harm it caused. Walzer argues instead that the act can be justified and still be morally wrong: whoever did it remains guilty and must answer for it. The question sets an [ethics](../axes/ethics-foundation.md) of consequences against an ethics of prohibitions ([deontology](../glossary/deontologism.md)).
