# Structural realism

The position that physical theories give us knowledge of the structure of the real — order, relations, laws — without delivering the intrinsic nature of what is thus ordered. We know nothing of the electron beyond its place in a network of relations, and that is enough for physics to be true. This is the price of a modest realism: it gains an explanation of why successive theories are preserved through their equations rather than their pictures of the world, and it concedes that the stuff of things remains undetermined. [Russell](../philosophers/russell.md) gave the first systematic formulation in 1927; [contemporary philosophy of science](../axes/knowledge-status.md) has taken it up under his name, and [neutral monism](../glossary/neutral-monism.md) lodges itself precisely in the place this realism leaves vacant.
