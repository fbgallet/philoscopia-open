# Noble lie

In the *[Republic](../works/republic-plato.md)*, [Plato](../philosophers/plato.md) imagines a fable the rulers would have the citizens believe: all are born of the same earth, but with a different metal in their soul (gold, silver, iron or bronze), which sets their place in the city, though a child may change rank if their metal differs from their parents'. This "noble lie" is meant to make them love their city as a mother and accept their rank; Socrates even hopes the rulers themselves will come to believe it.  The phrase has since come to mean any political lie justified by the common good: may a people be deceived so that it stays united? The question sets the [value of illusions](../axes/truth-value.md) against the [primacy of truth](../axes/truth-value.md), and raises the question of who has the right to decide ([paternalism](../glossary/paternalisme.md)).
