# Kierkegaard

*1813 – 1855 · Danish · [Existentialism](../movements/existentialism.md)*

Danish philosopher, a forerunner of existentialism: he makes faith a leap beyond reason and love a commitment of the will rather than a passion undergone.

## Influenced by

- **[Hegel](../philosophers/hegel.md)** (reacts against) : [Kierkegaard](../philosophers/kierkegaard.md) defines his project against [Hegel](../philosophers/hegel.md)'s system: dialectical mediation dissolves singular existence, whereas the individual, in anxiety and choice, will not be absorbed into the universal.

## Influence on

- **[Ellul](../philosophers/ellul.md)** (continues) : [Ellul](../philosophers/ellul.md) counted [Kierkegaard](../philosophers/kierkegaard.md) among his masters: he owes him dialectical thinking, the primacy of the individual before God against the crowd, and distrust of an established Christendom.
- **[Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md)** (reinterprets) : [Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md) takes from [Kierkegaard](../philosophers/kierkegaard.md) anxiety, the moment and existence as a task, detaching them from their Christian horizon: the notes of *[Being and Time](../works/being-and-time-heidegger.md)* acknowledge the debt while judging Kierkegaard's analysis to have remained "existentiell" rather than ontological.
- **[Sartre](../philosophers/sartre.md)** (continues) : [Sartre](../philosophers/sartre.md) takes from [Kierkegaard](../philosophers/kierkegaard.md) the themes of anxiety, choice and existential singularity, secularising them: existence precedes essence, without the leap of faith that had crowned them.
- **[Camus](../philosophers/camus.md)** (reacts against) : In The Myth of Sisyphus, [Camus](../philosophers/camus.md) calls [Kierkegaard](../philosophers/kierkegaard.md)'s leap of faith “philosophical suicide”: the absurd defines itself against that religious escape, as a demand for lucidity without consolation.
