# Philosophers

## Full profiles (54)

- [Aristotle](aristotle.md) (384 BCE – 322 BCE) : Inquiring into everything, from animals to cities, starting from what we observe and what is said about it: each field has its own method, and each thing is explained by its causes.
- [Bacon](bacon.md) (1561 – 1626) : Flushing out the prejudices that distort the mind, its "idols", then questioning nature through methodical experiment: a collective science, designed to improve human life.
- [Beauvoir](beauvoir.md) (1908 – 1986) : Philosophizing from lived experience: womanhood, old age, everything once held to be natural destiny, rethought as a socially shaped situation, and therefore open to change.
- [Bentham](bentham.md) (1748 – 1832) : Judging every institution by its effects: counting the pleasures and pains a law actually produces, rather than appealing to tradition or supposed natural rights.
- [Camus](camus.md) (1913 – 1960) : Refusing consolations and despair alike: facing a world that guarantees no meaning, seeking a lucid, [rebellious](../glossary/revolt.md) way to live.
- [Chalmers](chalmers.md) (1966 – ?) : Isolating and naming the [hard problem](../glossary/hard-problem.md) of consciousness: even once every function of the brain is explained, it remains to understand why they come with any experience at all.
- [Charles Sanders Peirce](peirce.md) (1839 – 1914) : Asking of every idea what difference it makes in experience: the founder of [pragmatism](../movements/pragmatism.md) makes thought a collective, corrigible inquiry, oriented toward an agreement to come.
- [Confucius](confucius.md) (551 BCE – 479 BCE) : No one becomes human alone: humaneness (ren 仁) is learned, through study, the rites and example, within the relationships that bind us to others.
- [Dennett](dennett.md) (1942 – 2024) : Treating our inner life as a natural phenomenon to be explained: what introspection tells us about it is data to interpret, not infallible testimony.
- [Descartes](descartes.md) (1596 – 1650) : Making doubt a method rather than a defeat: searching beneath everything we think we know for one firm point on which to rebuild.
- [Diogenes of Sinope](diogenes.md) (412 BCE – 323 BCE) : Philosophy by gesture rather than by book: a life lived in public, stripped bare and unashamed, with a biting humour that shows how many conventions rest on nothing but habit.
- [Ellul](ellul.md) (1912 – 1994) : Showing how technique, in promising efficiency, becomes an environment that imposes its own logic on us, and searching, as a Christian, for where freedom can still find a place.
- [Epictetus](epictetus.md) (50 – 135) : A former slave who taught a freedom no circumstance can touch: tirelessly sorting [what is up to us](../glossary/dichotomy-of-control.md) from what is not, and putting all one's effort into one's own judgments.
- [Epicurus](epicurus.md) (341 BCE – 270 BCE) : Philosophy conceived as a medicine for the soul: dispelling, through knowledge of nature, the fears that trouble us, and learning which desires are worth following.
- [George Berkeley](berkeley.md) (1685 – 1753) : Examining what we actually perceive, to challenge the idea of a matter existing outside any mind: the world is made only of minds and their ideas, whose order God upholds.
- [Hegel](hegel.md) (1770 – 1831) : Following how an idea, a way of life or an era runs up against its own contradictions, and is transformed by taking in what it seemed to exclude.
- [Heidegger](heidegger.md) (1889 – 1976) : Asking again what "to be" means, a question the tradition took to be self-evident, starting from our ordinary existence: the tools we handle, care, death.
- [Hume](hume.md) (1711 – 1776) : The most serene of [skeptics](../glossary/skepticism.md): putting human nature through the sieve of experience, finding habit beneath our surest beliefs and feeling beneath our moral judgments.
- [Husserl](husserl.md) (1859 – 1938) : Describing before explaining: [provisionally bracketing](../glossary/epoche.md) the world we take for granted, to grasp as closely as possible how it appears to consciousness.
- [Jonas](jonas.md) (1903 – 1993) : Faced with a technology able to jeopardize the future, grounding a responsibility toward those not yet born, by showing that life already carries ends and value within it.
- [Kant](kant.md) (1724 – 1804) : Setting reason to examine its own powers: distinguishing what it can know from what it can only think, sacrificing neither science nor freedom.
- [Laozi](laozi.md) (571 BCE – 471 BCE) : Overturning what seems obvious through brief paradoxes, to question the urge to master things, down to the art of governing: [non-action](../glossary/wu-wei.md) is not inaction, but action that forces nothing.
- [Leibniz](leibniz.md) (1646 – 1716) : Seeking a reason for all that is: even asking why there is something rather than nothing, and why this world among all possible worlds.
- [Locke](locke.md) (1632 – 1704) : Where do our ideas come from, and where does authority come from? Knowledge must trace back to experience, power to the consent of the governed.
- [Lucretius](lucretius.md) (99 BCE – 55 BCE) : Physics as a poem: singing of atoms, the void and the whole of nature, to free us from the fear of the gods and of death.
- [Machiavelli](machiavelli.md) (1469 – 1527) : Setting ancient history against the experience of his own time to understand how power is won, founded and lost, between human daring (*virtù*) and the whims of fortune.
- [Marcus Aurelius](marcus-aurelius.md) (121 – 180) : The emperor who wrote to himself: at the heart of power, training each day to remain just and serene, attuned to the [reason](../glossary/logos.md) of the world.
- [Marx](marx.md) (1818 – 1883) : Reading ideas through the labour, property and social conflicts that shape them, and making thought a tool for intervening in history.
- [Michel de Montaigne](montaigne.md) (1533 – 1592) : "“What do I know?”": a lived [skepticism](../glossary/skepticism.md), with no system, trying itself out on every subject from its own experience.
- [Mill](mill.md) (1806 – 1873) : Examining every question, from scientific method to the equality of the sexes, as an empiricist and a reforming [utilitarian](../movements/utilitarianism.md): the common happiness demands each person's freedom and self-development.
- [Nāgārjuna](nagarjuna.md) (150 – 250) : The dialectician who refuses to turn [emptiness](../glossary/sunyata.md) into a doctrine: testing every claim that a thing [exists by itself](../glossary/svabhava.md), and showing that it holds only through dependence.
- [Nagel](nagel.md) (1937 – ?) : Seeking how the subjective point of view can find a place in an objective description of the world, without objectivity erasing it.
- [Nietzsche](nietzsche.md) (1844 – 1900) : Where do our values come from, and what are they worth? Philosophizing as a physician of culture: sounding out our moralities to tell what affirms life from what denies it.
- [Plato](plato.md) (428 BCE – 348 BCE) : Putting our opinions to the test in dialogues, and seeking, beyond what changes, what makes it possible to know, to live well and to govern justly.
- [Plotinus](plotinus.md) (204 – 270) : Ascending through inward inquiry, beyond thought itself, to the [One](../glossary/the-one.md) from which all things proceed: a philosophy that culminates in the experience of union.
- [Pyrrho](pyrrho.md) (360 BCE – 270 BCE) : Carrying doubt as far as refusing to decide what things are, and making that restraint not a thesis but a way of living, peaceful and free of dogma.
- [Rousseau](rousseau.md) (1712 – 1778) : A man of the [Enlightenment](../movements/enlightenment.md) at odds with it: asking what society has made of man, and on what terms a political order might set him free rather than subject him.
- [Russell](russell.md) (1872 – 1970) : Breaking statements down with logic to see what they really assert, from mathematics to religion, and believing nothing beyond what one's reasons warrant.
- [Śaṅkara](shankara.md) (700 – 750) : "“Thou art that”": reading all the Upaniṣads in the light of these words, to show that the separate self is an error only knowledge can dispel.
- [Sartre](sartre.md) (1905 – 1980) : Tracking freedom in the most concrete of things, a gaze, a moment of shame, an act of [bad faith](../glossary/bad-faith.md): with no ready-made human nature, existing entails a responsibility that is total.
- [Schopenhauer](schopenhauer.md) (1788 – 1860) : Taking suffering seriously rather than justifying it: a blind will drives us without ever being appeased, and only art, compassion and renunciation can release us from it.
- [Searle](searle.md) (1932 – 2025) : Rejecting dualists and materialists alike: consciousness, lived in the first person, is a biological phenomenon produced by the brain, and no program is enough to produce it.
- [Seneca](seneca.md) (4 BCE – 65) : Adviser to Nero and a guide of souls by letter: turning every trial, disgrace, exile, death itself, into an occasion to exercise virtue.
- [Sextus Empiricus](sextus-empiricus.md) (160 – 210) : Setting arguments against each other methodically, [suspending judgment](../glossary/epoche.md) when their forces balance, and going on living by appearances, laws and customs.
- [Simondon](simondon.md) (1924 – 1989) : Thinking every being from its genesis rather than from a given essence, and extending that demand to machines, which our culture scorns or idolizes for want of understanding them.
- [Smart](smart.md) (1920 – 2012) : Refusing to let consciousness be the exception in a world science explains: sensations are not merely correlated with brain processes, they are those processes.
- [Socrates](socrates.md) (470 BCE – 399 BCE) : Philosophizing in the street, writing nothing: questioning everyone about what they think they know of courage, justice or virtue, until their certainties begin to waver.
- [Spinoza](spinoza.md) (1632 – 1677) : Understanding the passions through their causes rather than judging them: suffering less, acting more, and making of that lucidity a joy.
- [Strawson](strawson.md) (1952 – ?) : Taking seriously both experience, the one absolute certainty, and physics: if experience cannot arise from what wholly lacks it, matter must already contain something of it.
- [The Buddha](buddha.md) (480 BCE – 400 BCE) : Starting from the [unsatisfactoriness (dukkha)](../glossary/dukkha.md) everyone feels, tracing it back to the [craving (taṇhā)](../glossary/tanha.md) that feeds it, and mapping a path to freedom from it: a physician's approach rather than a pessimism.
- [Thomas Aquinas](aquinas.md) (1225 – 1274) : Bringing Christian faith into dialogue with [Aristotle](../philosophers/aristotle.md), weighing every objection: telling what reason can establish on its own from what faith alone gives it to think, without ever setting them against each other.
- [Turing](turing.md) (1912 – 1954) : Replacing an ill-posed question, "can a machine think?", with a test: if conversation no longer lets us tell the machine from the human, on what grounds do we deny it thought?
- [Wittgenstein](wittgenstein.md) (1889 – 1951) : Showing, through two successive methods, how language leads us astray: philosophical problems are not solved, they dissolve once we see clearly into our ways of speaking.
- [Zhuangzi](zhuangzi.md) (369 BCE – 286 BCE) : Through fable, humour and paradox, dislodging every point of view from its claim to judge everything, so as to move freely with the ceaseless transformations of things.
