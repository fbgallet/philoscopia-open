# Iris Murdoch

*1919 – 1999 · British*

Born in Dublin into an Anglo-Irish family, she read classics and philosophy at Oxford during the war, alongside [Anscombe](../philosophers/anscombe.md), [Foot](../philosophers/philippa-foot.md) and [Midgley](../philosophers/mary-midgley.md). After working in refugee relief, which led her to meet [Sartre](../philosophers/sartre.md) in Brussels in 1945, she published the first book on him in English, *[Sartre: Romantic Rationalist](../works/sartre-romantic-rationalist-murdoch.md)* (1953), and taught philosophy at St Anne's College from 1948 to 1963. Against the Oxford orthodoxy of [Ryle](../philosophers/ryle.md), [Hare](../philosophers/hare.md) and [Hampshire](../philosophers/stuart-hampshire.md), whose picture of the human being she reconstructs as an agent defined by his choices, with little room for inner life, she appealed to [Plato](../philosophers/plato.md) and to [Simone Weil](../philosophers/simone-weil.md)'s [attention](../glossary/attention.md) in the three essays of *[The Sovereignty of Good](../works/sovereignty-of-good-murdoch.md)* (1970). *[The Fire and the Sun](../works/fire-and-the-sun-murdoch.md)* (1977) argues with Plato over the place of art, and her Gifford Lectures became *[Metaphysics as a Guide to Morals](../works/metaphysics-guide-morals-murdoch.md)* (1992). She also wrote twenty-six novels, among them *[The Sea, the Sea](../works/sea-the-sea-murdoch.md)* (Booker Prize 1978). Stricken with Alzheimer's disease, she died in Oxford in 1999. Her philosophical work prepared the revival of [virtue ethics](../glossary/virtue-ethics.md) and later work on moral perception.

## Core positions

1. [Interiority](../axes/interiority.md) : **The moral life cannot be reduced to the decisions we make: it is played out above all, continuously, in moral vision, the way we look at others, for we only ever choose within the world we have learned to see.**
2. [The value of truth](../axes/truth-value.md) : **The first obstacle to the good is not ill will but [fantasy](../glossary/fantasy.md): the self ceaselessly manufactures consoling images that hide reality from it.**
3. [The highest good](../axes/sovereign-good.md) : **The Good serves no purpose and promises nothing, neither happiness nor reward: we love it for itself, as a perfection we never reach.**
4. [Where the sacred resides](../axes/transcendence.md) : **There is no personal God to guarantee value, but the Good remains real and surpasses us: [attention](../glossary/attention.md) takes the place left empty by prayer.**
5. [Purpose of art](../axes/art-purpose.md) : **Great art can be a school of virtue, because it teaches us to see, without bringing it back to ourselves, that something other than us exists.**

## Positions

### [Interiority](../axes/interiority.md)

*positioned · inferable · major*

The examined life 80% · Primacy of action 20%

Against the picture of the person she draws out of [Ryle](../philosophers/ryle.md) and [Hampshire](../philosophers/stuart-hampshire.md), in which the mental is analysed chiefly through what shows in action, [Murdoch](../philosophers/iris-murdoch.md) builds the example of [M and D](../thought-experiments/m-and-d.md). A woman finds her daughter-in-law vulgar and lacking in dignity; then, through patient effort and without any change in her behaviour, she learns to see her as simple and spontaneous rather than vulgar. Something moral has happened that the theory of choice cannot even describe. The moral life is therefore **continuous**: it takes place in the unbroken work of looking, and the moment of choice only gathers what that work has prepared. What she defends is the very reality of inner life. This work is not introspection, however: it consists in submitting one's own perceptions to criticism so as to see others better, and she distrusts complacent self-scrutiny, which can be just another form of egocentrism. It is through this refusal of the primacy of action, rather than any taste for turning inward, that she leans toward this pole.

> “I can only choose within the world I can see, in the moral sense of 'see' which implies that clear vision is a result of moral imagination and moral effort.”, The Sovereignty of Good, "The Idea of Perfection"

*Sources : The Sovereignty of Good, "The Idea of Perfection" (1964)*

### [The value of truth](../axes/truth-value.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

The primacy of truth 95% · Vital illusions 5%

[Murdoch](../philosophers/iris-murdoch.md) takes from [Freud](../philosophers/freud.md) what she considers a realistic finding: the psyche is an egocentric mechanism, busy protecting and consoling itself. Ordinary evil therefore lies less in willing evil than in preferring flattering or self-pitying [fantasies](../glossary/fantasy.md) to what is real. Against fantasy she sets not the will but truth: virtue is a kind of realism, a way of allowing what does not depend on oneself to be what it is. Hence the central place she gives to humility, which she defines as a selfless respect for reality. In her novels as in her essays, consolation is the constant trap, in love, in religion or in art.

*Sources : The Sovereignty of Good, "On 'God' and 'Good'" (1969) · "Against Dryness" (1961)*

### [The highest good](../axes/sovereign-good.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Virtue / moral perfectionism 100%

For [Murdoch](../philosophers/iris-murdoch.md), the Good has nothing to do with a purpose: it is not a means to happiness, and the good person expects no reward from it. She thereby parts with utilitarianism, and her approach contrasts with the naturalistic [eudaimonism](../axes/sovereign-good.md) her friend [Foot](../philosophers/philippa-foot.md) would later develop. The Good is a perfection that can be neither defined nor possessed, yet serves as a standard: we recognize the better without ever reaching the perfect. It is "sovereign" because the other moral concepts, such as courage or freedom, take their meaning only from it. It is at once indefinable and real, which is what distinguishes it from a mere ideal.

*Sources : The Sovereignty of Good, "The Sovereignty of Good over Other Concepts" (1967) · The Sovereignty of Good, "The Idea of Perfection"*

### [Where the sacred resides](../axes/transcendence.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Transcendence 78% · Immanence 22%

[Murdoch](../philosophers/iris-murdoch.md) does not believe in a personal God, and in her eyes no providence guarantees the value of our lives. Yet she refuses to conclude that value comes from us. Taking up [Plato](../philosophers/plato.md)'s Good, the sun that makes things visible without itself being looked at directly, she posits it as a real magnetic centre, outside the self, that draws attention away from oneself. She keeps from religion what she finds true, the [attention](../glossary/attention.md) inherited from [Simone Weil](../philosophers/simone-weil.md) and prayer understood as contemplation, but without its object. This Good transcends the self and does not depend on our choices, yet it is not a world behind the world: it is met in this world, through persons, nature and art. It nonetheless forms a higher order of value, which judges us and which we do not make, hence a clear but moderate value. She defends this demythologized Platonism as far as her Gifford Lectures, where she proposes to reinterpret the ontological argument as bearing on the Good rather than on God.

*Sources : The Sovereignty of Good, "On 'God' and 'Good'" · Metaphysics as a Guide to Morals, chapter on the ontological proof*

### [Purpose of art](../axes/art-purpose.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Truth 100%

For [Murdoch](../philosophers/iris-murdoch.md), great art can be a school of virtue, because it exercises the same faculty as morality: seeing what is not oneself. Mediocre art consoles, it flatters [fantasy](../glossary/fantasy.md); great art, that of Shakespeare or [Tolstoy](../philosophers/tolstoy.md), shows beings and a world that resist our wishes. That is why she thinks [Plato](../philosophers/plato.md), whose Platonism she otherwise takes up, is wrong to banish the artists: mimesis, rightly practised, is a form of truth. Its moral effect is in no way automatic: it depends on the attention of the reader or viewer. She is wary, on the contrary, of art enlisted in a cause, which bends reality to a message and so falls back into [fantasy](../glossary/fantasy.md): the worth of art lies wholly in the truth of what it shows.

> “Art and morals are, with certain provisos which I shall mention in a moment, one. Their essence is the same. The essence of both of them is love.”, "The Sublime and the Good" (1959), reprinted in Existentialists and Mystics (1997), p. 215

*Sources : "The Sublime and the Good" (1959) · The Fire and the Sun (1977)*

### [Status of norms (metaethics)](../axes/moral-scope.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Universalism 100%

Against the emotivism of [Ayer](../philosophers/ayer.md) and the prescriptivism of [Hare](../philosophers/hare.md), which separate fact from value, [Murdoch](../philosophers/iris-murdoch.md) argues that moral concepts are instruments of perception: there is a moral truth to be seen, and moral progress consists in seeing it better. This moral realism does not imply uniform rules: moral truth is often the truth of a particular situation or person. Her realism runs not through human nature, as [Foot](../philosophers/philippa-foot.md)'s does, but through Plato.

*Sources : "Vision and Choice in Morality" (1956) · The Sovereignty of Good, "The Idea of Perfection"*

### [Vision of humanity](../axes/human-vision.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Anthropological pessimism 75% · Anthropological optimism 25%

For [Murdoch](../philosophers/iris-murdoch.md), the self is a **fat relentless ego**, naturally egocentric, and she credits [Freud](../philosophers/freud.md) with giving a realistic description of the soul. This pessimism remains moderate: [attention](../glossary/attention.md) can correct it, slowly and without ever overcoming it.

*Sources : The Sovereignty of Good, "On 'God' and 'Good'"*

### [Essence and existence](../axes/essence.md)

*positioned · inferable · minor*

Essentialism 70% · Existentialism 30%

Having read [Sartre](../philosophers/sartre.md) sympathetically, [Murdoch](../philosophers/iris-murdoch.md) parts ways with him: what she reads in him as an empty freedom, choosing itself with nothing before it, overlooks that we have a psyche with constant, describable traits, above all a stubborn egocentrism, which precedes our choices and weighs on them. She therefore rejects the existentialist thesis that the human being is nothing but what it makes of itself. Yet this nature does not, for her, carry ends to be fulfilled in Aristotle's sense: it is rather what has to be corrected, hence a moderate position.

*Sources : Sartre: Romantic Rationalist (1953) · The Sovereignty of Good, "The Idea of Perfection"*

### [Moral demandingness](../axes/moral-demand.md)

*positioned · inferable · minor*

Maximalism 70% · Minimalism 30%

The idea of perfection makes morality an endless task: since the moral life takes place in how we look, it is present in every ordinary moment, and there is no morally neutral zone. [Murdoch](../philosophers/iris-murdoch.md) sets no threshold beyond which one would have done enough. The demand bears on the quality of our vision, however, more than on quantifiable material obligations, and she says nothing about how far we must help others, hence a moderate value.

*Sources : The Sovereignty of Good, "The Idea of Perfection"*

### [Criterion of right action](../axes/ethics-foundation.md)

*positioned · inferable · major*

Virtue ethics 80% · Ethics of care 20%

[Murdoch](../philosophers/iris-murdoch.md) puts at the centre character, vision and the virtues, humility and love foremost among them, rather than rules or calculations: she is counted among the forerunners of the revival of [virtue ethics](../glossary/virtue-ethics.md). Her ethics of loving attention to a singular person also bears a kinship with care ethics, which theorists such as [Sara Ruddick](../philosophers/sara-ruddick.md) explicitly drew on, hence a small share of that pole: a doctrinal affinity rather than membership.

*Sources : The Sovereignty of Good (1970) · Sara Ruddick, Maternal Thinking (1989), on "attentive love"*

### [Nature of love](../axes/love-nature.md)

*positioned · inferable · major*

Agape as gift 70% · Eros as lack 30%

For [Murdoch](../philosophers/iris-murdoch.md), love means stepping outside oneself to recognize the reality of another, asking nothing of them, which brings her close to [agape](../glossary/agape.md). But in *[The Fire and the Sun](../works/fire-and-the-sun-murdoch.md)* she takes up the [eros](../glossary/eros.md) of the *[Symposium](../works/symposium-plato.md)*, the desire for what we lack, and makes it, once purified, the energy that lifts the soul toward the Good. Desire is thus not to be abolished but redirected, hence a real share for that pole.

> “Love is the extremely difficult realisation that something other than oneself is real.”, "The Sublime and the Good", Chicago Review, vol. 13, no. 3 (1959), p. 51

*Sources : "The Sublime and the Good" (1959) · The Fire and the Sun (1977)*

Neither lack nor gift alone accounts for Murdochian love, which is first of all a form of knowledge: seeing the other as they are.

### [Self-interest and others](../axes/self-interest.md)

*positioned · inferable · minor*

Altruism 85% · Ethical egoism 15%

For [Murdoch](../philosophers/iris-murdoch.md), [unselfing](../glossary/unselfing.md) is the very movement of virtue. It is not a sacrifice but a shift of attention away from the self, toward what exists without it. It does not by itself set a systematic priority of others over oneself, but it strips self-interest of its role as a criterion, hence a clearly altruistic value short of the extreme.

*Sources : The Sovereignty of Good, "The Sovereignty of Good over Other Concepts"*

### [Existence of God](../axes/god-existence.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · minor*

Atheism 80% · Theism 20%

[Murdoch](../philosophers/iris-murdoch.md) says she does not believe in a personal God, while describing herself as close to Christianity and [Buddhism](../movements/buddhism.md) through their practices. She does not reduce religion to an illusion: she keeps its language of prayer and grace, and transfers to the Good part of what theology said of God. She thus denies a personal God without denying every reality that surpasses us, hence a moderate value.

*Sources : The Sovereignty of Good, "On 'God' and 'Good'" · Metaphysics as a Guide to Morals (1992)*

### [After death](../axes/afterlife.md)

*positioned · inferable · minor*

Nothing survives 100%

We are, [Murdoch](../philosophers/iris-murdoch.md) writes, transient mortal creatures subject to necessity and chance; she does not envisage personal survival. Accepting death is even, for her, a school of [unselfing](../glossary/unselfing.md).

*Sources : The Sovereignty of Good, "The Sovereignty of Good over Other Concepts"*

### [Attention and diversion](../axes/attention.md)

*positioned · inferable · minor*

Recollection 80% · Diversion embraced 20%

For [Murdoch](../philosophers/iris-murdoch.md), [attention](../glossary/attention.md) is a discipline, opposed to the complacent daydreaming in which the self consoles itself. She does not condemn diversion as such, but the drift that turns our gaze away from what is, hence a clear but not extreme value.

*Sources : The Sovereignty of Good (1970)*

### [Beauty](../axes/beauty.md)

*positioned · inferable · minor*

Objective beauty 80% · Subjective beauty 20%

The kestrel example: while I brood over a wound to my vanity, the sight of a hovering bird suddenly makes me forget that self. For [Murdoch](../philosophers/iris-murdoch.md), the beauty of nature is the most obvious occasion for [unselfing](../glossary/unselfing.md). Above all she takes from [Plato](../philosophers/plato.md) the idea that beauty is the visible face of the Good, and holds the excellence of great art to be a quality we discover rather than decree: beauty is not, for her, a projection of taste.

*Sources : The Sovereignty of Good, "The Sovereignty of Good over Other Concepts" · The Fire and the Sun (1977)*

### [Nature of art](../axes/art-nature.md)

*positioned · inferable · minor*

Mimesis 75% · Creation 25%

In "Against Dryness", [Murdoch](../philosophers/iris-murdoch.md) defends the realism of the great nineteenth-century novelists, who create free and opaque characters, against the "dry" novel reduced to a symbol and against art as self-expression. Invention is worth something only if it makes reality more visible.

*Sources : "Against Dryness" (1961)*

### [Beauty in life](../axes/aesthetic-life.md)

*positioned · inferable · minor*

Primacy of the ethical 70% · Aestheticism 30%

The sovereignty of the Good extends over the beautiful: for [Murdoch](../philosophers/iris-murdoch.md), beauty is a path to the Good, not an end, and the aestheticism that makes one's life a work of art is a refined form of [fantasy](../glossary/fantasy.md). She nonetheless grants art a very high moral dignity, hence a moderate value.

*Sources : The Sovereignty of Good, "The Sovereignty of Good over Other Concepts"*

### [Letter or spirit of the rule](../axes/rule-spirit.md)

*positioned · inferable · minor*

Equity-phronesis 75% · Legalism 25%

For [Murdoch](../philosophers/iris-murdoch.md), moral concepts have a depth that grows with experience: what "courage" or "justice" means changes as we learn to see. Applying a rule therefore calls for a look at the particular situation that the rule itself does not supply. Yet in *[Metaphysics as a Guide to Morals](../works/metaphysics-guide-morals-murdoch.md)* she keeps political **axioms**, such as the rights of the person, which must not be subjected to each individual's vision, hence a moderate value.

*Sources : The Sovereignty of Good, "The Idea of Perfection" · Metaphysics as a Guide to Morals, "Morals and Politics"*

### [The point of philosophy](../axes/metaphilosophy.md)

*positioned · inferable · minor*

Transforming oneself 60% · Understanding the world 40%

A moral philosophy, for [Murdoch](../philosophers/iris-murdoch.md), should offer a picture of the human being that one can inhabit and that helps one become better: she judges theories by what they make of those who adopt them. This practical concern goes with a contemplation of Platonic inspiration.

*Sources : The Sovereignty of Good, "On 'God' and 'Good'"*

### [Place of science](../axes/science-place.md)

*positioned · inferable · minor*

Epistemic pluralism 80% · Scientism 20%

Against an idea of truth modelled on science, which [Murdoch](../philosophers/iris-murdoch.md) holds responsible for the impoverishment of moral philosophy, she argues that moral truth also runs through literature, which is especially apt to make the opacity of persons perceptible.

*Sources : "Against Dryness" (1961)*

## Influenced by

- **[Sigmund Freud](../philosophers/freud.md)** (reinterprets) : [Murdoch](../philosophers/iris-murdoch.md) takes from [Freud](../philosophers/freud.md) what she considers a realistic description of the psyche, egocentric and driven by an energy it does not master; she sees it as the starting point of the moral task, not as an explanation of morality.
- **[Plato](../philosophers/plato.md)** (reinterprets) : [Murdoch](../philosophers/iris-murdoch.md) takes up [Plato](../philosophers/plato.md)'s Good, the sun that makes things visible, and the eros that lifts the soul, but without God or a world of Forms: a demythologized Platonism in which the turning of the gaze becomes moral attention.
- **[Gilbert Ryle](../philosophers/ryle.md)** (reacts against) : Against the critique of inner life inherited from [Ryle](../philosophers/ryle.md), for whom mind reduces to dispositions to act, [Murdoch](../philosophers/iris-murdoch.md) defends the reality of an inner moral activity that shows in no conduct.
- **[Sartre](../philosophers/sartre.md)** (reacts against) : An early and sympathetic reader of [Sartre](../philosophers/sartre.md), [Murdoch](../philosophers/iris-murdoch.md) defines herself against his freedom as the sovereign choice of a lone self: for her the moral life turns on seeing a reality that does not depend on us.
- **[Simone Weil](../philosophers/simone-weil.md)** (continues) : [Murdoch](../philosophers/iris-murdoch.md) says she borrows the word [attention](../glossary/attention.md) from [Simone Weil](../philosophers/simone-weil.md) to name a just and loving gaze directed on an individual reality; she keeps its moral sense while detaching it from prayer.
