# Martin Heidegger

*1889 – 1976 · German · [Phenomenology](../movements/phenomenology.md)*

A pupil and then the successor of [Husserl](../philosophers/husserl.md) at Freiburg, he turned [phenomenology](../movements/phenomenology.md) into an inquiry into the meaning of being, conducted through the analysis of human existence, which he calls [Dasein](../glossary/dasein.md). *[Being and Time](../works/being-and-time-heidegger.md)* (1927), left unfinished, renewed twentieth-century philosophy; from the 1930s the "turn" led him toward the history of being, the poetry of Hölderlin and, after the war, toward a meditation on technology (*[The Question Concerning Technology](../works/question-concerning-technology-heidegger.md)*, a 1953 lecture). A member of the Nazi party from 1933 to 1945 and rector of the University of Freiburg in 1933-1934, he was barred from teaching after the war as part of denazification and could teach again only in 1951. The publication from 2014 of the *[Black Notebooks](../works/black-notebooks-heidegger.md)*, which contain antisemitic passages, reopened the debate over the links between his thought and that commitment. His influence runs through [Sartre](../philosophers/sartre.md), [Arendt](../philosophers/arendt.md), [Levinas](../philosophers/levinas.md), [Merleau-Ponty](../philosophers/merleau-ponty.md), the hermeneutics of [Gadamer](../philosophers/gadamer.md), the deconstruction of [Derrida](../philosophers/derrida.md) and the [Kyoto School](../movements/kyoto-school.md), as well as the criticism of his own pupils, [Jonas](../philosophers/jonas.md) and [Anders](../philosophers/anders.md).

## Core positions

1. [Death](../axes/death.md) : **Death is not an event that will happen one day among others: it is each person's ownmost possibility, and it is by anticipating it that existence tears itself away from the anonymous "[one](../glossary/das-man.md) dies" and becomes its own.**
2. [Relation to technology](../axes/technology.md) : **What threatens in technology is not the machine but a mode of revealing, [enframing](../glossary/gestell.md), which lets nothing appear any longer, the human included, except as standing-reserve.**
3. [Purpose of art](../axes/art-purpose.md) : **The work of art is neither an object of pleasure nor a copy: it opens up a world, and truth happens in it in an eminent way, alongside thinking and poetic speech.**
4. [Place of science](../axes/science-place.md) : **Science grasps of the real only what its prior projection allows it to objectify: by its own means alone it cannot question the being of what it studies, and the work of art and poetic speech give a more originary access to it.**

## Positions

### [Death](../axes/death.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Living in view of one's death 100%

Division Two of *[Being and Time](../works/being-and-time-heidegger.md)* opens with a problem: how can existence be grasped as a whole, when it is never complete as long as it lasts? The analysis of [being-toward-death](../glossary/being-toward-death.md) answers without waiting for an end, as one would wait for the end of an object. Everything turns on an ordinary phrase: "one dies". The "[they](../glossary/das-man.md)" speaks truly, everyone will die, but it turns death into a fact that concerns everybody and never me. Yet one can die for another, not take away another's own dying: death is the ownmost possibility, certain and indefinite as to its moment. Anxiety reveals what idle talk tranquillizes, and anticipation of this possibility, joined to the resoluteness that answers the call of conscience, lets one own one's choices, in the light of their finitude: that is [authentic](../glossary/authenticity.md) existence.

*Sources : Being and Time, §46-53 · Being and Time, §40 (anxiety)*

### [Relation to technology](../axes/technology.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Techno-criticism 90% · Techno-optimism 10%

[Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md) starts from the current definition, technology as a means in the service of human ends, and calls it "correct" but not "true": it misses the essence, which rules out instrumentalism from the start. Modern technology is a mode of revealing that no longer lets things come forth, as craftsmanly poiesis did, but "challenges" them (herausfordern): the Rhine is ordered to deliver its energy to the power plant, the forest its cellulose, everything becomes standing-reserve (Bestand), a stock always on call. The human being is itself requisitioned by this challenging and threatened with understanding itself only in terms of availability. The gathering of all these ways of ordering, [enframing](../glossary/gestell.md) (Gestell), is the supreme danger, for it risks closing off every other relation to being. Heidegger nonetheless refuses to curse the apparatus: he quotes Hölderlin (“where danger is, grows the saving power also”) and recommends, in the 1955 speech collected in *[Discourse on Thinking](../works/gelassenheit-heidegger.md)*, a "yes and no" to technical objects: his critique targets the essence, not the instruments.

*Sources : The Question Concerning Technology (lecture 1953, publ. 1954) · Discourse on Thinking ("Memorial Address", 1955, publ. 1959)*

### [Purpose of art](../axes/art-purpose.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Truth 100%

In *[The Origin of the Work of Art](../works/origin-of-the-work-of-art-heidegger.md)*, the work is neither an object of enjoyment nor the copy of a thing: it is a "setting-into-work of truth". Heidegger interprets the shoes painted by Van Gogh as giving access to the world of a peasant woman, a reading the art historian Meyer Schapiro contested; the Greek temple, which represents nothing, opens up a world (the horizon of meaning of a historical people) and lets the earth appear (what withdraws and bears that world). Against aesthetics, which in his view reduces art to lived experience and pleasure, art is an eminent mode of [truth](../glossary/aletheia.md) as unconcealment.

*Sources : The Origin of the Work of Art (written 1935-1936, publ. 1950)*

### [Place of science](../axes/science-place.md)

*positioned · inferable · major*

Epistemic pluralism 85% · Scientism 15%

In *[The Age of the World Picture](../works/age-of-the-world-picture-heidegger.md)*, modern science becomes "research": it marks out its domain by constituting phenomena as objects according to a prior projection, nature as a calculable ensemble of motions. *[What Is Metaphysics?](../works/what-is-metaphysics-heidegger.md)* notes that it wants to know nothing of the nothing, and *[What Is Called Thinking?](../works/what-is-called-thinking-heidegger.md)* that it does not think, in the sense he gives the word: not as a failing, but because it does not determine, by its own procedures alone, the ontological presuppositions that constitute its domain. Against this monopoly, *[The Origin of the Work of Art](../works/origin-of-the-work-of-art-heidegger.md)* and the commentaries on Hölderlin make the work and poetic speech ways of access to being. This widening does not hold for every method or every kind of knowledge: it favours thinking and poetry.

> “Science does not think.”, What Is Called Thinking?, lectures 1951-1952

*Sources : The Age of the World Picture (lecture 1938, publ. 1950) · What Is Metaphysics? (1929) · What Is Called Thinking? (lectures 1951-1952, publ. 1954)*

### [Humanity's place in nature](../axes/human-place-nature.md)

*positioned · inferable · minor*

Stewardship 100%

In *[Building Dwelling Thinking](../works/building-dwelling-thinking-heidegger.md)*, mortals dwell by "saving" the earth, which [Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md) spells out: to leave it to its own being, to spare it, not to master it or subjugate it, which is only one step from boundless exploitation. The *[Letter on Humanism](../works/letter-on-humanism-heidegger.md)* likewise takes from the human being the rank of lord of beings and assigns it a guardianship, that of being. This guardianship is not the moral responsibility of ecological management: it is a relation of letting-be set against technological [challenging](../glossary/gestell.md), which ecological readings of Heidegger have taken up under that name. It does not make the human one living being among others, since he maintains an abyss between human existence and the animal, "poor in world".

> “Man is not the lord of beings. Man is the shepherd of Being.”, Letter on Humanism (1946)

*Sources : Building Dwelling Thinking (lecture 1951, publ. 1954) · Letter on Humanism (written 1946, publ. 1947) · The Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics (lectures 1929-1930), §42-63*

### [Status of emotions](../axes/emotions.md)

*positioned · inferable · major*

The primacy of feeling 75% · The primacy of reason 25%

Attunement (Befindlichkeit) is not a disturbance that would colour a neutral cognition after the fact: it has "always already disclosed [being-in-the-world](../glossary/being-in-the-world.md) as a whole" (§29), equiprimordial with understanding. Anxiety reveals the nothing and the bareness of existence; profound boredom discloses beings as a whole. Theoretical knowledge, too, presupposes an affective disclosure of the world. This is not a primacy of feeling over reason, but a refusal to treat affect as an obstacle to truth.

*Sources : Being and Time, §29-30, §40 · What Is Metaphysics? (1929)*

### [Attention and diversion](../axes/attention.md)

*positioned · inferable · minor*

Recollection 90% · Diversion embraced 10%

The analysis of everydayness describes idle talk, curiosity and ambiguity as modes of a falling in which existence scatters and flees from itself (§35-38); this agitation is a flight in the face of death, which the "[they](../glossary/das-man.md)" tranquillizes (§51). In 1955 [Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md) diagnoses a "flight from thinking": busy calculative thinking never pauses for meditative thinking, which asks us to dwell on what is near. This is not a theory of diversion, but his answer is to gather one's attention on what matters rather than be distracted from it.

*Sources : Being and Time, §35-38, §51 · Discourse on Thinking ("Memorial Address", 1955, publ. 1959)*

### [Nature of art](../axes/art-nature.md)

*positioned · inferable · minor*

Creation 75% · Mimesis 25%

*[The Origin of the Work of Art](../works/origin-of-the-work-of-art-heidegger.md)* rejects the idea that the work reproduces a particular being: the temple represents nothing, it brings a world about. Yet this is not the invention of genius: [Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md) decentres the artist in favour of the work, which "founds" a truth. Creation, for him, is an event of coming-to-pass rather than a making.

*Sources : The Origin of the Work of Art (written 1935-1936, publ. 1950)*

### [The point of philosophy](../axes/metaphilosophy.md)

*positioned · inferable · major*

Understanding the world 100%

[Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md) refuses to assign thinking a use: in 1966 he declares that philosophy will not be able to bring about any immediate change in the present state of the world, and the *[Letter on Humanism](../works/letter-on-humanism-heidegger.md)* states that thinking "acts insofar as it thinks". To think is to answer the call of being, not to transform the world or heal the soul. It is in this sense that he stands on the side of understanding, with one reservation: this thinking is not a systematic knowledge of the order of things.

*Sources : Letter on Humanism (written 1946, publ. 1947) · Der Spiegel interview (1966, publ. 1976)*

### [Relation to authority](../axes/authority.md)

*positioned · inferable · minor*

Authority 75% · Anarchism 25%

A dated position: the rectorial address (1933) places the university within an order of leading and following (Führung, Gefolgschaft) and subjects it to the three "services" of labour, defence and knowledge. In 1966 [Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md) still says he is "not convinced" that democracy is the regime suited to the technological age. He builds no philosophy of the state: for him the political remains subordinate to the history of being.

*Sources : The Self-Assertion of the German University (1933) · Der Spiegel interview (1966, publ. 1976)*

### [Belonging](../axes/belonging.md)

*positioned · inferable · minor*

Communitarianism and patriotism 75% · Cosmopolitanism 25%

Existence is historical and stands within the heritage of a people: *[Being and Time](../works/being-and-time-heidegger.md)* thinks the shared destiny of a community (§74), and the texts of the 1930s bind thinking to the native soil and to the German language, which he holds, with Greek, to be the most fit for thinking. These texts leave no room for cosmopolitanism. In the *[Letter on Humanism](../works/letter-on-humanism-heidegger.md)*, homelessness receives a sense tied to the remoteness of being, which is not reducible to the loss of a national territory.

*Sources : Being and Time, §74 · Why Do I Stay in the Provinces? (1934) · Introduction to Metaphysics (lectures 1935, publ. 1953) · Letter on Humanism (written 1946, publ. 1947)*

### [Essence and existence](../axes/essence.md)

*rejects the alternative*

In §9 of Being and Time, "the 'essence' of Dasein lies in its existence"; but the Letter on Humanism rejects Sartre's formula, "existence precedes essence", a mere reversal that in his view remains captive to metaphysics. Ek-sistence, standing out of oneself into the openness of being, is neither a given essence nor a self-invention.

### [Nature of reality](../axes/reality-nature.md)

*rejects the alternative*

Being and Time deconstructs the Cartesian split between thinking thing and extended thing (§19-21) and makes "reality" one mode of being among others (§43): materialism, idealism and dualism share the presuppositions he contests.

### [Freedom of the will](../axes/freedom.md)

*rejects the alternative*

Freedom is not a property of the will, the object of the debate between free will and determinism: it is first "letting beings be" (On the Essence of Truth, lecture 1930, publ. 1943); man does not possess freedom, freedom possesses man.

## Influenced by

- **[Aristotle](../philosophers/aristotle.md)** (reinterprets) : [Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md)'s lecture courses of the 1920s are to a large extent readings of [Aristotle](../philosophers/aristotle.md): he rereads phronesis as an understanding of existence in its situation and ousia as presence, materials that *[Being and Time](../works/being-and-time-heidegger.md)* transforms into the analytic of [Dasein](../glossary/dasein.md).
- **[Dilthey](../philosophers/dilthey.md)** (reinterprets) : [Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md) takes from [Dilthey](../philosophers/dilthey.md) the question of historical life and its understanding, to which he devotes a section of *[Being and Time](../works/being-and-time-heidegger.md)*. He reproaches him for not having raised the question of the being of that life.
- **[Husserl](../philosophers/husserl.md)** (reinterprets) : Husserl's assistant and then successor, [Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md) shifts [phenomenology](../movements/phenomenology.md) from transcendental consciousness toward the question of being: no longer describing lived experiences, but existence as being-in-the-world.
- **[Kierkegaard](../philosophers/kierkegaard.md)** (reinterprets) : [Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md) takes from [Kierkegaard](../philosophers/kierkegaard.md) anxiety, the moment and existence as a task, detaching them from their Christian horizon: the notes of *[Being and Time](../works/being-and-time-heidegger.md)* acknowledge the debt while judging Kierkegaard's analysis to have remained "existentiell" rather than ontological.
- **[Nietzsche](../philosophers/nietzsche.md)** (reinterprets) : [Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md) devotes extensive lecture courses to [Nietzsche](../philosophers/nietzsche.md), whom he reads as the last great metaphysician: the will to power and nihilism become the completion and the destiny of Western metaphysics.
- **[Romanticism](../movements/romanticism.md)** (reinterprets) : [Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md) takes up from Romanticism its critique of the world-as-machine and of calculative reason, its listening to nature and its privileging of the poetic as access to being. His reading of Hölderlin, the Romantic poet held to voice the « default of the gods » and the danger of technology, lies at the heart of his late thought: « to dwell poetically » extends the Romantic dream of a re-enchanted world.

## Influence on

- **[Hannah Arendt](../philosophers/arendt.md)** (reinterprets) : [Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md)'s student, [Arendt](../philosophers/arendt.md) shifts his analysis of existence toward the political: against the primacy of death, she thinks natality, the capacity to begin, and concerted action among human beings as the proper site of freedom.
- **[Philosophical hermeneutics](../movements/hermeneutics.md)** (continues) : [Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md) turned understanding from a method into a mode of being of [Dasein](../glossary/dasein.md), always caught in a circle of anticipations. [Gadamer](../philosophers/gadamer.md), his student, built [philosophical hermeneutics](../movements/hermeneutics.md) on this thesis.
- **[Kyoto School](../movements/kyoto-school.md)** (reinterprets) : [Tanabe](../philosophers/tanabe.md), [Nishitani](../philosophers/nishitani.md) and Kuki Shūzō studied with [Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md) in Freiburg. The [Kyoto School](../movements/kyoto-school.md) took up his question of nothingness and his reading of nihilism and carried them toward Buddhist emptiness, where Heidegger remained within the horizon of being.
- **[Merleau-Ponty](../philosophers/merleau-ponty.md)** (reinterprets) : [Merleau-Ponty](../philosophers/merleau-ponty.md) takes up [Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md)'s being-in-the-world and the pre-theoretical world, but redirects them to the perceiving body: it is the flesh, not the analytic of being, that originally opens the world.
- **[Sartre](../philosophers/sartre.md)** (reinterprets) : [Sartre](../philosophers/sartre.md) existentialises [Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md)'s analyses, being-in-the-world, facticity, anxiety, within a framework centred on freedom and project, which Heidegger would reject as a humanist misreading.
- **[Jonas](../philosophers/jonas.md)** (reacts against) : A pupil of [Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md), [Jonas](../philosophers/jonas.md) breaks with his existentialism, indifferent to nature, to found an ontology of the living and an ethics of [responsibility](../glossary/responsibility-principle.md) toward future generations. In "Gnosticism, Existentialism, and Nihilism" he likens existence thrown into a normless world to Gnostic dualism; in "Heidegger and Theology" (1964) he contests the theological use of his teacher's late thought, having experienced his 1933 commitment as a betrayal.
- **[Emmanuel Levinas](../philosophers/levinas.md)** (reacts against) : [Levinas](../philosophers/levinas.md) builds his thought in close contestation of [Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md): against the primacy of ontology, he posits ethics as first philosophy, the face of the other preceding any understanding of being.
