# John Jamieson Carswell Smart

*1920 – 2012 · British, later Australian · [Analytic Philosophy](../movements/analytic-philosophy.md)*

British, later Australian philosopher, chief architect of the mind-brain identity theory and of "Australian materialism": sensations are not correlated with brain processes, they are identical to them. He also defended act utilitarianism and a scientific realism.

**External links** : [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._J._C._Smart) · [Wikidata](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q600759)

## Positions

### [The enigma of consciousness](../axes/consciousness-nature.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Physicalism 100%

Smart argues that sensations are not merely correlated with brain processes: they are identical to them, as lightning is an electrical discharge. This identity theory rests on a principle of economy: if everything else in the living world is explained in physico-chemical terms, making consciousness the sole exception would force us to admit dangling laws, with no role in the edifice of science. It is objected that one can know one's own sensation while knowing nothing of the brain: he answers with a topic-neutral analysis, saying "I have an orange after-image" amounting only to saying that something is going on in me like what goes on when I see an orange. The statement says nothing of the nature of that "something", and nothing forbids its being a brain process.

*Sources : "Sensations and Brain Processes" (1959) · Philosophy and Scientific Realism (1963)*

### [Wholes and parts](../axes/reduction-emergence.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Reductionism 93% · Emergentism 8%

Smart holds that an explanation is complete only when carried back to physical constituents and their interactions. Admitting properties that would escape this analysis would force supernumerary laws, with no role in the edifice of science, which a principle of economy forbids. His identity theory applies this [reductionism](../glossary/reductionism.md) to mind: not one more property arising from the brain, but the same processes, described in another vocabulary.

*Sources : Philosophy and Scientific Realism (1963)*
