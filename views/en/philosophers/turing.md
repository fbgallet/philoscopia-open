# Alan Turing

*1912 – 1954 · British · [Analytic Philosophy](../movements/analytic-philosophy.md)*

British mathematician and logician, one of the founders of computer science: to get past the question "can a machine think?", which he judged too vague, he proposes a test, the imitation game, where what counts is not the machine's nature but its ability to pass for a mind.

**External links** : [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Turing) · [Wikidata](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q7251)

## Positions

### [Can a machine think](../axes/machine-mind.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

The substrate does not matter 100%

Turing judges the question "can a machine think?" too dependent on the use of words to be settled, and replaces it with a test, the [imitation game](../thought-experiments/turing-test.md): an interrogator converses blind with a human and a machine; if they can no longer tell them apart, there is no longer a reason to refuse to one what is granted to the other. To the objection that the machine would still lack consciousness of what it says, he answers that pressing it leads to solipsism: I know others think only by their behaviour, and refusing that same evidence to machines alone is arbitrary. The substrate therefore matters little, a universal machine being able to simulate any discrete-state machine.

> “The original question, "Can machines think?", I believe to be too meaningless to deserve discussion.”, "Computing Machinery and Intelligence", Mind (1950)

*Sources : "Computing Machinery and Intelligence", Mind (1950)*

Turing is an operationalist before he is a functionalist: he shifts the question toward an observable criterion rather than defining mental states by their causal role, a thesis later than him. His dated wager on the year 2000 concerns feasibility, not principle.
