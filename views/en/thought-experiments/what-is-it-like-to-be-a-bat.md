# What is it like to be a bat?

*Thomas Nagel, *"What Is It Like to Be a Bat?"*, 1974*

The bat perceives the world by echolocation: it emits cries and reads in their echoes the shape, distance and motion of things. A biologist can describe that system completely, down to the last neuron. But it would remain to be known what it is, **for the bat**, to perceive in this way. And we can imagine it only by imagining ourselves as a bat, which is not the same thing: that would be what it is like for a human being to behave as it does, not what it is like for the bat to be itself.

**Would a completed science of the bat's brain tell us what it is like to be one?**

**Axes at stake** : [The enigma of consciousness](../axes/consciousness-nature.md)

**Possible stances** :

- No: there is a fact here that physical description lets slip. Reality has a lived face that measurement does not reach.
- No, but that is a limit of ours, not of the world: its experience is physical through and through, we simply lack the point of view from which to grasp it.
- The question reaches far beyond bats: if there is something it is like to be one, why not, in vanishing degrees, everywhere in matter?
- The question wrongly assumes there is a "what it is like" to capture: introspection makes us believe in an inner glow that does not exist as such.
