# The collective afterlife

*Samuel Scheffler, *Death and the Afterlife*, 2013*

Imagine that you will live a normal life until you die, but that thirty days later the Earth will be destroyed, and every human being with it. Or imagine that, overnight, no more children are born: humanity will die out with the last living generation. In both cases nothing changes for you personally: you will not suffer more, you will die on the expected date. Only one thing disappears: the assurance that others will live after you.

**If humanity were to die out shortly after your death, would what you do keep the same value for you?**

## What it does in the debate

### Raises the problem of [Temporal orientation](../axes/time-orientation.md)

If humanity died out after us, what would what we build and hand on still be worth?

### Raises the problem of [The meaning of life](../axes/life-meaning.md)

Does the meaning of our activities depend on humanity's survival more than on our own?

**Possible stances** :

- Much would lose its point: researching, building, teaching, raising, all of it assumes there will be someone after us.
- Nothing essential would change: what matters has value now, for those who are alive.
- Some things would keep their value (loving, contemplating, playing), others would lose it (research, institutions, handing things on).
