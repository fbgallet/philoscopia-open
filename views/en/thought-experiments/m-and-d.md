# The mother-in-law and her daughter-in-law

*Iris Murdoch, *"The Idea of Perfection" (1964), reprinted in The Sovereignty of Good (1970)*, 1964*

A woman, M, feels hostility toward her daughter-in-law, D. She finds her vulgar, unpolished, rather insolent, and thinks her son has made a poor marriage. Yet M always behaves perfectly toward D: nothing in her conduct betrays what she thinks. Over time M tells herself that she may be snobbish, jealous, trapped in her prejudices, and she makes herself pay patient [attention](../glossary/attention.md) to D. Little by little D no longer seems vulgar to her but simple and frank, no longer insolent but spontaneous and cheerful. Nothing has happened outwardly: D may have gone to live abroad, or even died, in the meantime.

**Has something moral happened in M's mind, even though nothing has changed in her actions?**

**Axes at stake** : [Interiority](../axes/interiority.md)

## What it does in the debate

### Objects to [Interiority](../axes/interiority.md) : Primacy of action — attacks the position

Moral progress can occur without any act that shows it.

**The argument it makes**

1. If the moral life came down to observable acts, M's change would have no moral significance, since her conduct toward D stays the same.
2. Yet we judge that M has made real moral progress: she has become more just toward D.
3. This progress does not lie in a more flattering opinion but in an attention less distorted by jealousy and prejudice: M sees D more accurately.

**⇒** So the moral life does not come down to acts: it includes the inner work of attention.

- **I grant it: the way we look at others is already moral.**
- **The change counts only because it will shape how she acts later.** : An action-centred reading can reply that M has changed her dispositions to act. It is against this picture of the agent, [Hampshire](../philosophers/stuart-hampshire.md)'s in *Thought and Action* (1959), that [Murdoch](../philosophers/iris-murdoch.md) builds the example: M's conduct toward D stays the same from start to finish, and D may even have left or died. If the change counts, it counts first as a change of vision.
- **What never shows in action carries no moral weight.**
