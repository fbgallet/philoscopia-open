# The Chinese Room

*Searle, *Minds, Brains, and Programs*, 1980*

You are locked in a room. Through a slot, sheets covered in Chinese symbols are passed to you, symbols you do not understand in the slightest. But you have a thick rulebook, written in your own language, that tells you: "to this mark, reply with that one." You follow the rules, you pass sheets back, and outside a Chinese reader finds your answers flawless: to them, the room understands Chinese. You, though, have understood nothing. A machine that handles our words, a chatbot that answers so well, is it doing anything other than what you do in that room?

**Is running a program, however flawless, enough to **understand**, or does handling **symbols** by their form alone stay blind to their **meaning**?**

**Axes at stake** : [Can a machine think](../axes/machine-mind.md)

## What it does in the debate

### Objects to [Can a machine think](../axes/machine-mind.md) : The substrate does not matter — attacks the position

In the room the whole required organization is realized, and yet no one understands a word of Chinese: realizing the right program is therefore not enough to understand.

**The argument it makes**

1. In the room, everything functionalism asks for is assembled: the same inputs, the same intermediate states, the same outputs as a Chinese speaker. The organization is realized in full.
2. Yet whoever applies the rules understands nothing: he does not even know what the sheets he hands back are about. He has the shape of the signs, never their meaning.
3. If organization were what makes understanding, it would be there, present in the room. It is not.

**⇒** So realizing the right organization is not enough to think: something is missing that the program, by construction, cannot supply.

- **Running a program is not understanding; but an artifact reproducing the causal powers of the brain, or a body's engagement, could.** : This is exactly [Searle](../philosophers/searle.md)'s conclusion: his target is the program, not machines in general.
- **What the room lacks is dealing with the world on its own account: understanding belongs to what lives, not to what is manufactured.**
- **The man alone does not understand, but the whole system, he, the rulebook and the room, does: no isolated neuron understands either.** : The systems reply, which [Searle](../philosophers/searle.md) dismisses by imagining the man learning the rulebook by heart: objection and reply both turn on where understanding is to be located.

**Puts to the test** : [The multiple-realizability argument](../arguments/functionalism-multiple-realizability.md)

**Possible stances** :

- The room understands nothing: arranging symbols by their form is not grasping their meaning, and running a program does not suffice for it.
- The man alone does not understand, but the whole system, he and the rulebook and the room together, genuinely does.
- Hard to decide: perhaps understanding is, in the end, nothing more than that.
- If a system carries out all the functions by which we understand, it understands: requiring some hidden understanding on top is a prejudice.
