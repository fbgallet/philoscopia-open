# The Dark Knight

*The Dark Knight Trilogy : **The Dark Knight***

*Christopher Nolan, Jonathan Nolan and Christopher Nolan (screenplay), from a story by Christopher Nolan and David S. Goyer, 2008*

*The 2008 film alone; neither Batman Begins nor The Dark Knight Rises is used as evidence.*

A masked vigilante, an upright prosecutor and an honest cop are about to free Gotham from the mob. A new kind of criminal changes the game: he wants neither power nor fortune, he wants to prove something about the city's people. The film puts to the test two things we assume go together: trust in people, and the truth we owe them.

## The problem

**The common belief put to the test** : We usually assume that honest people are honest through and through, and that a well-defended city has nothing to hide from its inhabitants: the truth about those who govern it is owed to all.

Gotham is eaten away by the mob. Three men think they can free it: Batman, a masked vigilante who by day is the billionaire Bruce Wayne; Harvey Dent, the new district attorney; Jim Gordon, an honest cop in a police force that is not. Then comes the Joker, a criminal who wants neither money nor power. We usually hold two convictions together: people are better than we fear, and those who protect them owe them the truth. Step by step, the film builds a situation in which we can no longer keep both, and in which every way out costs something we took to be essential.

It all starts from a wager on what people are worth once they are pushed to the wall. The Joker has a thesis and he states it: the morals of "civilized" people only hold while things go well; at the first real blow, they will eat each other. His crimes are demonstrations. He traps his victims in rigged choices, then reads their reactions as proof of what they are. These ordeals, in his view, reveal human nature. [Hobbes](../philosophers/hobbes.md) pictured life without a common power as a war of every man against every man (the [state of nature](../glossary/etat-de-nature.md)), not because people are wicked, but because each has reason to fear the other; the Joker wants to create that state in the middle of a city, just to see. Against him, Alfred, Bruce's butler and confidant, tells a memory from Burma: a bandit who stole gemstones and threw them away. He could be neither bought nor reasoned with. Some men, Alfred concludes, just want to watch the world burn. If the Joker is right, a city's goodness rests only on what reassures it. Gotham seems half to believe it: it has placed its hope in a single man.

That man is Harvey Dent. Gotham sees in him the proof that the city can be cleaned up in the open and by the rules, where Batman works masked and in the shadows. Bruce Wayne himself counts on him to be able, one day, to hang up the cape. At a dinner, Dent recalls that in times of crisis Rome entrusted the city to a single man; Rachel Dawes, an assistant district attorney and Bruce's childhood friend, answers that the last one was Caesar, who never gave the power back. Dent replies with a line that foreshadows what follows: you either die a hero, or you live long enough to see yourself become the villain. The Joker, for his part, goes after Dent from his very first moves. He has understood that to strike a city there is no need to corrupt the crowd: it is enough to reach the one it believes in.

A city that lives on a symbol must also decide what it will know about it. The film raises this difficulty before anyone has fallen. Batman lies for a living: by day, Bruce Wayne plays the frivolous billionaire so that no one suspects what he does at night. When the Joker threatens to kill someone every day until Batman unmasks, Bruce decides to turn himself in, to stop the killings rather than out of love for the truth. (Spoilers : Dent stops him by declaring himself Batman before the press: the prosecutor who works without a mask lies to the whole city to trap the Joker.) An old question then returns: does a city hold together through the truth, or through what it believes? [Plato](../philosophers/plato.md) has the citizens of his ideal city told a [noble lie](../glossary/noble-lie.md) about their origins, so that they love it and keep to their place in it. [Dostoevsky](../philosophers/dostoevsky.md)'s Grand Inquisitor goes further: freedom weighs too heavily on human beings, who prefer bread, miracle and authority to it; a few take it from them for their own good and keep the truth to themselves. [Arendt](../philosophers/arendt.md) distinguishes the occasional lie, as old as politics, from organized lying, which destroys the shared facts without which there is no common world left. The film leaves these answers hanging while it puts the city and its symbol to the test.

**The question** : Is lying to people to protect them still believing in them?

## Other problems in the work

**How far may one go to stop a man?** *(The interrogation, the sonar)*

Batman has only one rule: do not kill. Everything else is negotiable: he abducts a mob accountant from Hong Kong, beats the Joker in the interrogation room, turns the city's phones into a sonar that sees everything. Lucius Fox, who runs Bruce's company and supplies his equipment, agrees to use it once while declaring it contrary to who he is. (Spoilers : The machine then destroys itself, as Batman had planned.) This is the problem of [dirty hands](../glossary/dirty-hands.md): acting well seems to require what one judges wrong. (Spoilers : The interrogation also shows a practical limit: violence wrings addresses out of the Joker, but they are the ones he wanted to give.)

**A vigilante, by what right?** *(The opening, the dinner)*

Copycats in hockey pads play armed Batmen; one of them asks what makes him different from them; Batman answers with a quip (he isn't wearing hockey pads), which says nothing about what would ground his right. The dinner talk about Rome and Caesar asks the same question on the scale of the city: who grants an emergency power, and who takes it back?

## Concepts

- **[The state of nature](../glossary/etat-de-nature.md)** : Hobbes imagined life without a common power to show why we need one. The Joker does the reverse: he tries to produce that state in the middle of an organized city, to prove that order was only a veneer.
- **[Noble lie](../glossary/noble-lie.md)** : The film stages a modern version of the noble lie: a fable kept alive so that the city keeps faith in its institutions. (Spoilers : It is no longer a myth of origins, but the legend of a dead man.)
- **[Dirty hands](../glossary/dirty-hands.md)** : Batman will not let himself kill, yet he abducts, beats and spies on a whole city to stop the Joker. The film raises the problem of dirty hands: can one act well in politics without committing acts one judges wrong oneself?
- **[Paternalism](../glossary/paternalisme.md)** : (Spoilers : The final choice decides on behalf of a whole city what it may know, for its own good: the film gives paternalism the face of a sacrifice.)
- **[Moral luck](../glossary/moral-luck.md)** : (Spoilers : What Dent becomes hinges on a night he did not choose: the film questions how much of what we blame someone for belongs to circumstance.)

## What the work answers

The film sides neither with the Joker nor with a naive trust: it shows people who can give way, and others who hold when pushed. It then takes seriously a more unsettling question: may those who protect a city decide what it should know?

## What it leaves open

The ferry test is built by the story: two gestures are not a thesis about people. The Joker is a character, not a philosopher: his thesis is staged, not argued, and he rigs his own proofs. On release, the film was read as a defense of surveillance and post-9/11 emergency measures, and also as a critique of them; this page sticks to the story.
