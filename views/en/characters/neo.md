# Neo

*Character from « The Matrix » (Lana and Lilly Wachowski, 1999)*

Thomas Anderson, a programmer by day and a hacker by night under the name Neo, has long sensed that something is wrong with the world. When Morpheus reveals that this world is a simulation, he chooses to wake up from it without knowing what he will lose. A character of apprenticeship, he moves from suspicion to sight: he learns that the simulation's rules are only rules.

## Positions

### [The value of truth](../axes/truth-value.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

**Practiced** : The primacy of truth 93% · Vital illusions 8%

Neo takes the red pill blind, on the sole promise of the truth, and does not go back on that choice when reality turns out to be a desert, even though he throws up aboard the Nebuchadnezzar on learning what has become of the world. His search in fact comes before the revelation: as a hacker, he was already looking for what the Matrix is.

*Sources : The two pills scene · The loading program: the desert of the real · Neo's collapse aboard the Nebuchadnezzar*

His actions follow his words: (Spoilers : when the Oracle seems to deny him the role of the One and tells him he will have to choose between his life and Morpheus's, he goes back for Morpheus, and that choice makes him the One he was told he was not).

### [Scope of action](../axes/control-scope.md)

*positioned · inferable · minor*

**Practiced** : Prometheanism 80% · What is up to us 20%

His training is an apprenticeship in mastery: Morpheus teaches him that some of the simulation's rules can be bent and others broken, and the spoon boy at the Oracle's shows him that it is oneself that bends, not the spoon. An inner lesson, but one that turns into power over the world: Neo does not merely discern what depends on him, he pushes back its limit.
