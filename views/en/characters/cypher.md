# Cypher

*Character from « The Matrix » (Lana and Lilly Wachowski, 1999)*

A member of Morpheus's crew, Cypher took the red pill years ago and has never got over it. A figure of disenchanted lucidity, he is not fooled by the illusion: he misses it knowingly, and embodies the temptation the film must face for Neo's choice to have a price.

## Positions

### [The value of truth](../axes/truth-value.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

**Declared** : Vital illusions 95% · The primacy of truth 5%

**Practiced** : Vital illusions 98% · The primacy of truth 3%

One night, Cypher wonders aloud in front of Neo why he did not take the blue pill. (Spoilers : Sitting with Agent Smith over a steak he knows does not exist, he concludes that ignorance is bliss and bargains to be put back in, provided he remembers nothing; the Matrix, he will say, can be more real than this world.) The pleasure he finds there is no less real to him: it is the object that is simulated, not the enjoyment.

*Sources : The night conversation with Neo on the ship · The dinner with Agent Smith · The betrayal aboard the Nebuchadnezzar*

### [Freedom of the will](../axes/freedom.md)

*positioned · extrapolated · minor*

**Practiced** : Free will 70% · Hard determinism 30%

Cypher does not believe in the prophecy and acts as if the outcome depended on him alone. (Spoilers : As he betrays them, he says that if Neo is the One, it would take a miracle to stop him.) Rejecting a prophecy does not, however, amount to a view on free will, and he casts himself as a victim, deceived by Morpheus: the position is extrapolated.

*Sources : The betrayal aboard the Nebuchadnezzar*
