# Batman (Bruce Wayne)

*Character from « The Dark Knight » (Christopher Nolan, 2008)*

In Christopher Nolan's film, Bruce Wayne is a billionaire who plays at frivolity by day and fights Gotham's mob by night, behind Batman's mask. He hopes that district attorney Harvey Dent will one day make his role unnecessary. Facing the Joker, who wants to prove that people's morality is only a veneer, he holds to a single rule, do not kill, and allows himself almost everything else. The film follows him through the choices that rule leaves him, up to the question of what he must tell the city.

## Positions

### [Vision of humanity](../axes/human-vision.md)

*positioned · inferable · major*

**Practiced** : Anthropological optimism 70% · Anthropological pessimism 30%

Batman wants to believe the city can do without him: he bets on Harvey Dent to lift it up through the law. (Spoilers : Facing the Joker, he bets that the ferry passengers will not kill each other, and answers him that the city has just shown it is full of people ready to believe in good.) This trust is not naive: he knows the city is corrupt and acts as though it could go wrong.

*Sources : His conversations with Alfred about Harvey Dent · The ferries*

### [The value of truth](../axes/truth-value.md)

*positioned · inferable · major*

**Practiced** : Vital illusions 80% · The primacy of truth 20%

He lives on a lie: the frivolous billionaire covers for the vigilante. After the Joker's ultimatum, he nonetheless decides to turn himself in, to stop the killings rather than on a principle of truth. (Spoilers : In the end, he chooses to bear Dent's crimes so that the city keeps faith in him: sometimes, he says, the truth isn't good enough.) His acts place him on the side of [vital illusions](../axes/truth-value.md), for the city as for himself: the vigilante exists only through a secret.

*Sources : The Joker's ultimatum · The ending*

### [Criterion of right action](../axes/ethics-foundation.md)

*positioned · inferable · major*

**Practiced** : Consequentialism 50% · Deontologism 50%

Batman has only one rule, do not kill, and the Joker knows it: his whole game is to make him break it. (Spoilers : Batman even catches the falling Joker. Dent's death, when Batman tackles him to save Gordon's son, complicates the picture without refuting it: he accepts a deadly risk to save a child, he does not seek to kill.) His other means are judged by their expected effect: he abducts a mob accountant from Hong Kong, beats the Joker in the interrogation room, has every phone in the city tapped, for one night. He thus holds an [absolute prohibition](../glossary/deontologism.md) in the middle of a largely [consequentialist](../glossary/consequentialism.md) practice.

*Sources : Lau's abduction in Hong Kong · The Joker's interrogation · The sonar · The final confrontation with Dent*

### [Sincerity](../axes/sincerity.md)

*positioned · inferable · minor*

**Practiced** : Benevolent lying permitted 80% · Truthful rigorism 20%

His double life rests on a constant lie, to the city and to almost everyone he deals with; only a few close to him (Alfred, Rachel, Lucius Fox) know who he is, and an employee of his company who finds out threatens to reveal it. (Spoilers : His last choice extends this lie to all of Gotham, for its own good.)

*Sources : Bruce Wayne's double life · The ending*
