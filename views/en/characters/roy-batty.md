# Roy Batty

*Character from « Blade Runner » (Ridley Scott, 1982)*

A replicant built for combat in the off-world colonies, Batty has slipped back to Earth with other runaway replicants. Like all of his model, he is built to live only four years. The film first presents him as a threat, then gradually gives him a voice: that of a being who has seen what no human has seen, and who refuses to let others dispose of his life.

## Positions

### [Essence and existence](../axes/essence.md)

*positioned · inferable · major*

**Practiced** : Existentialism 85% · Essentialism 15%

Batty was designed for a function, combat, and for a span, four years. He refuses both: he has come back to Earth to get more life, against the plan of those who made him. (Spoilers : His last act is the opposite of his function: this combat model saves the man who came to kill him.) He makes himself through his acts rather than remaining what he was made for, which places him clearly on the side of [existentialism](../axes/essence.md), in the sense that [existence precedes essence](../glossary/existentialism.md). One caveat: whatever he achieves, he achieves with the abilities he was given, and the film does not say whether this surpassing comes from him or from his design.

*Sources : Bryant's briefing: a combat model · The meeting with Tyrell · The rooftop*

### [Moral circle](../axes/moral-circle.md)

*positioned · inferable · major*

**Practiced** : Pathocentrism 100%

Batty insists that manufactured beings count: himself, his kind, their fear and their deaths. His concern goes first to his own, and he kills the humans who stand in his way. (Spoilers : On the roof he makes Deckard feel the fear replicants live with, then saves him: he recognizes in his hunter a being who is afraid as he is.) The criterion that emerges from his acts is not origin but the capacity to suffer and to fear. [Pathocentrism](../axes/moral-circle.md) is the closest pole; extending it to every sentient being, beyond human or manufactured persons, would be an extrapolation.

*Sources : The meeting with Tyrell · The rooftop*

### [Death](../axes/death.md)

*positioned · inferable · major*

**Practiced** : Refusing death 55% · Living in view of one's death 45%

For almost the whole film, Batty refuses the end that was set for him: he comes back to Earth to get more life, and the consolation he is offered does not appease him. This is [revolt against death](../axes/death.md). (Spoilers : When Tyrell tells him it cannot be done, he kills him. Then, on the roof, he stops fighting: he says that everything he has seen will be lost, like tears in rain, and that it is time to die. This final consent does not disown the revolt: he regrets what death will destroy. But it is in facing his end that he performs his freest act, which brings him close to [being-toward-death](../axes/death.md), without its full scope: for [Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md), [being-toward-death](../glossary/being-toward-death.md) shapes a whole existence, not just its last moments.)

*Sources : The meeting with Tyrell · The monologue on the rooftop*

### [Political liberty](../axes/political-liberty.md)

*positioned · inferable · minor*

**Practiced** : Republican non-domination 100%

Batty and his kind belong to those who use them: treated well or badly, they remain at someone else's mercy, even in how long they get to live. (Spoilers : When he makes Deckard feel fear, he names his condition himself: living in fear is what it is to be a slave.) The freedom he claims is to no longer depend on another's arbitrary power, [non-domination](../axes/political-liberty.md), more than the mere absence of obstacles.

*Sources : The rooftop*

### [Violence](../axes/violence.md)

*positioned · extrapolated · minor*

**Practiced** : Emancipatory violence 100%

Batty uses violence to free himself from a condition he did not choose, which brings it close to [emancipatory violence](../axes/violence.md), the nearest pole. But it overflows that aim, and no pole of the axis accounts for the excess: intimidation, revenge and cruelty toward whoever stands in his way. (Spoilers : He kills his maker, breaks Deckard's fingers to avenge Zhora and Pris, then gives up killing him and saves him, without that act making him an opponent of all violence.)

*Sources : The meeting with Tyrell · The hunt in Sebastian's building*
