# The antibiotic

*Objection to [Moral circle](../axes/moral-circle.md) : Biocentrism — attacks the position*

**To live is to destroy lives beyond counting: if every life deserves respect, that respect no longer guides any act.**

## The argument

1. Biocentrism grants a value of its own to every living thing, sentient or not: the plant, the insect, the bacterium.
2. Yet every day we destroy them by the million: to cure an infection, an antibiotic kills billions of bacteria; walking, farming, building, we crush, uproot, displace. Even a plant-based diet feeds on lives. [Schweitzer](../philosophers/schweitzer.md), a doctor, knew he had to sacrifice some lives to save others.
3. A principle that condemns our every gesture no longer tells anything apart: either it forbids living, or we break it constantly and it becomes a mere formula.

**⇒** So respect for all life, taken seriously, is either paralysing or empty: a narrower criterion, such as [sentience](../glossary/sentience.md), is needed for respect to really guide our choices.

## Ways out of the objection

- **True: one cannot respect every bacterium. What should guide our choices is the capacity to suffer.** : You tighten the circle around sentient beings: narrower, it becomes workable again.
- **Not all lives have the same value, but none is nothing: destroying a life calls for a reason, and having to choose does not erase the loss.** : You keep respect for all life while admitting degrees. [Schweitzer](../philosophers/schweitzer.md) himself refused to rank lives, but granted that sacrifice is sometimes necessary, without ever treating the loss as indifferent.
- **Respect does not demand destroying nothing, but destroying nothing without need: that is what sets the doctor apart from the one who pulls up a flower for no reason.** : You make respect a rule of necessity, not of abstention: the principle does not paralyse, it obliges us to justify.
