# The true-self objection

*Objection to [Political liberty](../axes/political-liberty.md) : Positive liberty — attacks the position*

**If liberty is obedience to my "true" will, someone else can claim to know it better than I do, and coerce me in the name of my freedom.**

*[Berlin](../philosophers/berlin.md), *"Two Concepts of Liberty"*, 1958*

## The argument

1. [Positive liberty](../glossary/positive-liberty.md), in the versions the objection targets, distinguishes two wills in each of us: the one we express, and a "true" (rational, higher) will that ought to rule.
2. Once these two wills can diverge, someone must say where the true one lies: and nothing prevents a third party, party, state, or majority, from declaring itself a better judge of it than I am.
3. Coercing my expressed will then no longer counts as oppression but as liberation: [Rousseau](../philosophers/rousseau.md)'s formula, “he will be forced to be free”, coined for obedience to the [general will](../glossary/volonte-generale.md), stops being a paradox and becomes a program in other hands.
4. A definition of liberty that allows coercion to be described as liberation has lost what any conception of liberty must keep: that my actual will counts.

**⇒** Liberty as the rule of the "true self" opens the door to a tyranny exercised in freedom's name: any definition of this type bears the burden of showing what blocks that slope, and until it does, it cannot serve as a definition, whatever the worth of the ideal of autonomy.

## Ways out of the objection

- **Let us stick to the will I express: we are free when no one prevents us, even from making mistakes.** : [Berlin](../philosophers/berlin.md)'s choice: negative liberty is the only one that cannot be turned against the person it claims to free.
- **Keep self-government, but as a collective procedure under guarantees: no third party may invoke my true self, only non-arbitrary institutions.** : Republicanism as the middle path between Berlin's two liberties.
- **The abuse of an idea is not its refutation: autonomy demands first-person examination, precisely what no third party can confiscate.** : A Kant-inspired reply: for [Kant](../philosophers/kant.md), autonomy is a relation of self to self, not a blank check handed to the state.
