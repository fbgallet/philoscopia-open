# The plurality-of-ends argument

*Argument for [Political liberty](../axes/political-liberty.md) : Negative liberty*

**To be free is not to be prevented: any richer definition of liberty opens the door to coercion exercised in its name.**

*[Berlin](../philosophers/berlin.md), *"Two Concepts of Liberty"*, 1958*

## The argument

1. Human ends are **plural and often incompatible**: sainthood, creation, success, pleasure, or the service of others cannot be ranked or combined into a single good life.
2. No third party, whether state, majority, or sage, can claim superior knowledge of the end I ought to pursue: to presume otherwise is already [paternalism](../glossary/paternalisme.md).
3. Any "thicker" definition of liberty ([governing oneself by reason](../glossary/positive-liberty.md), realizing one's true self, living virtuously) builds in one particular conception of the good life: it thereby makes it possible to declare those who live otherwise "unfree", and to coerce them in the name of their own freedom, a slope history has slid down more than once.
4. The [negative](../glossary/negative-liberty.md) definition is the one that prejudges ends least: free is the person whom others do not prevent from acting on their own choices. It assumes only that this latitude matters, not any conception of the good life.

**⇒** To be free is to be unhindered by others: society must guarantee each person's perimeter, not prescribe the use they make of it.

## Deepenings

### "Experiments of living"

1. No one knows in advance which lives are worth living: [Mill](../philosophers/mill.md) defends the diversity of "experiments of living" as the only path of discovery, the way science needs a diversity of hypotheses.
2. To forbid a way of living that wrongs no one else is therefore to close down an experiment from which everyone, its opponents included, might have learned.

**⇒** The protected plurality of ways of living is a condition of everyone's progress.

*[Mill](../philosophers/mill.md), *[On Liberty](../works/on-liberty-mill.md)*, 1859*
