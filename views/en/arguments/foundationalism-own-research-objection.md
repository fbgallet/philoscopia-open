# Doing your own research

*Objection to [Justification of belief](../axes/epistemic-justification.md) : Foundationalism — attacks the position*

**Believing only what you have checked yourself sounds rigorous; it is impossible, and often the first step toward conspiracism.**

## The argument

1. Foundationalism wants all knowledge to trace back to basic beliefs, justified by experience or self-evidence. Carried into ordinary life, this ideal often takes a cruder form: believing only what one has observed oneself.
2. Yet almost everything we know we hold from others: that the Earth turns, that germs cause disease, even our own date of birth. No one can check everything: this is [epistemic dependence](../glossary/epistemic-dependence.md), the condition of all human knowledge.
3. Whoever "does their own research" does not escape it: they replace proven sources with ones they have picked, a video, a forum, a stranger who "dares to say it." Refusing to depend leads to depending worse.

**⇒** So the ideal of grounding everything on one's own evidence, carried into ordinary life, does not make free minds, but minds handed over to worse sources.

## Ways out of the objection

- **True: knowing is mostly knowing whom to trust.** : You join reliabilism: what counts is the reliability of sources, not personal checking.
- **I cannot check everything, but I can check how a claim was established, on what observations.** : You shift the demand: no longer checking everything, but knowing what a claim rests on.
- **The conspiracist checks nothing, he believes other people: it is not the demand for evidence that leads him astray, it is having given it up.** : You turn the objection around: "doing your research" is only a caricature of the demand for foundations.
