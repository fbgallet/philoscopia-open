# The criterion of hindsight

*Objection to [Place of science](../axes/science-place.md) : Critical rationalism — attacks the position*

**The criterion of falsifiability lets us say in hindsight who was right, rarely at the time.**

## The argument

1. Critical rationalism distinguishes science from what is not science: what exposes itself to refutation and corrects itself is scientific.
2. But for centuries, learned medicine practised bloodletting. In 1847, Ignaz Semmelweis showed that when doctors disinfected their hands with chlorine, deaths among women in childbirth fell; many of his peers ignored or disputed him. In 1949, prefrontal leucotomy, the forerunner of lobotomy, earned Egas Moniz the Nobel Prize.
3. Yet these theories were testable, and some had even been tested: at the time, the line between science and quackery was not where we see it today. So today's healer readily presents himself as a misunderstood Semmelweis: who can tell him, at the time, that he is not one?

**⇒** So falsifiability tells us which theories can be put to the test, not which have actually been tested, nor which are true: on its own, Popper's criterion is not enough to settle the quarrels of the present.

## Ways out of the objection

- **True: the line moves, and what is rejected today may be recognized tomorrow; we must stay open to other approaches.** : You lean toward pluralism: the frontier of knowledge moves too much to rule out other paths in advance.
- **Science was wrong, but it eventually corrected these errors, far more through evidence than through intuitions: that is what sets it apart.** : You see in the correction of errors the proof of science's superiority: you lean toward scientism.
- **Semmelweis won through figures, not through conviction: the healer who claims to be misunderstood can do the same, by submitting to the same tests.** : You hold Popper's line: being misunderstood proves nothing; submitting to the test proves something.
