# At the bedside

*Objection to [Place of science](../axes/science-place.md) : Epistemic pluralism — attacks the position*

**That all forms of knowledge are equal can be argued in a debate; at a patient's bedside, this relativism is paid for in lives.**

## The argument

1. Pluralism denies science a monopoly on knowledge: tradition, lived experience, other medicines would have their share of truth.
2. Yet in medicine these "other forms of knowledge" can be measured. An American study published in 2018 in the Journal of the National Cancer Institute compared patients with non-metastatic cancers: those who had chosen alternative medicine instead of treatment had a risk of death about two and a half times higher. It is an observational study, which cannot by itself prove the cause, but the gap is massive.
3. What is at issue is not the idea that there are other ways of understanding the world, but its slide: from "science doesn't know everything" to "my energy healer knows something else", then to "I don't need my chemotherapy."

**⇒** So pluralism must be able to say where it stops: where a claim concerns measurable facts, such as curing a cancer, no "other path" can escape measurement.

## Ways out of the objection

- **Fair enough: on what can be measured, it is the best available evidence, always revisable, that must decide; plurality holds for meaning, not for facts.** : You join critical rationalism: several paths for meaning, a single test for facts.
- **Complementary practices can have their place alongside treatment, if they are safe and do not interfere with it, never instead of it: they support the person, they do not treat the tumour.** : You keep a plurality of care, but you limit it: alongside, never instead.
- **This study measures what science knows how to measure; it says nothing about what these patients were looking for, or what medicine denied them: time, listening, meaning.** : You shift the question: if so many patients turn elsewhere, it is also because medicine leaves part of what they live through unanswered, fear, loneliness, the need to be heard. Still, one can seek that listening elsewhere without entrusting one's cure to it.
