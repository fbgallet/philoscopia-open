# The hard-problem argument

*Argument for [The enigma of consciousness](../axes/consciousness-nature.md) : Property dualism*

**Explaining every function of the brain leaves untouched the question why they are accompanied by experience: the sign that experience is one more fact.**

*[Chalmers](../philosophers/chalmers.md), *"Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness"*, 1995*

## The argument

1. To explain, in science, is to show by what mechanism a function is carried out: how the organism discriminates colours, memorizes, controls its behaviour, reports on its own states. These [easy problems](../glossary/hard-problem.md), as they are called by contrast, are hard, but we know what their solution would look like.
2. Suppose them all solved, down to the last circuit. One question remains, perfectly intelligible, and it is the [hard problem](../glossary/hard-problem.md): why is all that work accompanied by experience? One can describe a system that discriminates, memorizes and reports without yet having said what it is like for it.
3. When a question stays open although the explanation has said all it could say, its object was not what that explanation described: **structure** and **function** do not contain experience.
4. Yet experience varies strictly with the state of the brain, and no one dreams of denying it: that dependence is as solid a fact as the gap.

**⇒** So experience is a further fact about the world, irreducible yet tied to the brain by a law of nature: a [dualism](../glossary/dualism.md) without miracle, where nothing is added to matter but what it regularly produces.

## Deepenings

### A psychophysical law

*What separates this dualism from Descartes's: nothing violates physics, the link is a fundamental law to be discovered.*

1. The strict dependence of experience on the brain rules out [Descartes](../philosophers/descartes.md)'s separate soul: there are not two substances here, one of them pushing the other.
2. It does not, however, rule out that a fundamental **psychophysical law** links certain physical states to certain experienced states, as other laws link mass to gravitation. Such laws physics does not derive: it records them and posits them at the outset.
3. Experience would then be a fundamental ingredient of the world, on a par with charge or spacetime, to be built into the theory rather than derived from it.

**⇒** This dualism is naturalistic: it does not add a soul to the world, it adds a line to the list of nature's laws.

### The philosophical zombie

*If a double physically identical to us but feeling nothing is so much as possible, physical truth does not fix experience.*

1. Conceive a **zombie**, a being physically identical to you, atom for atom, speaking and acting exactly as you do, but with nothing being lived: no one home inside.
2. The description involves no contradiction: the vocabulary of physics speaks only of structures and motions, and one can follow that description through without ever meeting a feel.
3. If such a world is possible, then the complete physical truth does not suffice to fix the facts of experience: two physically identical worlds could differ in what is felt in them.

**⇒** Experience therefore does not follow from the physical: it is added to it, which is exactly what property dualism says.

*[Chalmers](../philosophers/chalmers.md), *The Conscious Mind, ch. 3*, 1996*
