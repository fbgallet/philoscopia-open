# Taking science on trust

*Objection to [Place of science](../axes/science-place.md) : Scientism — attacks the position*

**Scientism makes science the only legitimate authority; but for most of us, believing it means taking it on trust, and the line between a scientific mind and credulity then runs less between kinds of knowledge than between ways of believing.**

## The argument

1. Scientism makes science the measure of all knowledge: what it does not establish counts only as opinion.
2. Yet among those who "believe in science", who could say how we know that continents drift, or that a vaccine protects? Relying on experts can be perfectly justified; but when that trust becomes blind, we hold these truths as others hold their horoscope: because we received them from an authority we respect. A true belief received without reasons is not yet a scientific mind.
3. And when scientism claims that everything that matters falls under science, it abandons meaning, care and lived experience to those who claim to provide them: the peddlers of energy healing may well thrive on the ground it has deserted.

**⇒** So scientism does not protect against magical thinking: it reproduces its form, faith in an authority, and gives it free rein wherever it falls silent.

## Ways out of the objection

- **Fair enough: what matters is not believing science, but understanding how it knows, and what it does not claim to know.** : You join critical rationalism: science is valuable for its method, not for an authority one would grant it over everything.
- **Faith in science is not science; but science remains the knowledge that submits most systematically to the facts, even if many believe it without understanding it.** : You distinguish science from the way people believe it: the flaw lies with the believer, not with the knowledge.
- **Believing scientists is not believing an astrologer: their authority rests on a proven method that others check. Trusting with good reason is not credulity.** : You hold that trust in science is justified even without redoing its proofs: the difference between an authority that is checked and one that is merely claimed.
