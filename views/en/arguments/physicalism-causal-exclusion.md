# The argument from mental efficacy

*Argument for [The enigma of consciousness](../axes/consciousness-nature.md) : Physicalism*

**Our thoughts move our bodies; yet in the physical world only the physical produces effects: consciousness is therefore already there, in the working brain.**

*Kim, *Physicalism, or Something Near Enough*, 2005*

## The argument

1. Every known variation in mental life tracks a variation in the brain: anaesthesia, lesion, alcohol, fatigue, a child's development. We know of no case of lived experience without a cerebral substrate, nor of any sizeable cerebral change without an echo in what is lived.
2. Our mental states have physical effects: my pain makes me cry out, my decision raises my arm. To deny it is to turn mental life into an [epiphenomenon](../glossary/epiphenomenalism.md), a spectacle with no purchase whatever on our acts.
3. Yet the physical world is [causally closed](../glossary/causal-closure.md), that is, every physical effect already has a sufficient physical cause: the cry is explained entirely by a chain of nerves and muscles, with no remainder and no gap where something else might slip in.
4. If the cry already has a sufficient physical cause and my pain causes it too, only two exits remain: either it is caused twice over, by a systematic and inexplicable **overdetermination**, or my pain is nothing other than that cerebral state, which is what **identity theory** holds.

**⇒** So consciousness is not one more ingredient set beside the brain: it is the brain at work. How remains to be understood, which is a difficulty of research and not a mystery of principle.

## Deepenings

### What "causally closed" means

*Causal closure is not a metaphysical postulate: it is the finding that physiology has never met an effect without a physical cause.*

1. To say the physical is closed is not to say nothing else exists, but that if one follows a chain of physical effects backwards, one finds only physical causes: every muscle contraction has its nerve impulse, every impulse its chemical cascade.
2. This closure is no decision of principle: it is the outcome of two centuries of physiology, which were looking, on the contrary, for the soul's point of insertion and never found it, and of the **conservation laws**, which a force coming from elsewhere would break.
3. It remains possible to reject it, but at a precise price: one must then grant that physics is false somewhere inside the skull, and say where.

**⇒** Rejecting closure is not a harmless metaphysical position: it is an empirical prediction, so far disconfirmed.

### Water and H₂O

*Science has repeatedly found that two apparently distinct things are one, and each time the identity first seemed inconceivable.*

1. Lightning and electrical discharge, heat and molecular agitation, water and H₂O: in each case two things were held distinct because they were reached by two different routes, one familiar, the other theoretical.
2. Each time, the identity at first seemed inconceivable: nothing in the experience of heat resembles a motion of particles. The strangeness of the identification was never an argument against it.
3. Nothing therefore forbids that sensation and cerebral process should likewise be two approaches to a single thing, which the [materialist](../glossary/materialism.md) tradition takes to be the natural outcome of the inquiry: one from within, through experience, the other from without, through measurement.

**⇒** The identity of mental state and cerebral state would be a discovery like any other, not a denial of experience.
