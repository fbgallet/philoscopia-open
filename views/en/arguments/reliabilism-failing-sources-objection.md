# When reliable sources fail

*Objection to [Justification of belief](../axes/epistemic-justification.md) : Reliabilism — attacks the position*

**The best-established sources sometimes fail for a long time, through error, negligence, or because those with an interest in deceiving them do so: a source's reliability is never acquired once and for all.**

## The argument

1. Reliabilism judges a belief by the process that formed it; for everything we cannot check, that process is [testimony](../glossary/epistemic-dependence.md), and it is only as good as its sources: health agencies, scientific institutions, the established press.
2. But an institution is not a measuring instrument: it is made of people who can be wrong, give in to routine or pressure, or find themselves in a **conflict of interest**. In France, the drug Mediator stayed on prescription from 1976 to 2009 despite repeated warnings: its manufacturer concealed part of what it knew, for which it was convicted of deception in 2021, and the medicines agency was slow to act, until a lung specialist, Irène Frachon, documented the harm it did. The history of public health records other failures of this kind.
3. A source's past reliability therefore does not guarantee its present reliability. And whoever trusted it believed, in good faith, something false. No all-encompassing plot was needed for that: the deception of an interested party and failing oversight were enough, and it was other institutions, the courts, the press, a stubborn doctor, that brought it to light.

**⇒** So relying on reliable sources is not enough to justify a belief: that trust must stay vigilant, and distrust of authorities is not always unreasonable, even when it has nothing conspiratorial about it.

## Ways out of the objection

- **Fair enough: one must be able to judge the evidence oneself, not just the reputation of sources.** : You move back toward foundations: examining the evidence itself, rather than deferring to whoever holds it.
- **Reliability is judged over time and collectively: it was the whole system, self-correcting science, a whistleblower, the press, the courts, that finally corrected the error.** : You move reliability from the single source to the system that checks it: that system is what brought the affair to light.
- **A reliable source is rarely wrong, not never: an error, however serious, does not make it less trustworthy than the rumours that claim to replace it.** : You recall that reliability is a matter of frequency, not infallibility.
