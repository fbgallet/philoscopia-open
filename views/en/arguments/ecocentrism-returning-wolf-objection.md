# The return of the wolf

*Objection to [Moral circle](../axes/moral-circle.md) : Ecocentrism — attacks the position*

**"Preserve the ecosystem", but which one? Nature has no state of balance to defend: we are the ones who choose the one that suits us.**

## The argument

1. Ecocentrism judges an action by whether it preserves the integrity and stability of the [biotic community](../glossary/biotic-community.md).
2. Having come back from Italy in the early 1990s, the wolf has reconquered the French Alps, where it kills thousands of sheep every year. Some hail an ecosystem regaining its balance; others defend high pastures shaped by centuries of herding, themselves rich in flowers and insects. Where does "integrity" lie: in the wolfless mountain of the 1950s, the medieval one, the one before farming?
3. Nature never stops changing: ice ages, migrations, extinctions. It singles out no state as the right one, and even an ecocentrism attentive to processes rather than a fixed balance must choose which to favour. Choosing which to preserve means deciding by our own values, aesthetic, economic or emotional.

**⇒** So the "health" of an ecosystem is not a good that nature carries within it, but a human judgment: behind ecocentrism may lurk an anthropocentrism unaware of itself.

## Ways out of the objection

- **Fair enough: we are the ones who choose which nature to preserve, according to what it is worth to us.** : You grant that nature's value passes through our choices: a door toward anthropocentrism, or toward an ecology that owns up to being human.
- **There is no single balance, but there are criteria: diversity, a habitat's ability to sustain itself without us. Preserving the whole remains the goal, provided we own our choices.** : You keep ecocentrism while giving up the myth of a fixed natural balance: nature does not choose, but not all states are equal.
- **Several possible states do not prevent us from telling a living habitat from a collapsing one: an ecosystem losing species until it can no longer sustain itself is degrading, whatever we think of it.** : You hold that the degradation of a habitat is a fact, not a preference: the health of the whole is observed before it is chosen.
