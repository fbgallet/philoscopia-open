# The myth of the given

*Objection to [Justification of belief](../axes/epistemic-justification.md) : Foundationalism — attacks the reasoning*

**Even "I see a tree" presupposes concepts, learning, other beliefs: no belief justifies itself on its own.**

*[Sellars](../philosophers/sellars.md), *Empiricism and the Philosophy of Mind*, 1956*

## The argument

1. Foundationalism looks for basic beliefs, justified without the support of any other.
2. But to see "a tree" is already to recognize, classify, interpret: the simplest perception involves concepts and a whole body of acquired knowledge. [Wilfrid Sellars](../philosophers/sellars.md) called the idea of a raw experience that would supply its own justification "the myth of the given."
3. If we go further down, to pure sensation, we do find something given, but it asserts nothing, and what asserts nothing can justify nothing.

**⇒** So the foundations are caught in a web too: either they say something and depend on other beliefs, or they depend on nothing and say nothing.

## Ways out of the objection

- **Fair enough: no belief stands alone, each leans on the others.** : You move to coherentism: justification is a web, not a building on a base.
- **Basic beliefs are not infallible, but they keep a priority: they are revised only in the light of other perceptions.** : A modest foundationalism: revisable foundations, but foundations all the same.
- **When I am in pain, I need no theory to know it: some experiences justify themselves.** : You hold that there is immediate access to some states, which needs no concept to be certain.
