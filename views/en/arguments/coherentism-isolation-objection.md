# Coherent, but cut off from reality

*Objection to [Justification of belief](../axes/epistemic-justification.md) : Coherentism — attacks the position*

**A well-built novel is perfectly coherent: coherence alone guarantees no contact with the world.**

## The argument

1. For coherentism, a belief is justified by its fit with the whole.
2. But one can build infinitely many coherent wholes that are incompatible with one another: a novel, a mythology, a well-organized delusion. Each holds together perfectly, and they cannot all be true.
3. If coherence alone justified, nothing would let us choose between them: something outside the web must constrain it, experience, observation, the world itself.

**⇒** So coherence is a condition, not a guarantee: knowledge must also be anchored in what does not depend on it.

## Ways out of the objection

- **Fair enough: there must be a footing in experience, which coherence cannot replace.** : You come back to foundations: experience anchors the web, instead of being one belief among others.
- **Coherence must include all our observations: a whole that takes in the available experience is not a novel.** : A demanding coherentism: coherence counts if it takes in experience, not if it does without it.
- **We never reach the world outside our beliefs: an observation counts only once it is part of the web.** : You hold the coherentist line: there is no standpoint outside the web from which to compare it with the world.
