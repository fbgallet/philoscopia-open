# The systems reply

*Objection to [Can a machine think](../axes/machine-mind.md) : Not by program alone — attacks the reasoning*

**No neuron understands English, and yet you understand: to look for understanding in the component is to look where it never is.**

*[Dennett](../philosophers/dennett.md), *The Mind's I*, 1981*

## The argument

1. The argument concludes from no part's understanding that the whole does not understand. It is a step refused everywhere else: none of your cells reads these lines, none of your neurons speaks English.
2. Understanding is therefore never in a component, but in the whole **system**, in the organization that links them: to look for it in whoever runs the rules is to help oneself to the answer in advance.
3. There remain the **causal powers** of the living brain, invoked to mark the difference. But so long as one does not say which ones, nor why another material would lack them, this is no explanation: it is a name given to the gap one wishes to preserve.
4. And the criterion turns around: our brain seems to us to understand because we know it from within. Seen from outside, it is only an assembly of components not one of which understands anything.

**⇒** The middle position holds only by granting cerebral matter a privilege it does not define: either organization suffices, or one must name what is missing.

## Ways out of the objection

- **If one cannot name what is missing, nothing is missing: organization suffices.**
- **What cannot be named may be precisely life: a being that deals with the world on its own account.** : The objection pushes you past the middle: if the criterion must be named, [Jonas](../philosophers/jonas.md) names it, and it is the living.
- **That the brain produces thought is a fact to be explained, not an arbitrary privilege: biology will say which of its powers count.**
