# Nothing happens by chance

*Objection to [Justification of belief](../axes/epistemic-justification.md) : Coherentism — attacks the position*

**A conspiracy theory is a model of coherence: everything fits, and every denial becomes one more proof.**

## The argument

1. Coherentism judges a belief by its place in a whole that holds together.
2. Yet the conspiracy story is the most coherent whole there is: it links scattered facts, leaves nothing to chance and absorbs every objection. An official denial proves something is being hidden; the absence of evidence, that the cover-up has succeeded.
3. The real world, by contrast, is full of chance, coincidences and pieces that do not fit: a story that is too coherent should arouse suspicion instead of winning out.

**⇒** So coherence cannot be enough to justify a belief: pushed to its limit, it vindicates the theory that explains everything, that is, the one nothing could refute, an [unfalsifiable](../glossary/unfalsifiability.md) theory.

## Ways out of the objection

- **Coherence is not enough: there must be facts established independently, by proven methods.** : You look beyond coherence for a safeguard: established facts, or reliable methods.
- **Coherence counts if it also takes in the awkward facts: the conspiracy is coherent only by setting aside what bothers it.** : You distinguish true coherence, which takes everything in, from surface coherence, which picks and chooses.
- **The conspiracy theory is not coherent: it requires thousands of silent accomplices, which contradicts everything we know about people and institutions.** : You turn the objection around: judged against everything we know, the conspiracy is the least coherent story.
