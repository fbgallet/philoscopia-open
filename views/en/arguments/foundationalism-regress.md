# The regress of reasons

*Argument for [Justification of belief](../axes/epistemic-justification.md) : Foundationalism*

**If every belief had to rest on another, nothing would ever be justified: there must be basic beliefs, justified without resting on other beliefs.**

*[Descartes](../philosophers/descartes.md), [Sextus Empiricus](../philosophers/sextus-empiricus.md)*

## The argument

1. I believe A because of B, B because of C: every reason in turn calls for a reason.
2. There are only three ways out, **Agrippa's trilemma**: the chain is infinite, it goes round in a circle, or it stops somewhere. An infinite chain grounds nothing, and a circle presupposes itself.
3. So it must stop at beliefs justified without inference, by experience or by self-evidence: what I perceive here and now, or what is self-evident, like [Descartes](../philosophers/descartes.md)'s "I think, therefore I am."

**⇒** So knowledge has foundations: first evidences on which everything else is built, and without which nothing would stand.
