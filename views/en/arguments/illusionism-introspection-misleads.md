# The argument from misleading introspection

*Argument for [The enigma of consciousness](../axes/consciousness-nature.md) : Illusionism*

**Nothing guarantees that introspection tells the truth about itself; and once we have explained why experience seems ineffable to us, nothing is left to explain.**

*Frankish, *"Illusionism as a Theory of Consciousness"*, 2016*

## The argument

1. Everything we know of [qualia](../glossary/qualia.md), those private, immediate, ineffable properties, comes from a single source, **introspection**: the way the brain presents its own states to itself. No outside observation attests to them.
2. But that mechanism is a mechanism like any other, and we know it errs: the richness of the visual field is largely reconstructed, we **confabulate** after the fact the reasons for our choices, we fail to detect huge changes before our eyes. It is not a mirror, it is a summary cut out for acting fast.
3. A mechanism that simplifies and errs elsewhere may also err about what it is itself: nothing compels us to treat its verdicts as infallible data, except their force of conviction, which is precisely what needs explaining.
4. One question then remains, and this one is tractable: why does the brain represent its states as having ineffable properties? Answering it would account for everything there is to explain, our reports, our astonishment, even our debates, with no remainder.

**⇒** So qualia are not to be explained but dissolved: what exists is a brain representing itself as having ineffable experience; what needs explaining is that representation, not its supposed object.

## Deepenings

### What introspection misses

*A reconstructed visual field, motives invented after the fact, huge changes gone unseen: the witness is established as unreliable.*

1. We believe we see a scene sharp and coloured from one edge of our gaze to the other; in fact precision and colour hold only at the centre, and the rest is filled in. The impression of richness matches nothing in the detail of what is processed.
2. Asked to explain a choice, we produce a reason without hesitation, including where the experimenter knows it is not the one that operated. The report's sincerity is no guarantee of its truth.
3. If introspection is wrong about what we see and about what makes us act, why should it be infallible about the very nature of what it reports?

**⇒** Privileged access to oneself is not transparent access: it is a report, produced by a device that can be studied and caught out.

### The meta-problem

*Even a dualist must physically explain why we say consciousness is mysterious: the question is whether that explanation is the whole story.*

1. Our talk about consciousness consists of behaviours: it has physical causes, so there must be a physical explanation of what we say about the [hard problem](../glossary/hard-problem.md). This is what [Chalmers](../philosophers/chalmers.md) called the **meta-problem**, and most dualists grant it, even if they reserve a role for experience in our judgments.
2. That explanation, once found, will say why it seems to us that there is ineffable experience, why it strikes us as indubitable, and why we judge that nothing physical could account for it.
3. Two attitudes then become possible: to add to the world an experience that this explanation has no need of, or to acknowledge that it exhausts the phenomenon. The second does not say that nothing is lived: it says that what is lived lacks the properties we ascribe to it.

**⇒** Once the meta-problem is solved, adding ineffable experience explains nothing further: parsimony leans toward the illusion.

*[Chalmers](../philosophers/chalmers.md), *"The Meta-Problem of Consciousness"*, 2018*
