# Elisabeth of Bohemia's objection

*Objection to [The enigma of consciousness](../axes/consciousness-nature.md) : Substance dualism — attacks the position*

**If the mind has neither extension nor surface, one cannot see by what end it could push a body, nor how a body could reach it.**

*[Elisabeth of Bohemia](../philosophers/elisabeth-of-bohemia.md), *Correspondence with Descartes, letter of 6 May 1643*, 1643*

## The argument

1. One thing moves another only by touching it, or by acting on it through a force that has a direction and a point of application: this is how we understand any action, from colliding balls to the signal running along a nerve.
2. But an **unextended** mind is nowhere, has neither surface nor force: it meets none of the conditions by which we understand one thing to modify another.
3. One would then have to admit an action of an entirely unprecedented kind, of which nothing can be said except that it occurs. That is not explaining the **union of soul and body**, it is giving it a name.
4. And the puzzle is twofold, for the mind would also have to be reached by the body: a blow, a fever, a glass of wine immediately change what I think and what I feel, as if matter had purchase on what is not matter.

**⇒** Dualism saves the mind's irreducibility at the price of its commerce with the body: the purer the soul, the less one understands how it could raise an arm.

## Ways out of the objection

- **A soul that would act only by miracle explains less than the brain, whose action can be followed step by step.** : You follow the path leading from [Descartes](../philosophers/descartes.md) to the brain sciences: what the soul was to do is seen being done in tissue.
- **Let us keep experience irreducible, but inside the physical world: a property of the brain, not a separate substance.** : The objection loses its grip on [property dualism](../axes/consciousness-nature.md): nothing there crosses a border, since there is only one substance.
- **That we do not understand how does not prove it does not happen: our model of impact is not the measure of the possible.** : This is [Descartes](../philosophers/descartes.md)'s reply to Elisabeth: the union of soul and body is not grasped by the understanding, but undergone at every moment.
