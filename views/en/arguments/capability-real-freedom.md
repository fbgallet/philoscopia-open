# The capabilities argument

*Argument for [Political liberty](../axes/political-liberty.md) : Capability freedom*

**A liberty one lacks the means to use exists only on paper: to be free is to be actually able to be and to do.**

*[Sen](../philosophers/sen.md), [Nussbaum](../philosophers/nussbaum.md), *Development as Freedom*, 1999*

## The argument

1. With equal rights, two people do not have the same real possibilities: the right to start a business, to study, or to travel opens nothing to those who lack money, health, or education.
2. What we prize in liberty are the lives it makes possible: a right its holder cannot take up may keep its value in principle, but by itself it opens no life.
3. A purely formal definition must nonetheless declare these two people equally free: as a measure of freedom, it is blind to what gives freedom its value.
4. The relevant measure is therefore the range of [capabilities](../glossary/capabilite.md): not resources themselves, but what each person, with them and given their situation, can actually choose to be and to do.

**⇒** A free society is one that widens what its members can really be and do, health, education, and resources included.

## Deepenings

### The critique of formal freedoms

1. Legal equality can coexist with actual dependence: proclaimed for all, freedoms remain, for those without the means to exercise them, **paper freedoms**.
2. The capability approach does not take over this political diagnosis, but it keeps the warning: a society can multiply rights without widening by an inch what its poorest members can actually do.
