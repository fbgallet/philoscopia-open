# The unwitting clairvoyant

*Objection to [Justification of belief](../axes/epistemic-justification.md) : Reliabilism — attacks the reasoning*

**A man has, without knowing it, a perfectly reliable gift of clairvoyance; one day, for no reason at all, he believes the president is in New York, and he is right. Is his belief justified?**

*[BonJour](../philosophers/bonjour.md), 1980*

## The argument

1. For reliabilism, it is enough that a belief comes from a reliable process, even if the one who forms it does not know so.
2. The example is [Laurence BonJour](../philosophers/bonjour.md)'s: his clairvoyant does not know he has the gift and has no reason, for or against, to trust it; yet he believes, and the process is reliable.
3. We judge that he believes lightly: the reliability of a mechanism he cannot assess does not make his belief reasonable.

**⇒** So justification cannot be reduced to a mechanism: the believer must also have access to reasons, or at least not believe against them.

## Ways out of the objection

- **Fair enough: being justified means being able to account for one's reasons.** : You join internalism, shared by foundationalists and coherentists: justification goes through accessible reasons.
- **Reliability matters, but it must be known: trusting a source means having reasons to believe it reliable.** : A reasoned reliabilism: reliability makes justification, provided one has some sign of it.
- **If his gift is really reliable, he really knows: our unease comes from the fact that no real gift is like that, not from reliability being insufficient.** : You hold reliabilism to the end: the contrary intuition comes from our distrust of clairvoyance, not from a flaw in the theory.
