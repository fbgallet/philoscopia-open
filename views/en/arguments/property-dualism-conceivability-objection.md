# The conceivability objection

*Objection to [The enigma of consciousness](../axes/consciousness-nature.md) : Property dualism — attacks the reasoning*

**That we can imagine a world does not entail that it is possible: our concepts are not the measure of the real.**

*Loar, *"Phenomenal States"*, 1990*

## The argument

1. The argument moves from "I conceive a double without experience" to "such a double is possible", then to "experience is not physical". Everything turns on the first move.
2. Yet one could **conceive**, without contradiction, water that was not H₂O: it was nonetheless impossible, water being H₂O wherever there is water. What we conceive depends on what we know, not on what is.
3. If experience is a cerebral state, conceiving a brain without experience amounts to conceiving a thing without itself: the description's apparent coherence comes from our having two distinct **concepts** of it, grasping that state in two ways, from outside by measurement and from inside by living it, without yet knowing it is the same.
4. The argument therefore assumes, without showing it, that whatever is imagined without contradiction can exist: its conclusion is already lodged in its premise.

**⇒** The zombie proves nothing until that step is justified: the gap may lie only in our concepts, between two ways of grasping one and the same state.

## Ways out of the objection

- **Two concepts for one state: the gap is in our heads, and experience stays cerebral through and through.**
- **The gap may be conceptual yet unbridgeable: we shall never be in a position to see the identity, lacking the point of view it would take.** : This is the cognitive-closure thesis defended by Colin McGinn: the problem would have a solution our species is not equipped to conceive.
- **Water can be redescribed by chemistry; experience, by contrast, does not stop being what it is when known otherwise: there is nothing hidden left to discover.**
