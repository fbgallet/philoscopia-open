# The idle-experience objection

*Objection to [The enigma of consciousness](../axes/consciousness-nature.md) : Property dualism — attacks the position*

**If experience is a further fact that changes nothing in the course of things, it is not what makes you speak of it, and your very conviction becomes inexplicable.**

*[Dennett](../philosophers/dennett.md), *Consciousness Explained*, 1991*

## The argument

1. This position grants the [causal closure](../glossary/causal-closure.md) of the physical world, self-sufficient as it is: no neuron waits on experience to fire, and that is what allows it to call itself naturalistic.
2. It follows that experience is a mere [epiphenomenon](../glossary/epiphenomenalism.md), doing nothing: my cries, my gestures, and even my words about my pain are explained without it, by the nervous machinery alone.
3. Yet I do speak of my experience, I marvel at it, I write books about the [hard problem](../glossary/hard-problem.md). Those behaviours therefore have, by the position's own lights, entirely physical causes.
4. My conviction that there is experience therefore does not come from experience: it would be exactly the same were there none, since the zombie, physically identical, also declares it has a feel and marvels at it.

**⇒** Either experience acts, and the closure of the physical world must be given up; or it does not, and the testimony on which the whole position rests is cut off from what it testifies to.

## Ways out of the objection

- **My testimony is worth something only if experience causes it: so it is the cerebral state itself.**
- **For experience to act while violating nothing, it must be in matter itself, not grafted onto it.** : This is the argument [Strawson](../philosophers/strawson.md) turns against dualism: lodging experience in matter's intrinsic nature restores its efficacy.
- **If our conviction would be explained just as well without experience, perhaps it is the conviction that needs explaining, not experience.** : You tip toward [illusionism](../axes/consciousness-nature.md): explaining why experience seems ineffable to us, rather than the ineffable itself.
- **That my belief has physical causes does not make it false: I know my pain by living it, not by inferring it from my cries.**
