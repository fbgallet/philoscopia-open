# The thermometer

*Argument for [Justification of belief](../axes/epistemic-justification.md) : Reliabilism*

**A child who recognizes her father, a doctor who makes a diagnosis without being able to explain it: their belief is justified because it comes from a process that rarely errs.**

*[Goldman](../philosophers/goldman.md), [Armstrong](../philosophers/armstrong.md), 1979*

## The argument

1. If being justified required being able to give one's reasons, the child, the animal and we ourselves, most of the time, would know nothing: who can prove they correctly remember their own first name?
2. What distinguishes a well-founded belief from a lucky one is how it was produced: perception in good light, a sound memory, valid reasoning, a source with a track record.
3. Just as a thermometer shows the right temperature without knowing any physics (the image is [David Armstrong](../philosophers/armstrong.md)'s), a well-tuned mind forms true beliefs without always being able to say why.

**⇒** So a belief is justified when it comes from a **reliable process**, whether or not one can prove it. This is also what grounds our trust in instruments, experts and tested testimony.
