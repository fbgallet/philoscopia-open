# Madhyamaka

*c. 150 – c. 1419, living tradition*

Madhyamaka (the [middle way](../glossary/the-middle-way.md)) is the school of Mahāyāna Buddhism founded by [Nāgārjuna](../philosophers/nagarjuna.md) around the second century, built on his Fundamental Verses on the Middle Way. It radicalizes the Buddhist teaching of [dependent origination](../glossary/pratityasamutpada.md) into a doctrine of [emptiness](../glossary/sunyata.md). Nāgārjuna defends it mainly by reducing opposing positions to absurdity; Bhāviveka reintroduced autonomous arguments, which [Candrakīrti](../philosophers/candrakirti.md) contested, hence the distinction, worked out in Tibet, between "Svātantrika" and "Prāsaṅgika" readings. With [Tsongkhapa](../philosophers/tsongkhapa.md), the last great renewer recorded here, it became the summit of the monastic curriculum of the Gelug school, which it remains today. Its controversy with [Nyāya](../movements/nyaya.md) over the [means of knowledge](../glossary/pramana.md) shaped Indian epistemology. In the twentieth century it was received in diverse ways, in the [Kyoto School](../movements/kyoto-school.md)'s dialogue with Western philosophy as well as in part of analytic philosophy.

## Core positions

1. [Essence and existence](../axes/essence.md) : **Nothing exists by itself: to be is to depend, and it is because everything is [empty](../glossary/sunyata.md) that everything can change.**
2. [Nature of truth](../axes/truth-nature.md) : **There are [two truths](../glossary/two-truths.md), and the higher one cannot be stated as a fact.**
3. [Where the sacred resides](../axes/transcendence.md) : **[Nirvāṇa](../glossary/nirvana.md) is not an elsewhere: ultimately it does not differ from [saṃsāra](../glossary/samsara.md), of which it is the stilling.**

## Positions

### [Essence and existence](../axes/essence.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Existentialism 80% · Essentialism 20%

Nothing has an [intrinsic nature](../glossary/svabhava.md) ([svabhāva](../glossary/svabhava.md)), that is, an existence depending on nothing else. A thing endowed with such a nature could neither arise, nor change, nor act; yet everything arises, changes and acts. To say that things are [empty](../glossary/sunyata.md) is not to say that they do not exist, but that they exist only in dependence. This emptiness is not nothingness, and [Nāgārjuna](../philosophers/nagarjuna.md) warns against anyone who would grasp it as a new thesis.

*Sources : Nāgārjuna, Fundamental Verses on the Middle Way, XV and XXIV.18*

### [Nature of truth](../axes/truth-nature.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Pragmatism 50% · Coherence 40% · Correspondence 10%

The doctrine of the [two truths](../glossary/two-truths.md) distinguishes a [conventional truth](../glossary/two-truths.md), that of usage, language and worldly transactions, from an [ultimate truth](../glossary/two-truths.md), emptiness, which cannot be stated as a fact. Without conventional truth the ultimate cannot be taught. Conventional truth rests on worldly conventions and on conventionally reliable cognition, which [Candrakīrti](../philosophers/candrakirti.md) accepts at that level; it does not presuppose things endowed with an intrinsic nature to correspond to, hence the small share of correspondence and the weight of coherence with practices and of their effectiveness.

*Sources : Nāgārjuna, Fundamental Verses on the Middle Way, XXIV.8-10 · Candrakīrti, Madhyamakāvatāra (Introduction to the Middle Way), VI*

### [Where the sacred resides](../axes/transcendence.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Immanence 98% · Transcendence 3%

For [Nāgārjuna](../philosophers/nagarjuna.md), there is ultimately not the slightest difference between [saṃsāra](../glossary/samsara.md) and [nirvāṇa](../glossary/nirvana.md): both are empty of intrinsic nature. Nirvāṇa is therefore not another place to escape to, but the stilling of the grasping that makes saṃsāra. The distinction nonetheless remains at the conventional level, where the path and liberation keep their full meaning. This radical immanence would be taken up, in diverse ways, by Zen and by the [Kyoto School](../movements/kyoto-school.md).

*Sources : Nāgārjuna, Fundamental Verses on the Middle Way, XXV.19-20*

### [Nature of the self](../axes/self-nature.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Bundle theory 100%

The self is neither identical to the [aggregates](../glossary/skandha.md) ([skandha](../glossary/skandha.md)) that make it up nor distinct from them: it is a conventional designation, just as the chariot is nothing apart from its parts and yet is none of them. Madhyamaka further denies the aggregates themselves any ultimate existence, which sets it apart from the Buddhist schools that took them as the real constituents: the bundle itself is only a designation.

*Sources : Nāgārjuna, Fundamental Verses on the Middle Way, XVIII · Candrakīrti, Madhyamakāvatāra, VI.150-165 (the sevenfold analysis of the chariot)*

### [Relation to certainty](../axes/certainty.md)

*contextualist*

Neither dogmatic nor sceptical in the Greek sense: [Nāgārjuna](../philosophers/nagarjuna.md) declares he has no thesis to defend and turns the demand for grounding back on the [means of knowledge](../glossary/pramana.md), while [Candrakīrti](../philosophers/candrakirti.md) accepts them at the conventional level. The axis does not capture this position.

### [The point of philosophy](../axes/metaphilosophy.md)

*positioned · inferable · major*

Transforming oneself 80% · Clarifying thought 20%

The Madhyamaka dialectic aims at the [pacification of conceptual proliferation](../glossary/prapancopasama.md) ([prapañcopaśama](../glossary/prapancopasama.md)): undoing attachment to views, a source of suffering, rather than building a doctrine of the world. Analysis is a therapy here, hence the share of clarification.

*Sources : Nāgārjuna, Fundamental Verses on the Middle Way, dedication and XXVII.30*

## Influenced by

- **[Nyāya](../movements/nyaya.md)** (reacts against) : [Nāgārjuna](../philosophers/nagarjuna.md) turns the demand for grounding back on [Nyāya](../movements/nyaya.md): if the means of knowledge validate everything, what validates them? He also attacks the list of debate categories Nyāya had codified.

## Influence on

- **[Kyoto School](../movements/kyoto-school.md)** (reinterprets) : [Nishitani](../philosophers/nishitani.md) makes the [emptiness](../glossary/sunyata.md) of [Madhyamaka](../movements/madhyamaka.md) the "field" where Western nihilism, as diagnosed by Nietzsche and Heidegger, is passed through and overcome: a Buddhist notion becomes the answer to a crisis of European modernity.
- **[Nyāya](../movements/nyaya.md)** (reacts against) : [Nyāya](../movements/nyaya.md) defends the means of knowledge against the [Madhyamaka](../movements/madhyamaka.md) objection: they establish themselves, as a lamp lights itself in lighting up objects, and denying all valid knowledge undermines the denial itself.
