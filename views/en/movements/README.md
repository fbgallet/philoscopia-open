# Movements

- [Analytic Philosophy](analytic-philosophy.md) (1900 – …) : Treating philosophical problems as knots to [untangle](../axes/metaphilosophy.md): what exactly are we saying, and what actually follows from our words and our reasons?
- [Aristotelianism](aristotelianism.md) (335 BCE – 1617, living tradition) : Putting [Aristotle](../philosophers/aristotle.md) to work on new questions, from the Arabic commentators to today's virtue ethics: understanding each being through its ends, and a [flourishing life](../glossary/eudaimonism.md) through the virtues.
- [Buddhism](buddhism.md) (c. 450 BCE – c. 1419, living tradition) : Making [suffering](../glossary/dukkha.md) a practical inquiry: seeking out its causes so as to undo them, through ethics, attention and meditation.
- [Chan / Zen](chan-zen.md) (c. 520 – c. 1769, living tradition) : Seeking awakening in practice rather than in books: seated meditation, ordinary gestures, a master's disconcerting questions.
- [Confucianism](confucianism.md) (c. 500 BCE – c. 104 BCE, living tradition) : Cultivating oneself to set the world in order: from family to state, well-ordered relationships hold society together, and authority is worth only as much as its moral example.
- [Cynicism](cynicism.md) (c. 400 BCE – c. 400) : Taking natural needs as the only measure, and defying hierarchies of wealth, birth and city through endurance and [frank speech](../glossary/parrhesia.md).
- [Empiricism](empiricism.md) (1620 – 1776, living tradition) : Tracing our ideas back to the experience they come from, and measuring by it alone what we can claim to know about the world.
- [Environmental Ethics](environmental-ethics.md) (1949 – …) : Moving the moral question [beyond the human](../axes/moral-circle.md): what do we owe animals, the living world and future generations, and [what place should we hold in nature](../axes/human-place-nature.md)?
- [Epicureanism](epicureanism.md) (307 BCE – 200, living tradition) : Philosophizing among friends, in the Garden, open to women and slaves: learning together to set limits to one's desires, so that happiness depends neither on wealth nor on power.
- [Existentialism](existentialism.md) (1843 – 1980, living tradition) : Starting from concrete existence, always in a situation, never finished: a finite [freedom](../axes/freedom.md) that makes itself through its choices and must answer for them.
- [Falsafa](falsafa.md) (c. 830 – c. 1198, living tradition) : Receiving the Greek legacy in Arabic, from [Aristotle](../philosophers/aristotle.md) to the Neoplatonists, and making it a philosophy of its own, probing by demonstration nature, the intellect and the meaning of prophecy.
- [Feminist philosophy](feminist-philosophy.md) (1792 – …) : Asking what philosophy's concepts owe to the exclusion of women, and what becomes of them once that exclusion is undone.
- [Frankfurt School](frankfurt-school.md) (1923 – …) : Thinking existing society with a view to emancipating those it dominates, beginning with distrust of reason reduced to an instrument.
- [German Idealism](german-idealism.md) (1781 – 1831) : Carrying on the work [Kant](../philosophers/kant.md) began, beyond the limits he drew: no longer opposing mind and world, but thinking freedom, nature and history together.
- [Kalām](kalam.md) (c. 750 – c. 1210, living tradition) : Making faith a matter of rational controversy: God's justice, human freedom, even the necessity of natural causes, debated argument against argument.
- [Kyoto School](kyoto-school.md) (1911 – 2019, living tradition) : Thinking Buddhist experience with the tools of European philosophy: seeking the ultimate not in a supreme being but in an [absolute nothingness](../glossary/absolute-nothingness.md) where everything finds its place.
- [Lebensphilosophie](life-philosophy.md) (c. 1883 – c. 1932) : Recapturing life in its very movement, which concepts freeze and science cuts up.
- [Liberalism](liberalism.md) (1689 – …) : How can we live freely together without agreeing on the good? Limiting all power by rights, and holding it legitimate only if it rests on consent.
- [Logical positivism](logical-positivism.md) (c. 1924 – c. 1960) : Scientists and logicians in Vienna and Berlin sorting out what is said: only what belongs to logic or can be confronted with experience has meaning.
- [Madhyamaka](madhyamaka.md) (c. 150 – c. 1419, living tradition) : A dialectic that undoes every thesis, its own included, to free the mind from the grip of views.
- [Marxism](marxism.md) (1848 – …) : Understanding capitalism in order to break free of it: linking the production of wealth to the class conflicts running through society.
- [Mohism](mohism.md) (c. 430 BCE – c. 221 BCE) : Judging every custom by what it yields for all, and daring to say that lavish funerals, court music and wars of conquest cost too much.
- [Neo-Confucianism](neo-confucianism.md) (1017 – 1584, living tradition) : Answering Buddhism on its own ground: giving Confucianism a metaphysics without leaving the world.
- [Neoplatonism](neoplatonism.md) (c. 244 – c. 529) : Reading the whole of [Plato](../philosophers/plato.md) as a single system: all things proceed from one principle, and study, ascesis and at times ritual lead the soul back to it.
- [Nyāya](nyaya.md) (c. 150 – c. 1650, living tradition) : A philosophy that first wants to know how we know: logic and debate raised into a path to liberation.
- [Ordinary language philosophy](ordinary-language-philosophy.md) (c. 1945 – c. 1970) : Listening to what we actually say, in which circumstances and why, before theorizing: the subtlety of common usage taken as a philosophical instrument.
- [Phenomenology](phenomenology.md) (1900 – …) : Returning "to the things themselves": describing how the world takes on meaning in lived experience, perception, the body, time, the encounter with others.
- [Philosophical hermeneutics](hermeneutics.md) (1960 – …) : Understanding is not one method among others but our way of existing: we make a meaning our own only from what we inherit.
- [Platonism](platonism.md) (387 BCE – 529, living tradition) : What changes does not exhaust the real: seeking, beyond the sensible, [stable realities](../glossary/theory-of-forms.md) that thought alone can grasp and that make the world intelligible.
- [Positivism](positivism.md) (c. 1830 – c. 1881) : Abandoning the search for first and final causes to seek only the laws of phenomena, and making that sobriety the principle of a reorganization of society.
- [Pragmatism](pragmatism.md) (1870 – …) : Testing ideas against experience and action: to think is to inquire in order to solve real problems, and every belief remains open to revision.
- [Rationalism](rationalism.md) (1637 – 1716, living tradition) : Experience informs, but cannot ground necessity: seeking in [reason](../axes/knowledge-source.md) the principles the senses alone cannot guarantee.
- [Renaissance humanism](renaissance-humanism.md) (c. 1341 – c. 1592) : Recovering, editing and translating ancient texts to learn anew how to live well, speak well and judge well: an education that shapes the whole person, and the citizen.
- [Romanticism](romanticism.md) (1795 – 1850) : Defending what calculation cannot exhaust: inner experience, creative imagination and a living nature, against a world reduced to mechanism.
- [Scholasticism](scholasticism.md) (c. 1077 – c. 1617, living tradition) : Making reason a shared method: posing each question, hearing the objections, then deciding by drawing distinctions, in theology as in logic or physics.
- [Skepticism](skepticism.md) (365 BCE – 210, living tradition) : Testing everything that claims to be knowledge, and suspending judgment when reasons do not decide: a doubt that frees rather than weighs down.
- [Stoicism](stoicism.md) (300 BCE – 180, living tradition) : Bringing one's life into accord with the rational order of the world: we do not choose events, only how we respond to them, and it is there, in virtue, that our whole good is at stake.
- [Structuralism](structuralism.md) (c. 1949 – c. 1971) : Explaining language, kinship or myths by the relations between their elements rather than by the intentions of speakers: rules that organize us without our knowing them.
- [Taoism](taoism.md) (c. 500 BCE – c. 312, living tradition) : What if we acted better by ceasing to force? Attuning to the [dao 道 (the Way)](../glossary/dao.md), through thought, meditation and care of the body, rather than imposing our will on the course of things.
- [The Enlightenment](enlightenment.md) (1685 – 1804) : Dare to use your own understanding: through reason and [enlightenment](../glossary/lumieres.md), humanity can free itself from the tutelage of authority, from superstition and from arbitrary power.
- [Transhumanism](transhumanism.md) (1990 – …) : Extending and enhancing human capacities through technology, to the point of going beyond their limits: aging, disease and death as problems to be solved.
- [Utilitarianism](utilitarianism.md) (1789 – …) : Can the good be measured? Judging acts and institutions by their consequences for everyone's well-being, privileging no one.
- [Vedānta (Advaita)](vedanta.md) (c. 150 – c. 1640, living tradition) : Joining the hearing of scripture, reflection and meditation to test whether the separation between the [Self (ātman)](../glossary/atman.md) and the [absolute (brahman)](../glossary/brahman.md) is not an error of knowledge.
- [Yogācāra](yogacara.md) (c. 350 – c. 700, living tradition) : Making consciousness the seat of illusion and of liberation alike: what we take for independent things is shaped by our mental habits.
