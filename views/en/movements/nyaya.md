# Nyāya

*c. 150 – c. 1650, living tradition*

Nyāya ("rule", "reasoning") is one of the six orthodox systems of Indian philosophy, founded on the Nyāya-sūtra attributed to [Akṣapāda Gautama](../philosophers/akshapada-gautama.md) (around the second century). A school of logic and theory of knowledge, it lists the [means of valid knowledge](../glossary/pramana.md) ([pramāṇa](../glossary/pramana.md)), codifies inference and the rules of debate, and defends a robust realism against Buddhists and sceptics. Its great commentators, from Vātsyāyana to [Udayana](../philosophers/udayana.md), engaged for a thousand years in a sustained, closely argued controversy with [Madhyamaka](../movements/madhyamaka.md) and Buddhist epistemology, and Nyāya eventually merged with its sister school, Vaiśeṣika. In the fourteenth century [Gaṅgeśa](../philosophers/gangesa.md) founded "new Nyāya" (Navya-Nyāya), whose extremely precise technical language became the common idiom of Indian scholasticism; the period recorded here runs to that school's last great masters in the seventeenth century. Nyāya draws the interest of analytic philosophy today for its logic and epistemology.

## Core positions

1. [Relation to certainty](../axes/certainty.md) : **We know, even if we do not always know that we know: the validity of a cognition is recognized by its fruits.**
2. [Nature of the self](../axes/self-nature.md) : **The self is not a flux of states: without an abiding subject, one could neither remember nor desire.**
3. [Universals](../axes/universals.md) : **Universals are seen: to perceive a cow is also to perceive what makes it a cow.**

## Positions

### [Relation to certainty](../axes/certainty.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Dogmatism 65% · Skepticism 35%

Against [Nāgārjuna](../philosophers/nagarjuna.md), who asks what validates the [means of knowledge](../glossary/pramana.md) themselves, Nyāya replies that a means of knowledge need not be validated in advance in order to work: just as a lamp shows objects without requiring another lamp to be seen, it first produces a cognition, and its reliability can then be checked by another means, without infinite regress. Nyāya thus distinguishes the production of a cognition, its validity, which consists in grasping the object as it is, and the recognition of that validity, which is not self-evident ([extrinsic validity](../glossary/paratah-pramanya.md), [parataḥ prāmāṇya](../glossary/paratah-pramanya.md)): it is established after the fact, notably by the success of the action it guides. Hence an anti-sceptical realism that nonetheless allows for error and correction.

*Sources : Nyāya-sūtra, II.1.8-20, with Vātsyāyana's commentary · Gaṅgeśa, Tattvacintāmaṇi, book I (section on validity)*

### [Nature of the self](../axes/self-nature.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Substantialism 100%

Against the Buddhist doctrine of no-self, Nyāya holds that the [self](../glossary/atman.md) ([ātman](../glossary/atman.md)) is a permanent substance, distinct from the body and from mental states. The main argument is from recognition: whoever desires what they saw yesterday must be the same subject who saw it, otherwise memory and desire would be inexplicable. [Udayana](../philosophers/udayana.md) devoted a whole treatise to refuting the Buddhists on this point.

*Sources : Nyāya-sūtra, I.1.10 and III.1 · Udayana, Ātmatattvaviveka (Discernment of the Reality of the Self)*

### [Universals](../axes/universals.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Realism about universals 95% · Nominalism 5%

When we see a cow, we also perceive "cowness" (gotva), a real, eternal [universal](../glossary/realism-of-universals.md) present in each of its individuals. Nyāya thereby opposes the Buddhist theory of [exclusion](../glossary/apoha.md) ([apoha](../glossary/apoha.md)), on which the word "cow" designates only what is not non-cow: for Nyāya, universals are perceived, not constructed.

*Sources : Nyāya-sūtra, II.2.58-69 · Uddyotakara, Nyāyavārttika*

### [Source of knowledge](../axes/knowledge-source.md)

*positioned · inferable · major*

Empiricism 75% · Rationalism 25%

Perception is the primary means of knowledge, on which the others depend. Inference rests on [invariable concomitance](../glossary/vyapti.md) ([vyāpti](../glossary/vyapti.md): smoke always accompanied by fire). Nyāya does not derive it from a mere enumeration of cases: it requires observing joint presence and joint absence, ruling out the [limiting conditions](../glossary/vyapti.md) ([upādhi](../glossary/vyapti.md)) that could vitiate the link, and the help of hypothetical reasoning ([tarka](../glossary/vyapti.md)). It is a knowledge that starts from experience, not from innate truths.

*Sources : Nyāya-sūtra, I.1.3-5 · Gaṅgeśa, Tattvacintāmaṇi, book II (inference)*

### [Nature of truth](../axes/truth-nature.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Correspondence 75% · Pragmatism 25%

A cognition is true when it grasps a thing as it is: it ascribes to the object a property the object really has. Correspondence defines truth; the success of action serves only to recognize it, hence the minor share of pragmatism.

*Sources : Gaṅgeśa, Tattvacintāmaṇi, book I (section on validity)*

### [Existence of God](../axes/god-existence.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · minor*

Theism 90% · Atheism 10%

Muted in the Nyāya-sūtra, theism became a developed doctrine of later Nyāya and of the Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika tradition, which made it the theistic school par excellence of Indian philosophy: [Udayana](../philosophers/udayana.md) seeks to establish the existence of a Lord (Īśvara), the intelligent maker of the world, against the Mīmāṃsakas and Buddhists who deny it. The central argument is from effects: the earth, being a product, presupposes an agent who produces it. Hence a clearly theistic value, though short of the extreme.

*Sources : Nyāya-sūtra, IV.1.19-21 · Udayana, Nyāyakusumāñjali, V.1*

## Influenced by

- **[Buddhism](../movements/buddhism.md)** (reacts against) : For a thousand years [Nyāya](../movements/nyaya.md) engaged in a sustained controversy with the logicians of [Buddhism](../movements/buddhism.md), Dignāga and Dharmakīrti: against no-self it defends a substantial self; against the theory of [exclusion](../glossary/apoha.md) ([apoha](../glossary/apoha.md)), real universals; against the momentariness of things, their duration.
- **[Madhyamaka](../movements/madhyamaka.md)** (reacts against) : [Nyāya](../movements/nyaya.md) defends the means of knowledge against the [Madhyamaka](../movements/madhyamaka.md) objection: they establish themselves, as a lamp lights itself in lighting up objects, and denying all valid knowledge undermines the denial itself.

## Influence on

- **[Madhyamaka](../movements/madhyamaka.md)** (reacts against) : [Nāgārjuna](../philosophers/nagarjuna.md) turns the demand for grounding back on [Nyāya](../movements/nyaya.md): if the means of knowledge validate everything, what validates them? He also attacks the list of debate categories Nyāya had codified.
