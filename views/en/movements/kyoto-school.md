# Kyoto School

*1911 – 2019, living tradition*

The Kyoto School is the first great movement of modern Japanese philosophy, born around [Nishida Kitarō](../philosophers/nishida.md) at Kyoto University with An Inquiry into the Good (1911). Its thinkers share the project of taking up resources from Zen Buddhism and [Madhyamaka](../movements/madhyamaka.md) in the language of Western philosophy, from [William James](../philosophers/william-james.md) to [Hegel](../philosophers/hegel.md) and [Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md), but each articulates them differently: Nishida thinks the "place" of [absolute nothingness](../glossary/absolute-nothingness.md), [Tanabe Hajime](../philosophers/tanabe.md) criticizes it in the name of a dialectic of conversion, [Nishitani Keiji](../philosophers/nishitani.md) makes it the path that passes through nihilism to [emptiness](../glossary/sunyata.md). The school opened a lasting dialogue with Christian theology and phenomenology, carried on by Ueda Shizuteru until his death in 2019, the bound recorded here. The wartime writings of some members, notably Tanabe and Nishitani, and their relation to the nationalism of the 1940s remain debated.

## Positions

### [Existence of God](../axes/god-existence.md)

*contextualist*

[Absolute nothingness](../glossary/absolute-nothingness.md) is neither a personal God nor a negation of God: it is the place in which all things, God included, are situated. Hence a dialogue with Christian mysticism (Meister Eckhart) and the theology of kenosis that escapes the alternative between theism and atheism.

### [Nature of reality](../axes/reality-nature.md)

*rejects the alternative*

[Nishida](../philosophers/nishida.md)'s [pure experience](../glossary/pure-experience.md) precedes the split between subject and object: neither mind nor matter is its ground, which rejects the alternative between materialism and idealism.

### [The point of philosophy](../axes/metaphilosophy.md)

*positioned · inferable · major*

Understanding the world 60% · Transforming oneself 40%

[Nishida](../philosophers/nishida.md)'s philosophy aims to be systematic and demonstrative, but it starts from lived experience and seeks a self-awakening. For [Nishitani](../philosophers/nishitani.md), its task is to pass through nihilism to the field of emptiness, where it is overcome.

*Sources : Nishida, An Inquiry into the Good · Nishitani, Religion and Nothingness (1961)*

### [Where the sacred resides](../axes/transcendence.md)

*positioned · inferable · major*

Immanence 65% · Transcendence 35%

For Nishida, the absolute is not found above the world: one meets it by descending to the depths of oneself, where the self gives up positing itself as a sovereign subject, hence the formula of a transcendence within immanence. The absolute remains radically other, yet it is not elsewhere. The axis can only approximate this: the moderate value, on the side of [immanence](../glossary/immanence.md), reflects this refusal to place the absolute outside the world without making it a mere part of it.

*Sources : Nishida, The Logic of the Place of Nothingness and the Religious Worldview (1945)*

## Influenced by

- **[Chan / Zen](../movements/chan-zen.md)** (transmits) : [Nishida](../philosophers/nishida.md) practised zazen for many years before writing his first work, and [Nishitani](../philosophers/nishitani.md) made Zen the privileged interlocutor of Western philosophy. The [Kyoto School](../movements/kyoto-school.md) takes from [Zen](../movements/chan-zen.md) the experience prior to the split between subject and object.
- **[Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md)** (reinterprets) : [Tanabe](../philosophers/tanabe.md), [Nishitani](../philosophers/nishitani.md) and Kuki Shūzō studied with [Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md) in Freiburg. The [Kyoto School](../movements/kyoto-school.md) took up his question of nothingness and his reading of nihilism and carried them toward Buddhist emptiness, where Heidegger remained within the horizon of being.
- **[Madhyamaka](../movements/madhyamaka.md)** (reinterprets) : [Nishitani](../philosophers/nishitani.md) makes the [emptiness](../glossary/sunyata.md) of [Madhyamaka](../movements/madhyamaka.md) the "field" where Western nihilism, as diagnosed by Nietzsche and Heidegger, is passed through and overcome: a Buddhist notion becomes the answer to a crisis of European modernity.
