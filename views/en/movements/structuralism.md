# Structuralism

*c. 1949 – c. 1971*

A method born of [Ferdinand de Saussure](../philosophers/saussure.md)'s linguistics, in which a sign is defined only by its differences from the others, then developed by the phonology of Prague and of Roman Jakobson; in France in the 1950s and 1960s it became the shared model for much of the human sciences. [Claude Lévi-Strauss](../philosophers/levi-strauss.md), who discovered phonology in New York through Jakobson, applied it to kinship (*[The Elementary Structures of Kinship](../works/elementary-structures-of-kinship-levi-strauss.md)*, 1949) and then to myths. [Jacques Lacan](../philosophers/lacan.md) reread [Freud](../philosophers/freud.md) in its light, [Louis Althusser](../philosophers/althusser.md) reread [Marx](../philosophers/marx.md), and Roland Barthes the signs of culture. These authors never formed a unified school around a programme shared by all; [Foucault](../philosophers/foucault.md), often counted among them, rejected the label. It asserted itself against philosophies of the subject, above all [existentialism](../movements/existentialism.md), while remaining in dialogue with [phenomenology](../movements/phenomenology.md) (Merleau-Ponty read Saussure). The dates used here (1949-1971, from the *[Elementary Structures](../works/elementary-structures-of-kinship-levi-strauss.md)* to the last volume of the Mythologiques) are landmarks: structuralist approaches continued in the work of Lacan and Althusser, while the currents called "post-structuralist", starting with [Derrida](../philosophers/derrida.md)'s, were developing from the mid-1960s by debating it.

**Key figures** : [Lévi-Strauss](../philosophers/levi-strauss.md) · [Lacan](../philosophers/lacan.md) · [Althusser](../philosophers/althusser.md)

## Core positions

1. [Individual and society](../axes/individual-society.md) : **It is not the terms that make the system but the system that makes the terms: an individual, a rule, a word only takes on meaning through its place in a structure.**
2. [Freedom of the will](../axes/freedom.md) : **The subject is not the first source of meaning: language, kinship and the unconscious precede it and set limits, without its knowing, on what it can think and will.**

## Positions

### [Individual and society](../axes/individual-society.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Holism 93% · Individualism 7%

[Saussure](../philosophers/saussure.md) had shown that the value of a term in language is purely differential, even though the sign taken as a whole is a positive fact. [Lévi-Strauss](../philosophers/levi-strauss.md) turned this into a method: the [incest prohibition](../glossary/incest-prohibition.md), present everywhere in varied forms, is explained as a rule of exchange between groups, not by the intentions of individuals. [Lacan](../philosophers/lacan.md) holds that the [unconscious](../glossary/unconscious.md) is “structured like a language”. The object of science is no longer the actor but the system of relations: a methodological priority given to relations, which places the movement on the side of [holism](../glossary/holism.md).

*Sources : Saussure, Course in General Linguistics (1916), Part II, ch. IV · Lévi-Strauss, The Elementary Structures of Kinship (1949) · Lacan, Écrits (1966), "Position of the Unconscious"; Seminar XI (1964)*

### [Freedom of the will](../axes/freedom.md)

*positioned · inferable · major*

Hard determinism 75% · Free will 25%

Structuralism does not deny human action; it disputes that consciousness is its key. “The ultimate goal of the human sciences is not to constitute man but to dissolve him”, writes [Lévi-Strauss](../philosophers/levi-strauss.md). [Althusser](../philosophers/althusser.md) defends a [theoretical anti-humanism](../glossary/theoretical-anti-humanism.md): history is a [process without a subject](../glossary/theoretical-anti-humanism.md): no constituting subject (Man, Spirit, the class as consciousness) directs it, which does not deprive actors of their capacity for political action; and [ideology](../glossary/ideology.md) turns individuals into subjects. What is at stake is the subject's determination by structures, which Althusser conceives as structural causality: the structure exists only in its effects, which it produces by organizing the relations between elements, rather than a thesis about free will: hence the moderate value.

*Sources : Lévi-Strauss, The Savage Mind (1962), ch. IX · Althusser, For Marx (1965), "Marxism and Humanism" · Althusser et al., Reading Capital (1965) · Althusser, "Ideology and Ideological State Apparatuses" (1970)*

The position concerns the subject's determination by structures (language, kinship, the unconscious, relations of production), not physical determinism or an explicit denial of free will.

### [The meaning of history](../axes/history-meaning.md)

*positioned · inferable · minor*

Contingency 68% · Directional meaning 32%

In the last chapter of The Savage Mind, [Lévi-Strauss](../philosophers/levi-strauss.md) answers [Sartre](../philosophers/sartre.md)'s Critique of Dialectical Reason: history is not the privileged place where humanity finds its meaning, but one order of knowledge among others. [Althusser](../philosophers/althusser.md), by contrast, keeps a structural explanation of history, driven by class struggle, but denies it any goal set in advance. Three things must be distinguished here: the absence of an end, which the movement asserts; the causal explanation of history, which it does not deny; and complete contingency, which it does not profess. Hence a weak value towards contingency: the movement rejects a meaning of history, not every historical regularity.

*Sources : Lévi-Strauss, The Savage Mind (1962), ch. IX · Althusser, Reply to John Lewis (1973)*

## Influenced by

- **[Existentialism](../movements/existentialism.md)** (reacts against) : [Structuralism](../movements/structuralism.md) rose in France against [existentialism](../movements/existentialism.md) and its philosophy of the subject's freedom. The last chapter of The Savage Mind (1962) answers [Sartre](../philosophers/sartre.md): consciousness and history are not the starting point of intelligibility.
- **[Saussure](../philosophers/saussure.md)** (continues) : [Structuralism](../movements/structuralism.md) generalizes the principle of [Saussure](../philosophers/saussure.md)'s Course in General Linguistics: an element has no value in itself, only through its differences from the other elements of the system. [Lévi-Strauss](../philosophers/levi-strauss.md) received it through Jakobson's phonology.

## Influence on

- **[Foucault](../philosophers/foucault.md)** (reinterprets) : [Foucault](../philosophers/foucault.md) shares with [structuralism](../movements/structuralism.md) the decentring of the subject: The Order of Things (1966) announces the end of man as a figure of knowledge. Yet he rejects the label and replaces timeless structures with historical configurations of knowledge.
- **[Derrida](../philosophers/derrida.md)** (reacts against) : In "Structure, Sign and Play" (1966), [Derrida](../philosophers/derrida.md) reads [Lévi-Strauss](../philosophers/levi-strauss.md) closely to show that every structure presupposes a centre it cannot ground. He keeps the primacy of difference but undoes its closure: it is one of the founding acts of post-structuralism.
