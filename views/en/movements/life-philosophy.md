# Lebensphilosophie

*c. 1883 – c. 1932*

A group of thinkers at the turn of the twentieth century who took life, not knowledge, as their starting point, against [positivism](../movements/positivism.md) and neo-Kantianism. Heinrich Rickert's critique (*Die Philosophie des Lebens*, 1920) is a landmark in the discussion of the label "philosophy of life", already in use. [Wilhelm Dilthey](../philosophers/dilthey.md) grounded the human sciences in [lived experience](../glossary/erlebnis.md) ([Erlebnis](../glossary/erlebnis.md)). [Henri Bergson](../philosophers/bergson.md) set [duration](../glossary/duration.md) against measured time and [intuition](../glossary/intuition.md) against the intellect. [Georg Simmel](../philosophers/simmel.md) conceived life as a flow that gives itself forms and then breaks them. [Nietzsche](../philosophers/nietzsche.md) is its acknowledged precursor. The movement is not a school: Bergson does not belong to the German tradition, and the label was often polemical. Its critique of abstract reason passed into [phenomenology](../movements/phenomenology.md) and [existentialism](../movements/existentialism.md).

**Key figures** : [Dilthey](../philosophers/dilthey.md) · [Bergson](../philosophers/bergson.md) · [Simmel](../philosophers/simmel.md)

## Positions

### [Place of science](../axes/science-place.md)

*positioned · inferable · major*

Epistemic pluralism 83% · Scientism 18%

“We explain nature, we understand mental life”, writes [Dilthey](../philosophers/dilthey.md): there are two ways of knowing reality, causal explanation and understanding, and the second does not reduce to the first. [Bergson](../philosophers/bergson.md) divides the roles differently: science, made for acting on matter, gives valid knowledge of it, but cuts reality up into things; only intuition, which metaphysics cultivates, grasps life from within. In Dilthey, this is a pluralism of scientific methods; in Bergson, a complementarity between science and metaphysics.

*Sources : Dilthey, Ideas Concerning a Descriptive and Analytic Psychology (1894) · Bergson, "Introduction to Metaphysics" (1903), in The Creative Mind*

### [Being and becoming](../axes/being-becoming.md)

*positioned · inferable · major*

Becoming and flux 88% · Permanence of being 13%

Life is first of all movement. When [Bergson](../philosophers/bergson.md) describes [duration](../glossary/duration.md) as a change in which there is no thing that changes, he rejects an immutable substrate beneath change, not the continuity of experience, which is on the contrary that of a melody. [Simmel](../philosophers/simmel.md) describes life as what constantly overflows its own forms. In [Dilthey](../philosophers/dilthey.md), this becoming is mainly historical, not cosmological.

*Sources : Bergson, "The Perception of Change" (1911), in The Creative Mind · Bergson, Creative Evolution (1907), ch. IV · Simmel, The View of Life (1918), ch. I*

## Influenced by

- **[Nietzsche](../philosophers/nietzsche.md)** (continues) : [Lebensphilosophie](../movements/life-philosophy.md) recognized in [Nietzsche](../philosophers/nietzsche.md) the one who made life the criterion of values and knowledge. [Simmel](../philosophers/simmel.md) devoted a book to him, and Rickert's critique (1920) counts him among the movement's founders.
- **[Positivism](../movements/positivism.md)** (reacts against) : [Lebensphilosophie](../movements/life-philosophy.md) was built against the positivist model of a knowledge that knows only laws: [Dilthey](../philosophers/dilthey.md) refused to apply [Comte](../philosophers/comte.md)'s and Mill's method to the human sciences, and [Bergson](../philosophers/bergson.md) set the intuition of duration against the measuring intellect.
