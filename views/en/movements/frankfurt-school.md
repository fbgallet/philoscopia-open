# Frankfurt School

*1923 – …*

The name given to the group formed around the Frankfurt Institute for Social Research, founded in 1923, which [Max Horkheimer](../philosophers/horkheimer.md) came to direct in 1930. In 1937 he set out its programme under the name [critical theory](../glossary/critical-theory.md): a social philosophy nourished by [Marxism](../movements/marxism.md), by [Freud](../philosophers/freud.md) and by [Weber](../philosophers/weber.md)'s sociology, asking why the expected revolution had given way to fascism. In exile in the United States, Horkheimer and [Adorno](../philosophers/adorno.md) wrote *[Dialectic of Enlightenment](../works/dialectic-of-enlightenment-horkheimer.md)* (1944, 1947) in California. [Herbert Marcuse](../philosophers/marcuse.md), who stayed in America, published *[One-Dimensional Man](../works/one-dimensional-man-marcuse.md)* (1964), read by the protest movements of 1968. A second generation, with [Jürgen Habermas](../philosophers/habermas.md), broke with the founders' pessimism: it refounded critique on [communicative reason](../glossary/communicative-reason.md) and deliberative democracy, reconstructing Marx's legacy rather than taking it over. Axel Honneth carries it on with a theory of [recognition](../glossary/recognition.md), in which social conflicts arise from disrespect.

**Key figures** : [Horkheimer](../philosophers/horkheimer.md) · [Adorno](../philosophers/adorno.md) · [Marcuse](../philosophers/marcuse.md) · [Habermas](../philosophers/habermas.md)

## Core positions

1. [The point of philosophy](../axes/metaphilosophy.md) : **A theory of society that merely describes what is ratifies it: critical thinking analyses existing society with a view to the emancipation of those it speaks about.**
2. [Place of science](../axes/science-place.md) : **Reason reduced to calculating means cannot judge ends; to take the method of the sciences for the whole of rationality is to surrender to the facts.**

## Positions

### [The point of philosophy](../axes/metaphilosophy.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Changing the world 85% · Understanding the world 15%

[Horkheimer](../philosophers/horkheimer.md) contrasts two kinds of theory. "Traditional" theory, the model of the sciences since [Descartes](../philosophers/descartes.md), believes it can stand outside the society it studies. [Critical theory](../glossary/critical-theory.md) knows it is part of that society and takes sides: it analyses existing society, its contradictions and its possibilities, with a view to a rational organization of common life. In [Adorno](../philosophers/adorno.md), this aim gives itself no programme: it works through the [determinate negation](../glossary/determinate-negation.md) of what is and through the possibilities art still holds; in [Habermas](../philosophers/habermas.md), it becomes a defence of discussion free from domination.

*Sources : Horkheimer, Traditional and Critical Theory (1937) · Adorno, Negative Dialectics (1966) · Habermas, The Theory of Communicative Action (1981)*

### [Place of science](../axes/science-place.md)

*positioned · inferable · major*

Epistemic pluralism 80% · Scientism 20%

The founders' shared thesis targets the reduction of reason to [instrumental reason](../glossary/raison-instrumentale.md), not rationality as such. The Enlightenment, which sought to free human beings from fear through knowledge, turned knowledge into an instrument for dominating nature, and then human beings. A reason that only calculates means cannot say whether an end is good. Hence the opposition to positivism, culminating in the "positivist dispute" in Tübingen (1961), where [Adorno](../philosophers/adorno.md) confronted [Popper](../philosophers/popper.md), who nonetheless rejected the positivist label. [Habermas](../philosophers/habermas.md) shifts the thesis: science is legitimate in its own order, the mistake is to take it for the only model of knowledge.

*Sources : Horkheimer and Adorno, Dialectic of Enlightenment (1947), "The Concept of Enlightenment" · Horkheimer, Eclipse of Reason (1947) · Adorno et al., The Positivist Dispute in German Sociology (German ed. 1969)*

### [Relation to technology](../axes/technology.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Techno-criticism 78% · Techno-optimism 22%

For [Marcuse](../philosophers/marcuse.md), technology is not neutral: advanced industrial society absorbs its opponents by satisfying needs it produces itself, and the technical apparatus becomes the vehicle of a faceless domination. [Habermas](../philosophers/habermas.md) replies that technology, the rationality of instrumental action, is not the problem; the danger lies in its extension to the spheres of interaction.

*Sources : Marcuse, One-Dimensional Man (1964) · Habermas, Technology and Science as "Ideology" (1968)*

### [Status of knowledge](../axes/knowledge-status.md)

*positioned · inferable · minor*

Constructivism 65% · Scientific realism 35%

According to [Horkheimer](../philosophers/horkheimer.md), the facts the scientist perceives are already "preformed" by social practice. Knowledge does not thereby become arbitrary: it remains answerable to the facts, but it is mediated by history and by the interests that guide it. [Habermas](../philosophers/habermas.md) turns this into a doctrine of three knowledge-constitutive interests (technical, practical, emancipatory), which does not relativize truth but distinguishes its domains.

*Sources : Horkheimer, Traditional and Critical Theory (1937) · Habermas, Knowledge and Human Interests (1968)*

## Influenced by

- **[Sigmund Freud](../philosophers/freud.md)** (synthesizes) : From the 1930s the Frankfurt Institute combined psychoanalysis with social critique to understand why the masses consent to their domination. In Eros and Civilization, [Marcuse](../philosophers/marcuse.md) drew from it a reading of [Freud](../philosophers/freud.md) in which the repression of drives becomes a historical fact.
- **[Marxism](../movements/marxism.md)** (reinterprets) : The [Frankfurt School](../movements/frankfurt-school.md) starts from [Marx](../philosophers/marx.md)'s critique of political economy but moves away from his historical schema: with no revolutionary subject, critique turns to culture, personality and reason itself, and rejects the official Marxism of the USSR.
- **[Max Weber](../philosophers/weber.md)** (reinterprets) : The rationalization [Weber](../philosophers/weber.md) described as the fate of the West becomes, in [Horkheimer](../philosophers/horkheimer.md) and [Adorno](../philosophers/adorno.md), [instrumental reason](../glossary/raison-instrumentale.md): no longer a fact to be recorded but a domination to be criticized. [Habermas](../philosophers/habermas.md) again takes up Weber to set instrumental action against communicative action.
