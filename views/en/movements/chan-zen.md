# Chan / Zen

*c. 520 – c. 1769, living tradition*

A school of [Buddhism](../movements/buddhism.md) born in China, which traces its lineage back to Bodhidharma, a largely legendary figure whose arrival in China tradition places around 520. The *[Platform Sutra](../works/platform-sutra-huineng.md)*, attributed to [Huineng](../philosophers/huineng.md), defends a [sudden awakening](../glossary/sudden-awakening.md) against a gradual path, an opposition largely built by polemics within Chan. [Linji](../philosophers/linji.md) taught in the ninth century through shouts and paradoxes; it was under the Song that the case collections and the [kōan](../glossary/koan.md) method took shape. In Japan the school took the name Zen: [Dōgen](../philosophers/dogen.md) founded Sōtō there, derived from Chinese Caodong and which emphasizes [shikantaza](../glossary/shikantaza.md) ("just sitting"), a sitting that is not a means to something else but a discipline of attention, and [Hakuin](../philosophers/hakuin.md) renewed kōan practice in Rinzai in the eighteenth century. These are pedagogical emphases, not exclusive practices: Dōgen comments on kōans, and Rinzai practises seated meditation. The period covered stops at Hakuin as an editorial choice; the living tradition has kept changing since, in Japan and then in the West. The formula that defines the school, "a special transmission outside the scriptures, not founded on words", is a construction of the Song period, and Chan produced an immense literature. Zen nourished the [Kyoto School](../movements/kyoto-school.md).

**Key figures** : [Huineng](../philosophers/huineng.md) · [Linji](../philosophers/linji.md) · [Dōgen](../philosophers/dogen.md) · [Hakuin](../philosophers/hakuin.md)

## Positions

### [The point of philosophy](../axes/metaphilosophy.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Transforming oneself 100%

The aim is not to adhere to a doctrine but to realize the awakening it points to, in order to be freed from suffering; Buddhist teaching remains the framework, study alone is not enough. For [Dōgen](../philosophers/dogen.md), practice and awakening are one: one does not sit as a means towards a future awakening, authentic sitting is already the expression of awakening. Chan told itself the story of an opposition between [sudden awakening](../glossary/sudden-awakening.md) and the [gradual path](../glossary/sudden-awakening.md) in connection with [Huineng](../philosophers/huineng.md), but both sides aim at the same transformation of the one who practises.

*Sources : Platform Sutra (Dunhuang version) · Dōgen, Bendōwa (1231)*

### [Where the sacred resides](../axes/transcendence.md)

*positioned · inferable · minor*

Immanence 75% · Transcendence 25%

Awakening is not to be sought in another world: it is realized here, in ordinary gestures, which brings the school close to [immanence](../glossary/immanence.md). [Linji](../philosophers/linji.md) urges his disciples to stop seeking outside themselves and simply to be ordinary. Dōgen refuses to make [Buddha nature](../glossary/buddha-nature.md) an inner substance that everyone would possess: he reads "all beings are Buddha nature" and identifies it with [impermanence](../glossary/anicca.md) itself. This nonduality of everyday life and awakening is not a denial of any beyond, and the Buddhist framework of karma and [rebirth](../glossary/samsara.md) remains: hence a moderate value.

*Sources : Linji lu (Record of Linji), "Discourses" · Dōgen, Shōbōgenzō, "Busshō" (1241)*

## Influenced by

- **[Taoism](../movements/taoism.md)** (synthesizes) : [Chan](../movements/chan-zen.md) took shape in a China where Buddhist texts were read and translated through the vocabulary of [Laozi](../philosophers/laozi.md) and [Zhuangzi](../philosophers/zhuangzi.md): spontaneity, distrust of words, everyday wisdom. The extent of this debt remains debated.
- **[Yogācāra](../movements/yogacara.md)** (transmits) : Early [Chan](../movements/chan-zen.md) claimed the Laṅkāvatāra Sūtra, a text close to [Yogācāra](../movements/yogacara.md), and kept from that school the idea that awakening consists in recognizing the nature of the mind.

## Influence on

- **[Kyoto School](../movements/kyoto-school.md)** (transmits) : [Nishida](../philosophers/nishida.md) practised zazen for many years before writing his first work, and [Nishitani](../philosophers/nishitani.md) made Zen the privileged interlocutor of Western philosophy. The [Kyoto School](../movements/kyoto-school.md) takes from [Zen](../movements/chan-zen.md) the experience prior to the split between subject and object.
