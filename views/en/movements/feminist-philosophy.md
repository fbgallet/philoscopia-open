# Feminist philosophy

*1792 – …*

A family of philosophies that make the condition of women and gender both an object and an instrument of critique. They had forerunners (Christine de Pizan, François Poulain de la Barre); the landmark taken here is *[A Vindication of the Rights of Woman](../works/vindication-of-the-rights-of-woman-wollstonecraft.md)* (1792), in which [Mary Wollstonecraft](../philosophers/wollstonecraft.md) turns the principles of the [Enlightenment](../movements/enlightenment.md) against [Rousseau](../philosophers/rousseau.md). [Simone de Beauvoir](../philosophers/beauvoir.md)'s *[The Second Sex](../works/the-second-sex-beauvoir.md)* (1949) ranks among its major works. From the 1970s the field diversified: [standpoint epistemologies](../glossary/standpoint-epistemology.md), materialist feminism (Christine Delphy) and Black feminism (bell hooks), the ethics of [care](../glossary/care-ethics.md) stemming from [Carol Gilligan](../philosophers/gilligan.md), [Judith Butler](../philosophers/butler.md)'s theory of gender [performativity](../glossary/gender-performativity.md). The disagreements bear on equality, on the place of the body and on sexual difference, which Luce Irigaray thinks of as a difference to be brought into existence rather than erased: the movement is a field of controversies more than a doctrine.

**Key figures** : [Wollstonecraft](../philosophers/wollstonecraft.md) · [Beauvoir](../philosophers/beauvoir.md) · [Butler](../philosophers/butler.md)

## Positions

### [Social identity](../axes/social-identity.md)

*positioned · inferable · major*

Social construction 70% · Social realism 30%

The argument goes back to [Wollstonecraft](../philosophers/wollstonecraft.md): without denying bodily differences, she disputes treating as natural the traits that education has produced. [Beauvoir](../philosophers/beauvoir.md) shows that woman has been constituted as man's [Other](../glossary/the-other.md), without biology or psychology imposing it. [Butler](../philosophers/butler.md), for her part, argues that gender expresses no [essence](../glossary/essentialism.md): it is produced through the repetition of acts governed by norms, which are not freely chosen. Materialist and Black feminisms insist on the social reality of these categories, which structure relations of domination, hence the share of social realism; Irigaray conceives sexual difference as an ontological difference rather than a mere [construction](../glossary/social-constructionism.md).

> “One is not born, but rather becomes, a woman. No biological, psychic, or economic destiny defines the figure that the human female assumes within society.”, The Second Sex, vol. II

*Sources : Wollstonecraft, A Vindication of the Rights of Woman (1792), ch. II · Beauvoir, The Second Sex (1949), vol. II, part 1 · Butler, Gender Trouble (1990) · Irigaray, Speculum of the Other Woman (1974) · bell hooks, Ain't I a Woman? (1981)*

### [The point of philosophy](../axes/metaphilosophy.md)

*positioned · inferable · major*

Changing the world 80% · Clarifying thought 15% · Understanding the world 5%

Feminist philosophy is critical in the strong sense: by showing that a hierarchy passes itself off as natural, it is already working to undo it. [Beauvoir](../philosophers/beauvoir.md) starts from the question of what a woman is in order to describe how man has posited himself as the subject and the absolute, and woman as the [Other](../glossary/the-other.md). [Standpoint epistemologies](../glossary/standpoint-epistemology.md) draw a thesis about knowledge from this: a critical standpoint, worked out from a dominated position, can see what the dominant position conceals; occupying that position is not enough to confer it. The share of conceptual clarification (of sex, gender, equality) is real, but it serves this critical project.

*Sources : Beauvoir, The Second Sex (1949), introduction · Harding, Whose Science? Whose Knowledge? (1991)*
