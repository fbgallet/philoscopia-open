# Falsafa

*c. 830 – c. 1198, living tradition*

[Falsafa](../movements/falsafa.md) (the Arabic transcription of the Greek **philosophia**) refers to philosophy in the Greek tradition as practised in the lands of Islam. Born of the great translation movement sponsored in Baghdad by the Abbasid caliphs and their courts, which tradition associates with the "House of Wisdom", it opens with [al-Kindī](../philosophers/al-kindi.md), is systematized by [al-Fārābī](../philosophers/al-farabi.md), the "second teacher" after [Aristotle](../philosophers/aristotle.md), culminates in [Avicenna](../philosophers/avicenna.md) and finds its last great Western representative in [Averroes](../philosophers/averroes.md), commentator on Aristotle in Córdoba. These thinkers do not form a uniform doctrine: al-Fārābī thinks above all about the city and prophecy, Avicenna builds a metaphysics of being that breaks free of Aristotle, Averroes returns to the Aristotelian text and to the interpretation of religious Law. Falsafa inherited an Aristotle read through the Neoplatonic commentators, weathered the critique of the [kalām](../movements/kalam.md) theologians and, translated into Latin in the twelfth and thirteenth centuries, transformed scholasticism. The period recorded here ends with Averroes, who closes the Andalusian line; in the East, Avicennism was renewed again by Suhrawardī, Naṣīr al-Dīn al-Ṭūsī and Mullā Ṣadrā, in the Persian tradition of ḥikma (wisdom), still taught today.

## Core positions

1. [Faith and reason](../axes/faith-reason.md) : **Truth does not contradict truth: where Revelation seems to belie demonstration, it is the text that must be interpreted.**
2. [Existence of God](../axes/god-existence.md) : **Existing is not contained in what a thing is: there must therefore be a being whose essence is to exist, the [Necessary Existent](../glossary/necessary-existent.md).**
3. [The point of philosophy](../axes/metaphilosophy.md) : **Man fulfils his nature by thinking: supreme happiness is the conjunction of the human intellect with the [agent intellect](../glossary/agent-intellect.md).**

## Positions

### [Faith and reason](../axes/faith-reason.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Harmony 90% · Separate domains 10%

Already for [al-Fārābī](../philosophers/al-farabi.md), religion expresses in symbols what philosophy establishes in concepts; [Averroes](../philosophers/averroes.md) turns this into an explicit legal and hermeneutical doctrine. Revelation and demonstration state one and the same truth to different audiences: the multitude is persuaded by images and rhetoric, the philosopher convinced by [demonstration](../glossary/burhan.md) ([burhān](../glossary/burhan.md), reasoning from certain premises). When a verse seems to contradict a demonstrated conclusion, it is the text that must receive a figurative interpretation, not reason that must yield. The "double truth" later ascribed to the Latin Averroists distorts this thesis, which on the contrary asserts the unity of truth.

*Sources : Averroes, The Decisive Treatise · al-Fārābī, Book of Religion*

### [Existence of God](../axes/god-existence.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Theism 98% · Atheism 3%

[Avicenna](../philosophers/avicenna.md) grounds God's existence in the analysis of being alone, without starting from motion as Aristotle had. A thing is [possible in itself](../glossary/essence-existence-distinction.md) when nothing in its nature requires it to exist: if it exists, a cause makes it exist. Even the totality of possible beings, taken as a whole, still depends on a cause that cannot itself be merely possible; there must therefore be a being [necessary in itself](../glossary/necessary-existent.md), the [Necessary Existent](../glossary/necessary-existent.md) ([wājib al-wujūd](../glossary/necessary-existent.md)), whose essence is to exist. Together with the [distinction between essence and existence](../glossary/essence-existence-distinction.md), this argument passed to [Aquinas](../philosophers/aquinas.md) and the whole of Latin metaphysics. [Al-Ghazālī](../philosophers/al-ghazali.md) would object that, in a world assumed to be eternal, the philosophers can establish neither a true Creator nor his oneness.

*Sources : Avicenna, The Book of Healing, Metaphysics, I.5-7 and VIII.1-3 · Avicenna, Remarks and Admonitions*

### [The point of philosophy](../axes/metaphilosophy.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Understanding the world 85% · Transforming oneself 15%

For the [falāsifa](../movements/falsafa.md), in formulations that vary from al-Fārābī to Averroes, man's ultimate happiness is intellectual: the perfection of the human intellect as it conjoins with the [agent intellect](../glossary/agent-intellect.md). The latter is a separate intelligence that brings our thinking from potentiality to act, as light makes colours visible. Philosophy is not one way of life among others but the completion of human nature, of which moral virtue is the precondition.

*Sources : al-Fārābī, The Attainment of Happiness · Ibn Bājja, The Governance of the Solitary*

### [Who should decide](../axes/who-decides.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · minor*

Epistocracy 85% · Radical democracy 15%

[Al-Fārābī](../philosophers/al-farabi.md) transposes [Plato](../philosophers/plato.md)'s republic into the Islamic city: the [virtuous city](../glossary/virtuous-city.md) is governed by a ruler who unites philosopher and prophet, able to grasp the truth through the intellect and to translate it into symbols for all. [Averroes](../philosophers/averroes.md) also wrote a commentary on the Republic, a sign that the ideal of government by knowledge is shared across the school.

*Sources : al-Fārābī, The Opinions of the People of the Virtuous City · Averroes, Commentary on Plato's Republic*

## Influenced by

- **[Platonism](../movements/platonism.md)** (synthesizes) : [Falsafa](../movements/falsafa.md) read Aristotle through [Plotinus](../philosophers/plotinus.md) and Proclus, whose Arabic paraphrases circulated under Aristotle's name: the Theology of Aristotle and the Book of Causes. The Neoplatonic doctrine of [emanation](../glossary/emanation.md) fused there with Aristotelian metaphysics to yield the cosmology of intellects of al-Fārābī and Avicenna.

## Influence on

- **[Maimonides](../philosophers/maimonides.md)** (continues) : [Maimonides](../philosophers/maimonides.md) thinks within the framework of [falsafa](../movements/falsafa.md): he recommends [al-Fārābī](../philosophers/al-farabi.md) above every other author and takes up his conception of the prophet as a philosopher endowed with a perfect imagination.
- **[al-Ghazālī](../philosophers/al-ghazali.md)** (reacts against) : [Al-Ghazālī](../philosophers/al-ghazali.md) refutes twenty theses of the [falsafa](../movements/falsafa.md) philosophers, notably the eternity of the world and the necessity of the causal link, and declares three of them incompatible with Islam: the eternity of the world, divine knowledge restricted to universals and the denial of bodily resurrection.
