# Philosophical hermeneutics

*1960 – …*

A movement that makes interpretation the central question of philosophy, refounded by [Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md)'s [phenomenology](../movements/phenomenology.md). Hermeneutics was long an art of reading difficult texts, scripture or law. [Schleiermacher](../philosophers/schleiermacher.md) made it a general theory of understanding, both grammatical (the language of the text) and psychological (the author's thought), and [Dilthey](../philosophers/dilthey.md) the method of the human sciences. Heidegger shifted it by making understanding a feature of existence itself. Philosophical hermeneutics is taken here to begin with [Hans-Georg Gadamer](../philosophers/gadamer.md)'s *[Truth and Method](../works/truth-and-method-gadamer.md)* (1960). [Paul Ricœur](../philosophers/ricoeur.md) broadened it to symbol, psychoanalysis and narrative, bringing it into dialogue with the human sciences. Gadamer's debate with [Habermas](../philosophers/habermas.md) (1967-1971) over the authority of tradition and the critique of ideology remains its high point; Ricœur sought a mediation between them.

**Key figures** : [Gadamer](../philosophers/gadamer.md) · [Ricœur](../philosophers/ricoeur.md)

## Core positions

1. [Place of science](../axes/science-place.md) : **There is a truth that method does not exhaust: the truth we undergo in the work of art, in history and in dialogue, which cannot be checked like a laboratory experiment.**
2. [Temporal orientation](../axes/time-orientation.md) : **We never understand from nowhere: our prejudices, inherited from a tradition, make understanding possible, even though understanding must put them to the test.**

## Positions

### [Place of science](../axes/science-place.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Epistemic pluralism 90% · Scientism 10%

[Gadamer](../philosophers/gadamer.md) starts from the experience of art: the work teaches us something, and this truth cannot be reduced to the outcome of a methodical procedure. The human sciences were wrong, in his view, to seek their legitimacy by imitating the natural sciences; he does not reject method, he rejects the claim that it is the only access to truth. [Ricœur](../philosophers/ricoeur.md) refuses to choose between explaining and understanding: the structural analysis of a text prepares our appropriation of its meaning instead of replacing it.

*Sources : Gadamer, Truth and Method (1960), introduction and Part I · Ricœur, "Explanation and Understanding", From Text to Action (1986)*

### [Temporal orientation](../axes/time-orientation.md)

*positioned · inferable · major*

Memory and transmission 80% · Project 20%

The Enlightenment discredited [prejudice](../glossary/prejudice.md); [Gadamer](../philosophers/gadamer.md) rehabilitates the word in its original sense, a judgment made before examination. Every interpreter belongs to a tradition that has already worked on the object as well as on the interpreter's own situation, what he calls [effective history](../glossary/effective-history.md) (*Wirkungsgeschichte*). Not all prejudices are legitimate: temporal distance and dialogue help sort those that illuminate from those that mislead. To understand is to achieve a [fusion of horizons](../glossary/fusion-of-horizons.md): the horizon of the present forms only by widening to include that of the past. The forward-looking share comes from the anticipation of meaning, inherited from Heidegger: we always understand by projecting ourselves towards a meaning to come, which application to the present fulfils. [Habermas](../philosophers/habermas.md) objects that a tradition can also pass on domination, which hermeneutics alone cannot unmask.

*Sources : Gadamer, Truth and Method (1960), Part II, II.1 · Habermas, On the Logic of the Social Sciences (1967)*

### [The meaning of words](../axes/meaning.md)

*positioned · extrapolated · minor*

Use theory 75% · Speaker's intention 25%

The axis concerns the meaning of words; hermeneutics speaks of the interpretation of texts, hence a correspondence that is only approximate. [Schleiermacher](../philosophers/schleiermacher.md) gave interpretation the aim of reconstructing the author's thought. [Gadamer](../philosophers/gadamer.md) replies that the meaning of a text goes beyond its author, not occasionally but always. [Ricœur](../philosophers/ricoeur.md) explains this through [distanciation](../glossary/distanciation.md): once written, the text frees itself from its author and its first audience. Meaning is at stake in application, which brings it closer to use; the author's intention remains a landmark, hence its share, without being the norm of interpretation.

*Sources : Gadamer, Truth and Method (1960), Part II, II.1.c · Ricœur, "The Hermeneutical Function of Distanciation", From Text to Action (1986)*

The axis's poles only approximate this position: for hermeneutics, meaning is neither a fixed reference nor a mere convention of linguistic use, but an event of understanding accomplished in application.

### [Nature of the self](../axes/self-nature.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · minor*

Narrativism 85% · Psychological continuity 15%

The position belongs first of all to [Ricœur](../philosophers/ricoeur.md): the self is not grasped directly but indirectly, through signs, works and narratives. He distinguishes idem-identity, the sameness of character, which answers the question of permanence over time, hence a share left to continuity, from ipse-identity, selfhood, self-constancy in keeping one's word. [Narrative identity](../glossary/narrative-identity.md) mediates between the two: by telling one's life, one holds together what endures and what changes.

*Sources : Ricœur, Time and Narrative, III (1985) · Ricœur, Oneself as Another (1990), fifth and sixth studies*

## Influenced by

- **[Dilthey](../philosophers/dilthey.md)** (reinterprets) : [Dilthey](../philosophers/dilthey.md) had made understanding the proper method of the human sciences. [Gadamer](../philosophers/gadamer.md) credits him with that question but reproaches him for remaining captive to the natural sciences' ideal of objectivity: understanding is not a method but our belonging to history.
- **[Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md)** (continues) : [Heidegger](../philosophers/heidegger.md) turned understanding from a method into a mode of being of [Dasein](../glossary/dasein.md), always caught in a circle of anticipations. [Gadamer](../philosophers/gadamer.md), his student, built [philosophical hermeneutics](../movements/hermeneutics.md) on this thesis.
- **[Schleiermacher](../philosophers/schleiermacher.md)** (reacts against) : [Schleiermacher](../philosophers/schleiermacher.md) founded general hermeneutics, whose aim is to understand the author as well as and better than he understood himself. [Philosophical hermeneutics](../movements/hermeneutics.md) keeps its scope but rejects this aim: the meaning of a text is not its author's intention.

## Influence on

- **[Habermas](../philosophers/habermas.md)** (reacts against) : Against [Gadamer](../philosophers/gadamer.md), [Habermas](../philosophers/habermas.md) argues that a tradition can pass on communication distorted by domination; what is needed is therefore a critique of ideology that does not depend on tradition. The debate (1967-1971) led [Ricœur](../philosophers/ricoeur.md) to seek a way of articulating the two attitudes.
