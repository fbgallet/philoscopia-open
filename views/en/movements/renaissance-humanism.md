# Renaissance humanism

*c. 1341 – c. 1592*

A movement born in fourteenth-century Italy around [Petrarch](../philosophers/petrarch.md), which made the [studia humanitatis](../glossary/studia-humanitatis.md) (grammar, rhetoric, history, poetry, moral philosophy) the basis of a new culture. The humanists sought out ancient texts, edited and translated them: their method was the return to the sources, ad fontes. They often criticized [scholasticism](../movements/scholasticism.md), the philosophy of the medieval universities, mocking its Latin and its subtleties, though some combined it with their reading, like [Pico della Mirandola](../philosophers/pico.md). In Florence, the [civic humanism](../glossary/civic-humanism.md) of the chancellors, from Leonardo Bruni to [Machiavelli](../philosophers/machiavelli.md), made the study of the ancients a school for political life; another, more contemplative current, that of Ficino, blended [Neoplatonism](../movements/neoplatonism.md) into it, and the two met in the same circles. In the North, [Erasmus](../philosophers/erasmus.md) made it a [philosophy of Christ](../glossary/philosophy-of-christ.md), a piety grounded in the Gospel reread in its sources rather than in the theology of the schools. [Montaigne](../philosophers/montaigne.md) reworked its legacy in a sceptical vein. The movement's unity lies in shared practices of reading and interpreting texts more than in a doctrine: Pico exalts [human dignity](../glossary/dignity-of-man.md) where Montaigne deflates human presumption. The dates used (1341, Petrarch's coronation; 1592, Montaigne's death) are landmarks.

**Key figures** : [Petrarch](../philosophers/petrarch.md) · [Pico della Mirandola](../philosophers/pico.md) · [Erasmus](../philosophers/erasmus.md) · [Montaigne](../philosophers/montaigne.md)

## Positions

### [Temporal orientation](../axes/time-orientation.md)

*positioned · inferable · major*

Memory and transmission 85% · Project 15%

For the humanists, the future is built through memory. [Petrarch](../philosophers/petrarch.md) judges his age barbarous and seeks living interlocutors in Cicero and [Seneca](../philosophers/seneca.md), to whom he addresses letters. To restore the exact text of the ancients, following the ad fontes method, is to restore a way of living.

*Sources : Petrarch, Letters on Familiar Matters, XXIV.3-4 (letters to Cicero and Seneca) · Erasmus, Antibarbari (1520)*

### [The point of philosophy](../axes/metaphilosophy.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Transforming oneself 75% · Changing the world 15% · Understanding the world 10%

Philosophy is worth what it makes of the person who practises it. [Petrarch](../philosophers/petrarch.md) asserts this against the dialecticians: it is better to will the good than to know the truth. [Erasmus](../philosophers/erasmus.md) and [Montaigne](../philosophers/montaigne.md) carry on this primacy of moral philosophy over speculation. The civic humanists of Florence, from Salutati to Bruni, also defend the active life in the service of the city: to form the citizen through letters is to serve the republic, which gives a smaller share to transforming the world.

*Sources : Petrarch, On His Own Ignorance and That of Many Others (1367) · Bruni, Dialogues to Pier Paolo Vergerio (c. 1401-1406) · Montaigne, Essays, I, 26*

### [Freedom of the will](../axes/freedom.md)

*positioned · inferable · minor*

Free will 80% · Hard determinism 20%

[Pico](../philosophers/pico.md) has God speak to Adam: the human being has no fixed place and gives itself the form it chooses, within a created order. [Erasmus](../philosophers/erasmus.md) defends against Luther a [free will](../glossary/free-will.md) that cooperates with grace, which remains indispensable to salvation, and Luther answers him with the bondage of the will (1524-1525). The position holds for Pico and Erasmus; [Montaigne](../philosophers/montaigne.md) stands back from this exaltation.

*Sources : Pico della Mirandola, Oration on the Dignity of Man (1486) · Erasmus, On Free Will (1524)*

## Influenced by

- **[Neoplatonism](../movements/neoplatonism.md)** (reinterprets) : In Florence, Ficino translated Plato and then Plotinus (1492) and drew from them a Platonic theology of the soul; [Pico della Mirandola](../philosophers/pico.md) took from them the idea of a concord of all wisdoms. [Neoplatonism](../movements/neoplatonism.md) gave humanism its metaphysics of love and beauty.
- **[Scholasticism](../movements/scholasticism.md)** (reacts against) : The humanists blamed [scholasticism](../movements/scholasticism.md) for its barbarous Latin and for subtleties that make no one better. [Petrarch](../philosophers/petrarch.md) mocked the dialecticians, and [Erasmus](../philosophers/erasmus.md) lampooned the theologians in The Praise of Folly.
