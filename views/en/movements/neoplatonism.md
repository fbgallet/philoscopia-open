# Neoplatonism

*c. 244 – c. 529*

The name moderns give to the last great form of ancient [Platonism](../movements/platonism.md), which opens with [Plotinus](../philosophers/plotinus.md)'s teaching in Rome from 244. His pupil [Porphyry](../philosophers/porphyry.md) edits his treatises as the *[Enneads](../works/enneads-plotinus.md)*. With [Iamblichus](../philosophers/iamblichus.md) and then the schools of Athens and Alexandria, the tradition becomes a systematic commentary on [Plato](../philosophers/plato.md) and [Aristotle](../philosophers/aristotle.md), whom it holds to be in agreement. In the fifth century [Proclus](../philosophers/proclus.md) gives it its most architectonic synthesis, the *[Elements of Theology](../works/elements-of-theology-proclus.md)*. Yet the Neoplatonists part ways on decisive points: the part of the soul that would never descend into the body, defended by Plotinus and rejected by Iamblichus and Proclus; and the place of [theurgy](../glossary/theurgy.md), the rites meant to unite the soul with the gods, alongside contemplation. The school's end is conventionally dated to 529, when Justinian banned pagan teaching in Athens; in Alexandria, teaching went on for a few more decades. Its thought passes into [Augustine](../philosophers/augustine.md), into Pseudo-Dionysius, into [falsafa](../movements/falsafa.md) through the Theology of Aristotle, and then into the Renaissance with Ficino's translation of Plotinus (1492).

**Key figures** : [Plotinus](../philosophers/plotinus.md) · [Porphyry](../philosophers/porphyry.md) · [Iamblichus](../philosophers/iamblichus.md) · [Proclus](../philosophers/proclus.md)

## Core positions

1. [Where the sacred resides](../axes/transcendence.md) : **The first principle is not the supreme being: [the One](../glossary/the-one.md) lies beyond being and thought, and that is why it can ground everything.**
2. [Nature of reality](../axes/reality-nature.md) : **Reality is a cascade: everything [proceeds](../glossary/emanation.md) from the One by decreasing degrees, from [Intellect](../glossary/nous.md) to Soul and down to matter, almost non-being.**
3. [The meaning of life](../axes/life-meaning.md) : **To live is to ascend: the meaning of a life is measured by the [conversion](../glossary/epistrophe.md) that turns it back towards its source.**

## Positions

### [Where the sacred resides](../axes/transcendence.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Transcendence 98% · Immanence 3%

The argument starts from unity. Everything that is, is one; but [Intellect](../glossary/nous.md), which thinks, is already double, subject and object. The principle must therefore precede this duality. [Plotinus](../philosophers/plotinus.md) takes up the *[Republic](../works/republic-plato.md)*'s claim that the Good lies beyond being and turns it into a thesis about the origin. All predication fails before the One: it can be designated only as the cause of what comes from it, or by negation, hence a [negative way](../glossary/apophatic-way.md) that Christian theology would take up. [Proclus](../philosophers/proclus.md) pushes this [transcendence](../glossary/transcendence.md) to the extreme: the One lies even beyond the name "One".

*Sources : Plotinus, Enneads, V.4 and VI.9 · Plato, Republic, VI, 509b · Proclus, Platonic Theology, II*

### [Nature of reality](../axes/reality-nature.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Idealism 100%

[Procession](../glossary/emanation.md) answers the problem of how the many come from the One. What is perfect overflows without being exhausted, as light radiates from its source. Intellect arises from the One by turning towards it, and Soul from Intellect in the same way. This procession is outside time: it expresses dependence, not succession. Matter is the point where this power dies away, not a second principle: Neoplatonism is a spiritual monism, not a [dualism](../glossary/dualism.md). The status of matter nonetheless varies by author: a sheer lack in Plotinus, it proceeds directly from the principle in Proclus.

*Sources : Plotinus, Enneads, V.2 and II.4 · Proclus, Elements of Theology, props. 25-35*

### [The meaning of life](../axes/life-meaning.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Teleology and providence 100%

The movement by which things come forth reverses into a movement of return, [epistrophē](../glossary/epistrophe.md). The soul, turned towards the body, has forgotten itself; it finds itself again by turning away from the sensible through the [cathartic virtues](../glossary/cathartic-virtues.md), which purify the soul of its attachments to the body, then through dialectic, up to [union](../glossary/henosis.md) with the One, which exceeds discursive thought itself. This detachment is not a renunciation of a worthless world: it fulfils what everything tends towards. [Porphyry](../philosophers/porphyry.md) reports that [Plotinus](../philosophers/plotinus.md) attained this union four times during the years he was with him. For [Iamblichus](../philosophers/iamblichus.md) and [Proclus](../philosophers/proclus.md), reason alone is not enough for this ascent: [theurgy](../glossary/theurgy.md) is needed too.

*Sources : Plotinus, Enneads, I.6 and VI.9 · Porphyry, Life of Plotinus, 23 · Iamblichus, On the Mysteries, II.11*

### [The problem of evil](../axes/problem-of-evil.md)

*positioned · inferable · major*

Evil has its reasons 100%

If everything comes from the Good, evil cannot be a positive reality. [Plotinus](../philosophers/plotinus.md) identifies it with matter, the absolute [privation](../glossary/privation.md) of form, and defends a providence that orders the whole, as a playwright hands out unequal parts. [Proclus](../philosophers/proclus.md) rejects this identification: matter, which also issues from the principle, cannot be evil. He makes evil a parasitic existence, born of the failure of particular actions. Both readings share the same [theodicy](../glossary/theodicy.md), transposed here from a creator God to a metaphysics of the Good: evil is a lack, not a principle.

*Sources : Plotinus, Enneads, I.8 and III.2-3 · Proclus, On the Existence of Evils*

### [The point of philosophy](../axes/metaphilosophy.md)

*positioned · explicit in the texts · major*

Understanding the world 50% · Transforming oneself 50%

Philosophy here is at once the science of principles and an exercise of purification. Commenting on Plato and practising the [cathartic virtues](../glossary/cathartic-virtues.md) are two stages of one path, which [Porphyry](../philosophers/porphyry.md)'s hierarchy of virtues orders from the civic virtues up to the contemplative ones.

*Sources : Porphyry, Sentences, 32 · Plotinus, Enneads, I.2*

## Influenced by

- **[Aristotelianism](../movements/aristotelianism.md)** (synthesizes) : The [Neoplatonists](../movements/neoplatonism.md) built [Aristotle](../philosophers/aristotle.md)'s logic and physics into a curriculum leading to Plato, holding the two masters to be in agreement. [Porphyry](../philosophers/porphyry.md)'s Isagoge and his commentaries on the Categories are the centrepiece of this synthesis.

## Influence on

- **[Hegel](../philosophers/hegel.md)** (reinterprets) : [Hegel](../philosophers/hegel.md) saw in [Plotinus](../philosophers/plotinus.md) and above all [Proclus](../philosophers/proclus.md) the summits of ancient philosophy: their triad of remaining, procession and return foreshadows, in his eyes, the dialectical movement of Spirit, which he sought to think without ecstasy.
- **[Renaissance humanism](../movements/renaissance-humanism.md)** (reinterprets) : In Florence, Ficino translated Plato and then Plotinus (1492) and drew from them a Platonic theology of the soul; [Pico della Mirandola](../philosophers/pico.md) took from them the idea of a concord of all wisdoms. [Neoplatonism](../movements/neoplatonism.md) gave humanism its metaphysics of love and beauty.
- **[Scholasticism](../movements/scholasticism.md)** (transmits) : [Neoplatonism](../movements/neoplatonism.md) reached [scholasticism](../movements/scholasticism.md) through texts that concealed their origin: Pseudo-Dionysius, read as a disciple of Saint Paul, and the *Liber de causis*, ascribed to Aristotle, which [Thomas Aquinas](../philosophers/aquinas.md) was among the first to recognize as drawn from [Proclus](../philosophers/proclus.md).
